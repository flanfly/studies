Category: comparison (25 candidate factor strategies screened; 9 significant, each given its own sort) combined with a factor-model construction. Sample and protocol are shared across all strategies, so the strategies below are grouped as the paper groups them (§3.1–§3.4).

### Claim

Using daily Coinmarketcap.com data on all coins with market capitalization above \$1 million, the authors sort the cross-section of 1,707 cryptocurrencies (109 coins in 2014, 1,583 in 2018) into value-weighted weekly quintile portfolios on 25 price- and market-based factors derived from the equity factor zoo of Feng et al. (2017) and Chen and Zimmermann (2018), from the beginning of 2014 to the end of 2018. Nine factors form zero-investment long-short strategies with sizable, statistically significant weekly excess returns: market capitalization (−3.4% weekly, t = −2.557), last-day price (−3.9%, t = −2.420), maximum week price (−4.1%, t = −2.483), one-, two-, three-, and four-week momentum (2.7%, 3.3%, 4.1%, 2.5%; t = 1.994, 2.442, 2.742, 2.002), dollar volume (−3.2%, t = −2.016), and standard deviation of dollar volume (−3.0%, t = −2.269) (Tables 3–6). A cryptocurrency CAPM (single market factor) leaves all nine alphas significant (average alpha decline of only 8.61% of the unadjusted values, §4); a three-factor model of cryptocurrency market (CMKT), size (CSMB), and momentum (CMOM) factors leaves none of the nine alphas statistically significant (Table 11). The paper's stated main results: nine cross-sectional predictors exist, and the three-factor model captures the cross-section of expected cryptocurrency returns.

### Fit with our constraints

The instrument class is spot cryptocurrency (daily prices, volume, market cap); no futures, perpetuals, or funding data are used. Every one of the nine strategies requires a short leg, and the paper itself flags that "shorting is not readily available for most of the coins" (§5.1); it addresses this by re-running each strategy shorting Bitcoin instead of the short quintile, reporting results "qualitatively similar" (§5.1), though the one-, two-, and four-week momentum means lose significance in that form (Table 12). Long and short legs are reported separately: every quintile table (Tables 3–7) gives each of the five value-weighted portfolio means and t-statistics. No leverage is described; the long-short portfolios are zero-investment by construction. Replication needs daily OHLC, volume, and market-capitalization data per coin (Coinmarketcap.com aggregates over 200 exchanges; price is the volume-weighted average of prices at each market, §2), the one-month Treasury bill rate for the risk-free rate, and weekly portfolio rebalancing. Returns are gross: "this strategy does not take into account trading costs and the feasibility of short selling" (§3.1). Survivorship is addressed: Coinmarketcap lists both active and defunct cryptocurrencies (§2). Turnover is not reported anywhere.

### Strategies

All nine share one protocol, stated once here (§3): each week, coins are sorted into quintile portfolios by the factor value; the portfolio's return is tracked "in the week that follows"; average excess returns (over the risk-free rate) of each portfolio are time-series means of weekly value-weighted portfolio excess returns; the strategy is the fifth-minus-first-quintile spread; all strategies are rebalanced weekly. The signal is measured as of the last day (or week) of the formation week, so there is a roughly one-week gap between signal and return. All non-return variables are winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles each week (§2). Coins with market cap below \$1,000,000 are excluded; point-in-time reconstruction of the universe is not discussed. No microcap, penny-stock, or liquidity screen beyond the \$1M cap is applied.

**Size factors (§3.1, Tables 2–3).** Signals: MCAP — log last-day market capitalization in the formation week; PRC — log last-day price; MAXDPRC — maximum price of the formation week; AGE — number of weeks listed on Coinmarketcap.com (insignificant, Table 7). MCAP, PRC, and MAXDPRC each produce −3.4% to −4.1% weekly spreads, i.e. small coins outperform large coins, monotonic across quintiles, significant at 5%. The authors do not offer a risk or behavioural rationale; the short leg is the large/big quintile.

**Momentum factors (§3.2, Tables 2, 4).** Signals r1,0, r2,0, r3,0, r4,0 are one- through four-week past returns (return over the preceding 1–4 weeks; exact compounding is not specified). Only these four of the eight tested momentum horizons (the others: 8-, 16-, 50-, 100-week) are significant. Spreads are roughly monotonic in quintiles, 2.5–4.1% weekly, significant at 5% (1% for three-week). No risk-based or behavioural mechanism is proposed by the authors. The short leg is past losers.

**Volume factors (§3.3, Tables 2, 5).** Signal PRCVOL — log average daily volume times price in the formation week. The spread is −3.2% weekly (t = −2.016), i.e. low-dollar-volume coins outperform; the plain volume (VOL) and volume-scaled-by-market-cap (VOLSCALED) sorts are insignificant (Table 7). The short leg is the high-dollar-volume quintile.

**Volatility factors (§3.4, Tables 2, 6).** Of ten volatility factors (BETA, BETA2, IDIOVOL, RETVOL, RETSKEW, RETKURT, MAXRET, DELAY, STDPRCVOL, DAMIHUD), only STDPRCVOL — log standard deviation of dollar volume in the formation week — is significant: −3.0% weekly (t = −2.269), monotonic across quintiles. The authors note STDPRCVOL "strongly correlates with size… primarily driven by the differences in the price levels of coins" (fn 5) and that it is accounted for by the size factor.

**What the comparison establishes (§4).** The nine strategies are spanned by three factors. CMKT is the value-weighted return of all available coins minus the one-month T-bill rate (§2); CSMB is built Fama-French (1993)-style: coins split each week into bottom-30% (S), middle-40% (M), top-30% (B) size groups by market cap, value-weighted portfolios, CSMB = small minus big; CMOM uses three-week momentum (chosen "because it generates the largest long-short spread in the data", fn 7), same 30/40/30 split, top minus bottom. A one-factor (crypto CAPM) model leaves all alphas significant with long-short R²s from about 0% (one-week momentum) to 6.8% (MAXDPRC) and mean absolute significant betas of 0.39 (Table 8). Adding CSMB explains the five non-momentum strategies but none of the momentum ones; adding CMOM explains the four momentum strategies but none of the others (Tables 9–10). Under the full three-factor model, none of the nine long-short alphas is significant (Table 11); r1,0 loads on both size and momentum, MCAP and STDPRCVOL on size only, r2,0–r4,0 on momentum only, PRC, MAXDPRC, PRCVOL on market and size; none loads on market only.

### Test setup

Sample: weekly returns from the first week of 2014 through the end of 2018, constructed from daily close prices; the year is divided into 52 weeks, the first 51 of seven days and the last of eight (nine for 2016) (§2, fn 2). Universe: 1,707 coins on Coinmarketcap.com with price, volume, and market-capitalization data and cap ≥ \$1,000,000; 109 coins in 2014 rising to 1,583 in 2018; mean (median) market cap 356.71 (8.17) million dollars; mean (median) daily dollar volume 18,305.83 (103.89) thousand dollars (Table 1). The coin market index averaged 1.3% weekly with weekly standard deviation 0.117 (vs. Bitcoin 1.2%/0.114, Ripple 3.5%/0.267, Ethereum 4.6%/0.241). Stock factors (Fama-French 3, Carhart 4, Fama-French 5) come from Kenneth French's website (§2). Risk-free rate: one-month Treasury bill rate. There is no out-of-sample or post-publication period; the sample is the entire window. Delisting is handled by the data vendor (defunct coins retained), otherwise not addressed. Headline numbers are gross weekly excess returns; no transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity analysis appears anywhere in the paper. All tests use Newey-West-style t-statistics as reported in parentheses.

### Robustness

Reported in the body: tercile versions of all successful strategies are "qualitatively similar" (fn 4; Appendix Table A.5 gives tercile-based factor regressions). Replacing the short quintile with short Bitcoin leaves results "virtually do not change" except that the one-, two-, and four-week momentum means and the dollar-volume one-factor alpha lose significance (Table 12). Stock factor models do not price the strategies: under Fama-French three-factor, the MCAP alpha is −3.2% (t = −2.372) versus −3.4% unadjusted, and the r3,0 alpha is 4.2% (t = 2.799) versus 4.1% unadjusted (Table 14); Carhart four-factor and Fama-French five-factor results in Appendix Tables A.3–A.4 are "qualitatively similar." In Fama-MacBeth regressions (Table 13), size factors are individually but not jointly significant, PRCVOL and STDPRCVOL are individually significant, and momentum is insignificant — the authors attribute this to Fama-MacBeth approximating equal weighting. Momentum is size-dependent: double-sorting on size (median) then three-week momentum gives 0.6% weekly (insignificant) below-median and 4.2% (t = 2.57) above-median (Table A.2); Panel E of Table 13 confirms momentum significant for coins above \$10M market cap. Hedging unpriced risks per Daniel et al. (2018) raises the size factor's squared weekly Sharpe ratio from 0.057 to 0.076 but does not improve momentum's, which the authors conjecture reflects "more transient" momentum factor loadings (§5.4).

### Specification search

The paper reports examining 25 factors (Table 2) drawn from the compiled equity zoo, screening only price/market-based ones because "financial and accounting data for the cross-section of coins is either not readily available or not applicable" (§3). All 25 sorts are reported (9 in Tables 3–6, 16 in Table 7). Variants reported: tercile sorts for all successful strategies (Table A.5), double sorts (Table A.2), two alternative two-factor models, three stock-factor models, and a Bitcoin-short variant of every strategy (Table 12). No multiple-testing or data-snooping adjustment (e.g., Feng et al.) is applied to the 25 screens.

### Limitations and future work

The authors' own statements: the strategies ignore trading costs and the feasibility of short selling, and "short selling is either not possible or limited for most of the coins" (§1, §3.1, §5.1); they note in passing that some coins are untracked because their exchanges lack accessible APIs (fn 1). The conclusion frames the contribution as "a set of stylized facts… to assess and develop theoretical models of cryptocurrency" (§6); no specific future-work agenda is given.

### Separable variants for our pipeline

The nine significant signals are independently testable, each defined in Table 2 and §3.1–§3.4: MCAP, PRC, MAXDPRC (size group); r1,0, r2,0, r3,0, r4,0 (momentum group); PRCVOL; STDPRCVOL. The three factors themselves (CMKT, CSMB, CMOM; §4, Table 2 for definitions) are separately constructible. The sixteen insignificant signals (Table 2/Table 7) are also defined precisely enough to retest, and the paper explicitly reports which momentum horizons (8-, 16-, 50-, 100-week) and which volatility measures fail. Alternative measurements the paper tests in robustness: tercile breakpoints, Bitcoin as the short leg, and size-double-sorted momentum.
