**Category: comparison.** Twenty-seven on-chain factors from Ethereum blockchain data are
evaluated against each other and against 26 price/volume/volatility benchmark factors within a
unified three-framework design (double-selection LASSO, portfolio sorts, three-factor
regressions). The document is a preprint and is labelled "not peer reviewed" throughout.
Exact definitions of the on-chain factors are in Table 3, which was not recoverable from the
extraction; every signal description below comes from §2.3 prose (see Extraction gaps).

### Claim

The paper asks whether on-chain factors derived from Ethereum blockchain data carry
cross-sectional pricing information beyond established cryptocurrency risk factors. Using
proprietary Upbit data, the authors construct 27 on-chain factors across four dimensions —
network activity and adoption, scale-adjusted activity, valuation ratios, and token
distribution and holder structure — for 122 Ethereum-based tokens from July 1, 2020 to August
31, 2025 (270 weekly observations, §2.1), and test them against 26 benchmark factors (size,
momentum, volume, volatility) with the double-selection LASSO of Feng, Giglio and Xiu (2020),
complemented by value-weighted double-sorted portfolio spreads and three-factor (CMKT, CSMB,
CMOM of Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022) regressions. Eight of the 27 on-chain factors are
significant at the 10% level or better in the cross-sectional pricing test (Table 4), with
the strongest evidence in scale-adjusted activity and token distribution. Transaction count
to network value (TXNV) is the only factor significant in all three frameworks — pricing
coefficient 232 bp/week, t = 3.21 (Table 4); Big-group spread 67 bp/week, t = 1.72 (Table 5);
three-factor alpha 0.6%/week, t = 2.09 (Table 6). Market-to-realized-value ratios do not
survive once momentum is accounted for: none has a significant three-factor alpha and all
load positively on CMOM (Table 6). Token-distribution factors, especially small-holder share
(SSH, alpha 0.9%/week, t = 3.13) and centralized-exchange share (CEXSH, 0.8%, t = 2.68), give
the most robust abnormal returns and survive equal-weighting and cost assumptions up to 50 bp
(§5). Subperiod analysis shows a rotation: scale-adjusted activity factors are strong in the
first half of the sample and weaken sharply in the second, while distribution factors
strengthen (Table 8).

### Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is spot cryptocurrency tokens traded on Upbit (KRW, BTC and USDT markets);
no derivatives are involved anywhere in the paper. Every factor strategy is a long-short
High-minus-Low portfolio and therefore needs a short leg; the paper assumes spot shorting and
does not discuss futures or perpetuals as the expression vehicle. Long and short legs are
reported separately in Table 5 (High and Low columns within each size group), not only as
spreads. No leverage is used or reported. Micro-cap tokens (below the 20th market-cap
percentile) are excluded from factor construction entirely (footnote 3, §2.4). Replication
requires Upbit proprietary data — prices and on-chain analytics integrated by Dunamu Inc.;
the compiled dataset is available from Upbit Data Lab upon request (§Data Availability), so
public-data replication is not possible as specified. On-chain factors are constructed using
information available prior to portfolio formation, i.e., point-in-time by construction, but
the paper does not describe a separate filing-date-style lag for on-chain data (blockchain
data are public continuously). All headline numbers are net of a 20 bp per-transaction
assumption on both legs at every weekly rebalancing under full turnover (§2.4); Table 10
reports gross and net figures across a 0–50 bp cost ladder.

### Strategies

Portfolio construction is common to all factors (§2.4): at the end of each week, returns are
cleaned by excluding weekly returns above 100% and winsorizing the remaining cross-section at
the 1%/99% levels; assets are split into Micro (<20th percentile), Small (20–50th) and Big
(>50th) by market cap; within Big and Small, assets are sorted on the signal with breakpoints
at the 30th/70th percentiles of the combined Big+Small universe (Low/Neutral/High); portfolio
returns are value-weighted with weights capped at the 80th percentile of the asset universe;
rebalanced weekly. The size-neutral factor return is

$$f_{i,t} = \frac{1}{2} \left( R_{i,t}^{\text{BH}} + R_{i,t}^{\text{SH}} \right) - \frac{1}{2} \left( R_{i,t}^{\text{BL}} + R_{i,t}^{\text{SL}} \right), \quad (1)$$

(§2.4, Eq. 1). Turnover is not reported as a quantity; the cost analysis assumes full
turnover of both legs each week (§5.3).

**TXNV — transaction count to network value.** Scale-adjusted activity factor defined as
transaction count normalized by network value (market capitalization) (§2.3, citing Liebi
2022). The only factor significant in all three frameworks: pricing coefficient 232 bp/week
(t = 3.21, |K³| = 19) (Table 4); value-weighted spread 67 bp/week in the Big group only
(t = 1.72) (Table 5); alpha 0.6%/week (t = 2.09) (Table 6). Authors' framing is that
transaction counts weight each interaction equally and are therefore a cleaner signal than
volumes or active-address counts, which large transactions can dominate (§4.1). Subperiod:
alpha 1.7%/week (t = 3.90) in the first half (07/2020–01/2023) turning to −0.5% (t = −1.82)
in the second (02/2023–08/2025) (Table 8). Equal-weighted alpha 0.6% (t = 2.26) (Table 9).
Sharpe 0.874 at 20 bp, 1.427 gross; at 50 bp annualized return 0.014, Sharpe 0.038 (Table 10)
— effectively breakeven at the top of the cost ladder.

**AANV — active addresses to network value.** Same construction as TXNV but with active
addresses (§2.3). Insignificant in the double-selection test (27 bp, t = 0.41, |K³| = 19) but
significant in sorts (Big 84 bp, t = 2.77; Small 82 bp, t = 1.71) and alpha 0.6%/week
(t = 2.64), with positive CMKT (0.128, t = 3.21) and CSMB (0.219, t = 1.98) loadings (Tables
4–6). Strongest first-half alpha among activity factors (1.3%, t = 3.36), then −0.08 (n.s.)
in the second half (Table 8). Sharpe 1.150 at 20 bp, shallowest on-chain drawdown (−33.8%)
(Table 7).

**SSH — small-holder share.** Share of token supply held in small wallets (§2.3, citing
Sakkas and Urquhart 2024; holder-size thresholds are in the unreadable Table 3). Negative
pricing coefficient in the cross-section (−195 bp, t = −1.77, |K³| = 26) but positive
unconditional spreads (Big 110 bp, t = 3.13; Small 118 bp, t = 2.39) and the largest
three-factor alpha of all 27 factors, 0.9%/week (t = 3.13) with a positive CMKT loading
(0.111, t = 2.70) (Tables 4–6). The paper does not reconcile the sign difference between the
pricing coefficient and the spread/alpha signs. Not significant in the first subperiod
(t = 1.61); strongest alpha of any factor in the second, 0.9%/week (t = 3.74) (Table 8).
Robust to equal weighting (0.9%, t = 3.31) (Table 9) and to costs: Sharpe 1.326 at 20 bp,
above 1.0 at 30 bp, and 0.606 (annualized return 26.3%) even at 50 bp (Table 10).

**CEXSH and DEXSH — exchange-venue shares.** Share of supply held at centralized versus
decentralized exchanges (§2.3). CEXSH: insignificant in the double-selection test (54 bp,
t = 0.95) but significant spreads (Big 97 bp, t = 2.30; Small 92 bp, t = 1.97), alpha 0.8%
(t = 2.68) (Tables 4–6); strengthens under equal weighting (t 2.69 → 3.38) (Table 9); Sharpe
1.132 at 20 bp, above 0.65 at 40 bp (Tables 7, 10). DEXSH: significant negative pricing
coefficient (−162 bp, t = −2.56, |K³| = 6) but no significant spreads (max t = 1.63) and
alpha 0.6% (t = 1.81) (Tables 4–6); ΔDEXSH has the largest absolute pricing coefficient in
the paper, −236 bp (t = −5.40), interpreted as larger balances in DEX pools increasing
readily tradable supply and future selling pressure (§4.1).

**LSH and MSH — large- and medium-holder share.** LSH: alpha −0.5%/week (t = −1.99) with
negative CMKT loading (−0.101, t = −2.22) and R² of only 0.025, i.e., largely orthogonal to
the benchmark model (§4.3); negative Big-group spread −78 bp (t = −2.33); insignificant
pricing coefficient (−45 bp, t = −0.55) (Tables 4–6). ΔLSH also has alpha −0.5% (t = −1.98).
LSH strengthens to marginal significance in the second subperiod (t = −1.73) and under equal
weighting (t = −2.47) (Tables 8–9). Sharpe 0.781 at 20 bp, falling to 0.076 at 50 bp (Table
10). MSH: alpha 0.7% (t = 2.37), Small-group spread 107 bp (t = 2.23), second-half alpha
t = 2.88 (Tables 6, 5, 8).

**TURNOVER — network turnover.** Token circulation relative to supply, a velocity proxy
(§2.3). The only network-activity factor significant in the cross-sectional test: −146 bp
(t = −4.80) with an empty control set (|K³| = 0), i.e., standalone pricing relevance; the
paper interprets high velocity as speculative trading rather than productive usage (§4.1).
Not significant in sorts or in the three-factor alpha (−0.4%, t = −1.35), where it loads on
CMKT (0.107, t = 2.37) and CMOM (0.240, t = 3.32) (Tables 5–6). Small-group spread −119 bp
(t = −2.36) (Table 5).

**Valuation ratios (MVRV family) — the negative result.** MVRV and its decompositions
MVCEX, MVDEX (by venue) and MVL, MVM, MVS (by holder size) compare market price to realized
price of circulating tokens (§2.3). MVDEX (130 bp, t = 2.13) and MVL (217 bp, t = 2.05) are
significant in the double-selection test but the aggregate MVRV is not (17 bp, t = 0.23)
(Table 4). No valuation measure produces a significant spread in either size group (Table 5)
or a significant three-factor alpha; all six load positively on CMOM, coefficients 0.207
(MVL, t = 2.16) to 0.538 (MVCEX, t = 6.31), which the authors attribute to the mechanical
relation between recent price appreciation in the numerator and slowly adjusting realized
values in the denominator (§4.3). The momentum subsumption holds in both subperiods and under
equal weighting (Tables 8–9).

**What the comparison establishes.** No single framework fully characterizes any factor: the
paper explicitly documents divergence across frameworks — MVDEX, MVL, DEXSH and ΔDEXSH are
priced in the double-selection test but produce no significant unconditional spreads, while
CEXSH and AANV produce significant spreads/alphas despite insignificant pricing coefficients
(§4.3). TXNV is the lone factor significant in all three. The authors conclude that on-chain
pricing content is selective, strongest in scale-adjusted activity and holder structure, and
that the pricing power of activity factors has decayed within the sample while distribution
factors have strengthened (§4.4, §5.1).

### Test setup

Sample period July 1, 2020 – August 31, 2025 (270 weeks), chosen as the Upbit Ethereum-token
sample with data collection starting July 1, 2018 to support lookbacks up to 100 weeks (§2.1).
122 unique Ethereum-based tokens, including network-in and network-out migrants; stablecoins
excluded; delisted tokens included with returns computed through the final trading week using
last available price (30-day Upbit liquidation period noted) — survivorship bias is
explicitly addressed (§2.1). Cross-section averages 33 tokens/week in H2 2020 rising to 96 in
2025; mean market cap exceeds median by roughly 30× (§2.1, Table 1). Data: Upbit proprietary
infrastructure integrating exchange trading records and blockchain analytics; the paper names
no external data vendor (§2.1). Test assets: 7 Upbit Data Lab portfolios (Smart Contract
Platform, DeFi, UPBIT COMP, UPBIT ALT, Momentum, Low Volatility, Contrarian) plus the four
corner portfolios (Big/Small × High/Low) of each of the 26 benchmark factors, excluding
Neutral and Micro — 109 test portfolios in total (§2.5). Benchmark factors standardized by
univariate regression coefficients before LASSO (Bryzgalova 2015, §2.4). Newey–West standard
errors with four lags throughout (Tables 5, 6). Headline figures are net of the 20 bp
per-transaction assumption (both legs, weekly, full turnover); Table 10 gives gross and
higher-cost figures. Benchmark: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu (2022) three-factor model (CMKT, CSMB,
CMOM).

exactly the Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu three factors.

### Robustness

Three robustness exercises (§5). Subperiod analysis: two equal halves of 135 weeks; the
activity factors TXNV and AANV carry alphas of 1.7% (t = 3.90) and 1.3% (t = 3.36) in the
first half and weaken to −0.5% (t = −1.82) and −0.08 (n.s.) in the second, while SSH, MSH,
LSH and DEXSH become significant only in the second half; valuation ratios never produce an
alpha in either half and load positively on CMOM in both (Table 8). Equal weighting: all 27
factor portfolios reconstructed with equal weights within size groups; TXNV and AANV alphas
unchanged at 0.6%; distribution factors strengthen (CEXSH t 2.69→3.38, LSH −1.99→−2.47, MSH
and DEXSH reach significance); valuation results unchanged (Table 9). Transaction costs: 0–50
bp per transaction, full turnover, both legs; all five headline factors positive at every
cost level; SSH Sharpe above 1.0 through 30 bp and 0.606 at 50 bp, while TXNV and LSH
approach breakeven at 50 bp (Table 10). The paper reports the decay of the activity factors
and does not resolve it beyond noting the rotation toward distribution factors.

### Specification search

The paper reports results for all 27 on-chain factors in each of Tables 4, 5 and 6, and for
all 27 in the equal-weighted and subperiod replications; Tables 7 and 10 narrow to five
selected factors. The LASSO penalty grid is 100 values from λ₀ to λ₀·10⁻³⁵ with 10-fold CV
repeated over 200 random seeds and the 1-SE rule (SE from across-seed variation) (§3.2).
Selection outcomes are reported fully: first stage selects no benchmark factors (|K¹| = 0)
for all 27; final control sets are non-empty for 20 of 27 (Table 4). The paper does not
report how many of its positive findings survive a multiple-testing adjustment.

### Limitations and future work

Authors' own: the sample is limited to Ethereum-based tokens on a single exchange, and the
small cross-section constrains portfolio granularity and power in some tests; the weakening
of scale-adjusted activity factors suggests some on-chain premia may attenuate as blockchain
analytics are incorporated into prices. Future work named: generalizing the distribution
findings across exchanges and blockchain ecosystems, adding richer protocol-usage and
smart-contract interaction measures, and studying time variation in on-chain pricing for
dynamic factor allocation (§6).

### Separable variants for our pipeline

The 27 on-chain factors are independently testable signals; the paper is a factor
horse-race, so this list is essentially the strategies again. Signal definitions in §2.3
(qualitative) and Table 3 (not readable — see Extraction gaps). The factors with any
significant result: TXNV, AANV, CIRCSUP, TURNOVER (scale-adjusted/network activity); MVDEX,
MVL (valuation, conditionally priced only); SSH, CEXSH, DEXSH, LSH, MSH, ΔDEXSH, ΔLSH (token
distribution, including the Δ/change versions). The three-factor model (CMKT, CSMB, CMOM) of
Liu et al. (2022) is the benchmark any candidate must beat; the double-selection LASSO setup
with the 109-portfolio test asset base is a replicable screening procedure (§3).
