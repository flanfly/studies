**Category: methodology** (with empirical content). The unit of interest is a statistical technique — the "effective degrees of freedom" statistic N\*(N) — applied to discriminate between two covariance models for daily cryptocurrency returns. There is no strategy, no backtest, and no tradable signal anywhere in the paper.

## Claim

Giller applies a model-free, distribution-free statistic, the functional relationship between effective degrees of freedom and equal-weighted portfolio size, N\*(N), to daily returns of the fourteen cryptocurrencies tradable via the *Robinhood* retail brokerage API (data from end of July 2024; 125 daily returns as of writing), and separately to CoinMarketCap data from 2018–2024 (abstract; §4.1; §4.2). The average pairwise correlation of daily returns is high — the abstract states "of order 60%", while the full enumeration of the 91 pairs gives a mean of 54.17% with three pairs below 20% and four above 80% (§4.2) — and the data favours a homoskedastic isotropic correlation model (common pairwise ρ) over a linear factor model with additive noise "with high confidence" (abstract). For the Robinhood universe, the all-asset portfolio gives N\*(14) = 1.96 with imputed ρ = 47.25%; the isotropic curve fits the data (total χ² = 21.0 with 12 d.o.f., p = 0.0497) while the best-fitting linear factor model fails (total χ² = 572.7 with 9 d.o.f., vanishingly small p; F = 36.3, p = 4.8×10⁻⁷) (§4.3; §4.4). The description appears stable through time: year-by-year estimates on CoinMarketCap data give N\*_y with mean 1.85 (s.d. 0.38, s.e. 0.15) for 2018–2023, and the Robinhood value 1.96 lies inside the 68% confidence region [1.60, 2.00] (§5.2).

## Fit with our constraints

No strategy is constructed, so short legs, leverage, transaction costs, and financing never arise. Instruments are spot cryptocurrencies only (Robinhood API mid-prices; CoinMarketCap via Yahoo Finance); no futures or perpetuals appear; all evidence is on equal-weighted portfolios of N = 1…14 assets. Replication would need Robinhood best bid/ask quotes sampled every ten minutes since end of July 2024 (publicly available file, per Appendix A) and CoinMarketCap daily data via `yfinance`. Point-in-time issues are not addressed: the Robinhood universe is the *current* list of API-tradable assets, and the long CoinMarketCap sample excludes COMP, SHIB, UNI for data-quality reasons plus AAVE/AVAX pre-existence, so the 2018–2023 sample is survivorship-restricted by construction — the paper does not flag this. The content relevant to our pipeline is contextual: with imputed ρ ≈ 47–48% and N\* ≈ 1.9–2.0 at N = 12–14, a dozen-asset equal-weighted basket diversifies like roughly two independent assets, and under the isotropic model residual variance does not vanish as N grows because lim_{N→∞} N\*(N) = 1/ρ (§3.3; §6).

## Strategies

There are no strategies; the "strategies" of this paper are the competing covariance models. The statistic: N\*(N) = N·V\^I/V\^P (eq. 3), where V\^I is the portfolio variance expected if the assets were independent, computed from individual sample variances, and V\^P is the sample variance of an equal-weighted portfolio. The three model-specific forms are:

- **Independent returns:** N\*(N) = N (eq. 4).
- **Homoskedastic isotropic correlation:** covariance matrix with common correlation ρ (eq. 5, generalisable to heteroskedastic via diagonal scaling, as in DCC models of Engle and Sheppard), giving N\*(N) = N/(1 + (N−1)ρ) (eq. 6), with ρ ∈ [1/(1−N), 1] and lim_{N→∞} N\*(N) = 1/ρ (eq. 7).
- **Linear K-factor model:** r_t = μ + Bf_t + ε_t (eq. 8), E[f] = 0_K, E[ε] = 0_N (eq. 9), V[f] = I_K, V[ε] = (diag s)², V[f, ε] = 0 (eq. 10), so V[r] = BV[f]Bᵀ + (diag s)² (eq. 11), giving N\*(N) = N(b̄²N + s̄²)/(b̄ᵀb̄N + s̄²) (eq. 12), where b̄² is the mean-squared element of the factor loadings matrix B, b̄ the mean loadings vector, and s̄² = sᵀs/N the mean residual variance.

The two structures are discriminated by functional shape: isotropic converges to the single value 1/ρ, while independence and factor models diverge linearly in N (coefficient ≈ 1/K for homogeneous loadings); such asymptotics are not testable here because Nmax = 14 is small, though the author notes the test is feasible for the S&P 500 or RUSSELL 3000 (§3.5). The sampling experiment (§3.1): for j ∈ [1, Niter], draw N^j uniformly from [1, Nmax], draw a unique subset S^j of N^j assets uniformly from the universe, compute V\^P and V\^I, and record N\*(N^j) — independent of any distributional assumptions. Estimation differs by model: the isotropic parameters N\*(14) = 1.96 and ρ = 47.25% are fixed by the "final datum" (the all-asset portfolio) and the curve is *extrapolated* from it, required to pass through (1, 1) — not fitted (§4.3); the factor-model curve is fitted by non-linear least squares (scipy.optimize `curve_fit`) treating b̄², b̄ᵀb̄, s̄² as three holistic non-negative coefficients, with estimates 0.000 ± 0.165, 7.24 ± 1.80×10⁷, and 17.58 ± 4.36×10⁷ — the author states the model is "poorly constrained by the data" and its curve misses the sample means almost everywhere (§4.4, footnote 10). The χ² test statistic uses the sample mean and standard error of N\* at each portfolio size; portfolios of one asset are excluded (N\* ≡ 1 always) and portfolios of all assets are excluded (used for imputation) (§4.4).

## Test setup

Two datasets. **Robinhood:** fourteen API-tradable cryptocurrencies — AAVE, AVAX, BCH, BTC, COMP, DOGE, ETC, ETH, LINK, LTC, SHIB, UNI, XLM, XTZ; best bid/ask quotes sampled every ten minutes since end of July 2024; daily returns from mid-prices at the 23:59 UTC print; 125 daily returns (§4.1; §4.2). Robinhood shows more assets on its website but only API-tradable ones are used (§4.1). **CoinMarketCap (via Yahoo Finance/`yfinance`):** the same assets minus COMP (series terminates before the current year), SHIB (numerical precision at very low nominal values), and UNI (abrupt mid-sample value change by many orders of magnitude); AAVE and AVAX did not exist at the sample start; the first year of each series is arbitrarily removed to exclude initial-issuance volatility (§5.1). Analysis is stratified by year y ∈ [2018, 2024], with Nmax = 10 for 2018–2020 and 12 for 2021–2024 (Table 2). No delisting or survivorship handling is described beyond these exclusions; no transaction costs, financing, leverage, or benchmark figure anywhere — there is no portfolio return stream to adjust. All dates are UTC.

## Results

The Robinhood experiment: 1,000 trials with duplicates removed yield 822 distinct portfolio pairs (N\*, N) (2¹⁴ − 1 = 16,383 possible portfolios) (§4.3). Table 1 reports the mean and standard error of N\* by portfolio size (counts 50–83 per size); the isotropic extrapolation lies within sampling error for most sizes, the fitted factor curve mostly does not. The formal comparison (§4.4):

| Model | Total χ² | d.o.f. | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isotropic correlation (parameters imputed from final datum) | 21.0 | 12 | 0.0497 |
| Linear factor model (3-parameter fit) | 572.7 | 9 | vanishingly small |

The F statistic for the ratio of reduced χ² values, (572.7/9)/(21.0/12) = 36.3, distributed F(9, 12), has p = 4.8×10⁻⁷, rejecting equivalence of the two descriptions with high confidence (§4.4). The isotropic model itself fails to describe the data with "barely over 95% confidence" (p = 0.0497) — a rejection the author reports without further comment.

Year-by-year stability on CoinMarketCap data (Table 2; variance units not stated in the paper):

| Year | Nmax | V\^P (portfolio) | V\^I (independent) | N\* | ρ (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 10 | 30.91 | 4.87 | 1.58 | 59.40 |
| 2019 | 10 | 10.39 | 2.13 | 2.05 | 43.07 |
| 2020 | 10 | 16.65 | 2.68 | 1.61 | 57.98 |
| 2021 | 12 | 31.81 | 6.57 | 2.48 | 34.90 |
| 2022 | 12 | 15.64 | 1.93 | 1.48 | 64.59 |
| 2023 | 12 | 6.14 | 0.98 | 1.92 | 47.74 |
| 2024 | 12 | 10.15 | 1.62 | 1.91 | 47.97 |

(2024 is a partial year to the time of writing.) For the first six years, mean N\*_y = 1.85, s.d. 0.38, s.e. 0.15; the Robinhood value 1.96 ∈ [1.60, 2.00], the 68% confidence region, so the author finds "little reason to suspect gross non-stationarity" (§5.2). The author concludes the characteristic form of N\*(N) is consistent with homoskedastic isotropic covariance and *not* with a linear factor model — "not even a single factor model" — and that, although such a structure appears to support a "market factor," there is no common exogenous driving force: returns "just happen to be similar a lot of the time" (§6).

## Robustness

There is no formal robustness section. The nearest analogue is the year-stratified CoinMarketCap analysis of §5, which serves as the stability check on a reduced universe: ρ imputed from the all-asset portfolio ranges 34.90%–64.59% and N\* ranges 1.48–2.48 across years, reported as consistency, not degradation. The author notes the asymptotic discrimination between models (1/ρ vs. linear divergence) cannot be executed at Nmax = 14 (§3.5). Nothing is tested on alternative parameters, alternative universes beyond the two data sources, delayed execution, or additional markets.

## Specification search

One design parameter, Niter = 1,000 sampling trials, reduced to 822 distinct portfolios by deduplication (§4.3); one factor-model fit with three free parameters; one isotropic model with two parameters fixed by the final datum; results reported for every portfolio size and both data sources. No parameter sweeps are reported; all specifications examined are reported.

## Limitations and future work

The author's own: the Robinhood record spans only about five months at the time of writing (§5); asymptotic model discrimination is infeasible at Nmax = 14 (§3.5); the factor-model fit is poorly constrained (§4.4). The paper's single prescriptive remark: at pairwise correlation around 50%, a mean-variance optimal allocator should size positions proportional to a "mostly centered" Z-score of alpha divided by volatility, not alpha over variance, because residual returns survive diversification (§6). Capacity, turnover, costs, and tradable implementations are never discussed.

## Separable variants for our pipeline

No strategies, so nothing tradable is separable. Descriptive elements that could be re-estimated on other universes (futures, perps): (a) the N\*(N) random-sampling experiment as a model-free cross-sectional diagnostic (§3.1); (b) the homoskedastic isotropic correlation model with common ρ (eq. 5–7), including its heteroskedastic generalisation (§3.3); (c) the linear factor model discriminant and its N\*(N) form (eq. 8–12), which is the paper's falsifiable alternative. This list is the strategy list of the paper: there is nothing else in it.
