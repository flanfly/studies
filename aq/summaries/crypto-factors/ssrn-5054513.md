## Claim

The paper asks whether the cross-section of daily cryptocurrency returns is better described by a homoskedastic *isotropic* covariance model (one common pairwise correlation $\rho$) or by a linear $K$-factor model with additive noise. The discriminator is a model-free, distribution-free statistic, $N^*(N)$ — the "effective" degrees of freedom of an equal-weighted portfolio as a function of portfolio size $N$ — estimated by random sampling (eq. (3), §3.1). On 125 daily returns for the 14 cryptocurrencies tradable via the Robinhood retail API (end of July 2024 to the date of writing, November 2024), the mean pairwise correlation is 54.17% (§4.2), and $N^*(N)$ is strongly consistent with the isotropic form $N^*(N) = N/(1+(N-1)\rho)$ with $N^*(14)=1.96$, $\rho=47.25\%$, and inconsistent with a factor model: the linear-factor $\chi^2$ is 572.7 on 9 d.o.f. (vanishing p-value) versus 21.0 on 12 d.o.f. ($p = 0.0497$) for the isotropic model, and the F-test of model equivalence is $F(9,12) = 36.3$, $p = 4.8\times10^{-7}$ (§4.4). Year-by-year replication on CoinMarketCap data for 2018–2024 shows $N^*$ between 1.48 and 2.48 (§5, Table 2), which the author reads as the isotropic description being "relatively stable through time" (abstract).

## Fit with our constraints

The paper proposes no strategy and reports no tradeable signal: there are no long or short legs, no leverage, no transaction costs, and no factor-portfolio returns. Its output is a statement about the return *covariance* structure, quantified as $\rho$ (imputed 34.90%–64.59% by year; Table 2) and $N^*$ (1.48–2.48); the stated allocator implication is that, at $\rho \approx 50\%$, a mean-variance-optimal allocator should size positions proportional to a "mostly centered" Z-score of alpha and inversely to volatility, not alpha-over-variance (§6). Prices are mids, spot, USD-quoted; the analysis uses the retail-tradable list at a broker, not exchange futures or perpetuals. Replication needs only public data: Robinhood REST API quotes (available from end of July 2024 onward) and CoinMarketCap data via Yahoo Finance/yfinance (2018–2024), both named in Appendix A.

## Strategies

No strategies. The unit of the paper is the discrimination between three covariance hypotheses, each given a distinct closed-form $N^*(N)$ in §3, tested by the sampling experiment of §3.1: for $j \in [1, N_\text{iter}]$ iterations, pick $N^j$ uniformly from $[1, N_\text{max}]$, a unique subset $S^j$ of $N^j$ assets, compute the equal-weighted portfolio variance $V^P$ and the variance expected under independence $V^I$, and form $N^*(N^j) = N^j \times V^I/V^P$ (eq. (3)).

- **Independent returns** (§3.2): $N^*(N) = N$ (eq. (4)). Dismissed as "unlikely" based on the author's market experience (footnote 2).
- **Isotropic returns** (§3.3): covariance matrix $\mathbb{V}[\mathbf{r}_t] = \sigma^2 \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \rho & \cdots & \rho \\ \rho & 1 & \cdots & \rho \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \vdots \\ \rho & \rho & \cdots & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (eq. (5)), generalized to heteroskedasticity by pre- and post-multiplying by a diagonal volatility matrix (the DCC setup of Engle and Sheppard). Gives $N^*(N) = \frac{N}{1+(N-1)\rho}$ (eq. (6)) with $\rho \in [1/(1-N), 1]$ and $\lim_{N\to\infty} N^*(N) = 1/\rho$ (eq. (7)).
- **Linear factor models** (§3.4): $r_t = \mu + Bf_t + \varepsilon_t$ (eq. (8)) with $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{f}_t] = \mathbf{0}_K$, $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t] = \mathbf{0}_N$ (eq. (9)), $\mathbb{V}[\mathbf{f}] = I_K$, $\mathbb{V}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}] = (\text{diag } \mathbf{s})^2$, $\mathbb{V}[\mathbf{f}, \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}] = 0$ (eq. (10)), giving $\mathbb{V}[r_t] = B\mathbb{V}[\mathbf{f}]B^T + (\text{diag } \mathbf{s})^T$ (eq. (11); second term rendered as extracted — see Extraction gaps) and $N^*(N) = N \frac{\overline{b^2} N + \overline{s^2}}{\overline{b}^T\overline{b}\, N + \overline{s^2}}$ (eq. (12)), where $\overline{b^2}$ is the mean-squared element of the loadings matrix, $\overline{b}$ the mean loadings vector, and $\overline{s^2}$ the mean residual variance. For a homogeneous $K$-factor model the large-$N$ slope coefficient is approximated by $1/K$ (§3.5).

The author notes (§3.5) that full asymptotic discrimination is infeasible here because the total asset count is low; the $N/K$ asymptotics are checkable only for equity indices such as the S&P 500 or Russell 3000.

## Anomaly evidence

Not applicable in the paper's own terms: there is no conditioning variable, no portfolio sort on any ex ante characteristic, no long-short or spread return, and no return-predictability claim. The empirical content is variance scaling of randomly sampled equal-weighted portfolios, and any short-leg reading of the results is moot because no leg of any trade is defined. The one sort-like object is the year-by-year stratification of the same experiment in §5 (Table 2).

## Test setup

Robinhood analysis (§4): daily returns (UTC midnight to UTC midnight) formed from the last mid-price print at 23:59 UTC of best bid/ask quotes sampled every ten minutes; universe is the 14 cryptocurrencies tradable through the Robinhood client API — AAVE, AVAX, BCH, BTC, COMP, DOGE, ETC, ETH, LINK, LTC, SHIB, UNI, XLM, XTZ (§4.1). Sample is 125 daily returns; the pairwise-correlation standard error is of order $1/\sqrt{122} \approx 9\%$ via Fisher's approximation (§4.2). $N_\text{iter} = 1000$ trials over $2^{14}-1 = 16{,}383$ possible portfolios, deduplicated to 822 distinct $(N^*, N)$ observations (§4.3). CoinMarketCap analysis (§5): daily closes via yfinance from the data vendor CoinMarketCap, for 2018–2024; COMP and UNI dropped (terminated series / an abrupt order-of-magnitude level change), SHIB dropped (numerical precision at low nominal values), AAVE and AVAX absent early in the sample, and the first year of each series is arbitrarily removed to exclude initial-issuance volatility (§5.1); $N_\text{max} = 10$ (2018–2020) and 12 (2021–2024). No survivorship or delisting adjustment is described beyond these exclusions; no transaction or financing costs appear because no trades are studied; there is no benchmark index. The paper's headline numbers are gross by construction.

## Results

The Robinhood findings are reported as means of $N^*$ by portfolio size (Table 1): from 1.306 at $N=2$ (s.d. 0.143, count 71) rising to 1.853 at $N=8$ (s.d. 0.193, count 50) and 1.951 at $N=13$ (s.d. 0.057, count 83), with the $N=1$ datum (mean exactly 1.000) and the all-assets final datum excluded from the statistics. The isotropic curve is not fitted to the trend but extrapolated from the final datum, and must pass through (1,1); the factor-model asymptote fixes $K=7$ and visually describes the data poorly (§4.3). The NLS fit of the factor model to eq. (12) yields holistic coefficients $\overline{b^2} = 0.000 \pm 0.165$, $\overline{b}^T\overline{b} = 7.24 \pm 1.80 \times 10^{7}$, $\overline{s^2} = 17.58 \pm 4.36 \times 10^{7}$ (numbers and exponents as rendered; see Extraction gaps), which the author judges poorly constrained by the data (§4.4).

The decision statistics: total $\chi^2 = 572.7$ on 9 d.o.f. for the linear factor model versus 21.0 on 12 d.o.f. for the isotropic model; the ratio of reduced $\chi^2$ is $(572.7/9)/(21.0/12) = 36.3$, $F(9,12)$, $p = 4.8\times10^{-7}$ (§4.4). The author concludes the factor model fails "with high confidence" while the isotropic model fails "with barely over 95% confidence" — i.e., the isotropic description is preferred but is itself rejected at 5% (§4.4). No Sharpe ratios, alphas, t-statistics, or return moments are reported anywhere; the paper's metrics are the model-selection statistics only.

## Robustness

The stability analysis is the year-by-year CoinMarketCap replication (§5.2, Table 2). For the first six years (excluding 2024, which overlaps the Robinhood window) the mean $N^*_y$ is 1.85 with standard deviation 0.38 and standard error 0.15; the Robinhood value 1.96 lies within the 68% region $[1.60, 2.00]$, which the author reads as no evidence of gross non-stationarity. Degradation is reported honestly: the isotropic curve itself is rejected at $p = 0.0497$, the factor-model fit is "poorly constrained," and the author concedes a 14-asset universe is too small for asymptotic discrimination (§3.5). The paper has no robustness section beyond §5.

## Specification search

One experimental design: 1,000 random portfolio draws, deduplicated to 822 distinct observations (§4.3); note that the per-size counts in Table 1 sum to 931 rather than 822 (see Extraction gaps). One factor-model NLS fit (three holistic coefficients, §4.4) and one zero-free-parameter isotropic extrapolation from the final datum; seven yearly stratifications (2018–2024), all of which are reported (Table 2); two data sources. No alternative parameters, universes, or execution variants are examined.

## Limitations and future work

As stated by the author: the asset count is low ($N_\text{max}$ of 14, 10–12 in the longer sample), precluding asymptotic comparisons; the Robinhood window is only five months, motivating the CoinMarketCap extension (§5); the factor-model parameters are poorly constrained by the data (§4.4); the Robinhood collection code is proprietary ("part of a trading system operated privately by the author") and is not released, though the data file and the analysis notebook ("Longer Term Crypto Degrees of Freedom.ipynb" in the Financial-Data-Science-in-Python folder of the author's GitHub) are public (Appendix A). The author notes the isotropic model has no common exogenous driving force — "they just happen to be similar a lot of the time" — so a market factor is not implied (§6).

## Separable variants for our pipeline

This empirical paper contains no return-predicting signal, so there is no strategy component to separate. The separable objects are the measurement outputs and the estimator itself: (i) the yearly $\rho$ and $N^*$ series (Table 2, §5.2), which describe diversification capacity of the crypto cross-section and could be re-estimated on exchange (futures/perps) universes; and (ii) the $N^*(N)$ sampling experiment of §3.1, which is a reusable, model-free variance-scaling estimator applicable to any asset universe. Both are stated descriptively in the paper; neither is a tradable specification.
