Category: **single-strategy** — one signal (CTREND) is developed and tested as a unit; no pre-existing strategy is modified. The paper additionally builds an asset-pricing model around the same signal and compares it against the CCAPM and the Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu (LTW, 2022) three-factor model, but the unit of interest throughout is the single CTREND signal.

## Claim

The paper proposes CTREND, a cross-sectional cryptocurrency trend factor that aggregates 28 technical indicators — momentum oscillators, moving averages, volume indicators, and volatility indicators — into a single expected-return signal using a machine-learning pipeline, the cross-sectional combined elastic net (CS-C-ENet) of Han et al. (2023), built on the combined elastic net of Dong et al. (2022) (§3.3). Using weekly data on 3,245 unique coins from Coinmarketcap.com over April 2015–May 2022 (423 weekly observations, §3.1), a value-weighted long-short quintile portfolio sorted on CTREND earns 3.87% per week (t-stat = 5.19) with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.94 (Table 3). The spread portfolio has near-zero CMKT and CSMB betas but a momentum beta of 0.79, and still earns an LTW three-factor alpha of 2.62% per week (t-stat = 4.22) (§4.1). The mechanism the authors offer is that, because cryptocurrency fundamentals are scarce, uninformed investors rationally infer future adoption and long-run value from past prices, producing trend continuation; the data bear this out in the sign — indicators classically read as reversal signals (e.g., rsi) show trend-following rather than reversal in the cross-section (§2, §3.2.2). The effect survives bivariate sorts and Fama-MacBeth regressions with momentum and other controls (§4.2–4.3), and a three-factor model replacing the LTW momentum factor with CTREND ("TREND" model, eq. 15) prices a battery of cryptocurrency anomalies better than the LTW model (§6.2). Note: the conclusion restates the headline as "3.87% per month," inconsistent with "3.87% per week" in the abstract, introduction, and Table 3 (§8).

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument class: spot cryptocurrencies — the full Coinmarketcap cross-section with market capitalization ≥ USD 1 million (§3.1). The strategy requires a short leg: the factor is a long-short quintile spread, buying the highest-CTREND quintile and selling the lowest (§4.1). The paper shorts spot coins and never discusses futures, perpetuals, or options as vehicles; no borrow-cost or short-selling-constraint model is described, and no cost of shorting is charged beyond the transaction-cost rate on the short leg (§7.2). Long and short legs are reported separately as quintile columns (L, 2, 3, 4, H) in Tables 2, 3, 6, 11, 12, and 15, though many of those cells are garbled in this extraction (see Extraction gaps). No leverage is used; the spread is zero-investment, and gross exposure is not discussed. Replication would require daily OHLC prices, dollar volume, and market capitalization from Coinmarketcap for the full cross-section over 2015–2022, the LTW factor returns, a stablecoin list, and the Lucey et al. (2022) cryptocurrency uncertainty index (§5.2, Table 7). The paper does not state whether sample membership is reconstructed point-in-time, and survivorship and delisting handling are not addressed. Microcap influence is mitigated by the USD 1 million cap filter and by estimating all regressions with value-weighted sums of squared residuals (§3.1, §3.3).

## Strategies

### CTREND (the paper's only strategy)

**The 28 input indicators** (§3.2.1, Table 2): (a) momentum oscillators — rsi (ratio of average gains to average losses over the preceding 14 days, bounded [0, 100]), stochK and stochD (price relative to its 14-day range; stochD is the 3-day moving average of stochK), stochRSI (current rsi relative to its 14-day range), and cci (deviation of price from its moving average; overbought > 100, oversold < −100); (b) moving averages — 3-, 5-, 10-, 20-, 50-, 100-, and 200-day simple moving averages of price, each scaled by the closing price (Han et al., 2016b), plus macd (26-day EMA minus 12-day EMA of daily closes, expressed as a percentage of the fast EMA, making it the percentage price oscillator) and macd diff signal (macd minus its 9-day EMA); (c) volume indicators — 3-, 5-, 10-, 20-, 50-, 100-, and 200-day SMAs of dollar trading volume, normalized by current volume (Liu et al., 2023), volmacd (the volume analogue of macd, i.e., the percentage volume oscillator), volmacd diff signal, and the Chaikin money flow (chaikin); (d) volatility indicators — Bollinger lower, middle, and upper bands (middle = 20-day moving average of closes; bands = middle ± 2 standard deviations of previous closes), scaled by current closing price, and boll width ((upper − lower)/middle). The paper states the full formulas are in Appendix A, which is not present in this extraction. Horizon choices are justified as "the common estimation horizons suggested in the literature" to avoid data snooping (§3.2). The indicators are computed over daily data; longer SMAs (400–1000 day) used in the equity literature are omitted because cryptocurrency time series are short (footnote 2).

**Aggregation into CTREND** (§3.3): the signal is a one-week-ahead expected return. For each indicator j, a univariate Fama-MacBeth regression is estimated over a fixed rolling window of M = 52 weeks:
$$r_{it} = \alpha_t + z_{i,jt-1}\beta_{jt} + u_{it} \quad \forall j, t \quad (7)$$
with coefficients smoothed over the M periods, $\bar{\alpha}_t = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\hat{\alpha}_{t-m}$ and $\bar{\beta}_{j,t} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\hat{\beta}_{j,t-m}$ (eqs. 4–5), yielding one forecast per indicator, $\hat{r}_{i,t+1}^j = \bar{\alpha}_t + \bar{\beta}_{j,t} z_{i,j,t}$ (eq. 8). The naive combined forecast is the equal-weight average of the J forecasts (eq. 9). These forecasts are then fed into a pooled elastic-net regression, $r_{i,t} = \xi + \sum_{j=1}^J \theta_j \hat{r}_{i,t}^j + \eta_t$ (eq. 10), with the L1/L2 mixing parameter set to 0.5 and the regularization strength λ optimized by the corrected Akaike information criterion (footnote 6). The final CTREND forecast is the equal-weight average of only those univariate forecasts whose elastic-net weight satisfies θ_j > 0 (eq. 11) — the CS-C-ENet estimator. Before estimation, characteristics are transformed into cross-sectional ranks mapped to [−0.5, 0.5] (Kelly et al., 2019; Gu et al., 2020); regressions minimize value-weighted sums of squared residuals (Hou et al., 2020); and the cross-sectional mean is subtracted from returns to control for time-fixed effects (footnote 10).

**Portfolio construction**: quintile portfolios, value-weighted, rebalanced weekly (§4.1). Signal inputs use data through week t−1 (z_{i,j,t−1} in eq. 7); the baseline assumes no implementation lag, with a one-day-lag variant tested in robustness (Table 7, "return lead"). Holding period is one week; variants up to six weeks are tested (§7.3). Turnover of the long-short portfolio is 68.45% per week (Table 15).

**Rationale and counterparties**: framed as trend continuation driven by investor information-processing, not as compensation for risk — the authors invoke models where uninformed traders rationally infer long-run fundamentals from past prices (Wang, 1993; Han et al., 2016b; Liu et al., 2023) and cite evidence that crypto retail investors chase past returns (Kogan et al., 2023; Hackethal et al., 2022; Weber et al., 2023) (§2). The framework distinguishes informed investors from uninformed trend-following traders; the paper does not otherwise identify who is on the other side of the trade.

## Test setup

Sample period April 2015–May 2022 (423 weekly observations), chosen because earlier data are needed to compute indicators; no separate out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported for the main sorts — the only out-of-sample exercise is the frontier-expansion test, which uses out-of-sample mean-variance-efficient portfolios over April 2016–May 2022 (§6.1, Table 10). Universe: 3,245 unique coins, from below 100 in 2015 to 2,213 in 2021 (Table 1). Data source: Coinmarketcap.com (high/open/low/close prices, volume, market capitalization). Screens: require non-missing close, volume, and market cap; drop observations where market cap exceeds that of Bitcoin (10 daily observations in total, footnote 1); require market cap ≥ USD 1 million; truncate returns at the 0.5% and 99.5% percentiles (§3.1). Survivorship and delisting are not addressed. Headline numbers are gross; net returns are reported under transaction-cost assumptions of 30 bps long / 40 bps short (Bianchi et al., 2022) with 40/50 and 50/60 bps variants (§7.2). Benchmarks: the one-factor CCAPM on the LTW market factor CMKT (eq. 1) and the LTW three-factor model CMKT, CSMB, CMOM (eq. 2). Factor returns are weekly; t-statistics in Table 5 use Newey–West (1987) adjustments, and the paper otherwise does not state the standard-error convention.

What the paper concludes from these: the CTREND long-short spread is profitable, monotonically increasing from bottom to top quintile, and cannot be spanned by the LTW factors (all six spanning-test p-values < 1%, Table 9); a CMKT+CSMB+CTREND model makes CMOM redundant (adding CMOM to it yields an insignificant frontier-expansion alpha of 0.37%, t = 1.02, while adding CTREND to the LTW model yields 2.77%, t = 3.80, Table 10); and the TREND model leaves only two of the tested anomaly/indicator portfolios with significant alphas — mcap (α = −1.58, t = −3.40) and stochK (α = 1.06, t = 1.98, borderline) (Table 12). The paper favors the TREND model as a potential benchmark for cryptocurrency asset pricing on grounds of lower pricing errors, fewer significant alphas, and the GRS test (§6.2). No Sharpe ratio net of costs, no drawdown, and no capacity estimate are reported anywhere.

## Robustness

- **Subperiods and market states** (§5.2, Table 6): sample split into equal halves at the first week of November 2018; high/low states by market volatility (value-weighted average of daily-return standard deviations over the previous week), by the cryptocurrency uncertainty index of Lucey et al. (2022), and by bull/bear (12-month trailing market return above/below its median). Returns decline but remain significant in the second half (3.26% per week); they are higher in stable periods (5.54%) than volatile ones (2.27%), and positive in both bull (4.49%) and bear (3.25%) markets; LTW alphas are "almost unchanged" in the second half.
- **Non-standard errors** (§5.3, Figure 4, Table 8): across 36,864 research-design combinations, the CTREND factor's Sharpe ratio is positive and significant at 5% (Lo, 2002 test) in 78% of combinations, versus 56% for CSMB and 49% for CMOM. CS-C-ENet Sharpe ratios range −0.62 to 9.14 with median 1.35; CSMB max 4.60, median 0.94; CMOM max 2.30, min −4.47, median 0.83. Excluding volume indicators raises the median CTREND Sharpe from 1.25 to 1.45; a $1 price filter lowers it from 1.69 to 1.14; adding a one-day implementation lag decreases CTREND profitability — the sentence as printed reads "but not for the CSMB and CTREND factors," naming CTREND twice, presumably a typo for CMOM (§5.3). The simple multivariate Fama-MacBeth estimator shows much worse, more dispersed performance than the penalized approaches (§5.3, Figure 4 Panel D).
- **Coin/token split**: results qualitatively unchanged (footnote 7; Table B.2, not in this extraction).
- No further robustness sections exist beyond the above; the extended-holding-period analysis (§7.3) doubles as persistence evidence.

## Specification search

Table 7 enumerates 36,864 design combinations: outlier treatment (truncation vs winsorization, at 0.5% or 1%), stablecoins (include/exclude), market-cap filter (none / $1m / $2m), price filter (none / $1), estimation method (FM, CFM, CS-C-ENet, POLS, CPOLS, C-ENet), estimation window (expanding/rolling), in-sample observations (26/52/72/104 weeks), weighting (equal/value), breakpoints (terciles/quintiles), return lead (0/1 day), and volume indicators (exclude/include). Results are reported only as aggregate distributions (Figure 4) and medians per design choice (Table 8), not per combination. Table 2 reports all 28 single-indicator quintile sorts; Table 5 reports 12 cross-sectional regression specifications; Table 11 reports 11 anomaly sorts; Table B.4 (not in this extraction) is referenced as covering additional anomalies that produced no significant spreads.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§8): the study relies on a number of preselected technical features; they point to Jiang et al. (2020) and Kaczmarek and Pukthuanthong (2023), who extract information directly from price charts and their graphical representations, as an alternative direction. They also call for future work on the nature and sources of the CTREND effect — mechanisms generating price patterns in the cryptocurrency universe have been "examined... limited thus far."

## Separable variants for our pipeline

- The aggregate CTREND signal via CS-C-ENet (eq. 11), with the full 28-indicator input set (§3.3).
- Each of the 28 individual technical indicator signals, each with its own quintile-sort results in Table 2: rsi, stochK, stochD, stochRSI, cci; sma 3/5/10/20/50/100/200d; macd, macd diff signal; volsma 3/5/10/20/50/100/200d; volmacd, volmacd diff signal; chaikin; boll low/mid/high/width (§3.2.1).
- Alternative aggregation estimators reported: multivariate Fama-MacBeth (FM), combined Fama-MacBeth (CFM), pooled OLS (POLS), combined pooled OLS (CPOLS), and C-ENet (§5.3, Table 8).
