Category: **comparison** — several strategy specifications (minimum-variance, shrinkage-regularised, CARA utility with fourth-order moments, and reduced-moment variants) are evaluated against a Fama–MacBeth characteristic-sorted benchmark, for each of three conditioning characteristics (size, 1-month momentum, tokenomics) in cryptocurrency markets.

### Claim

The paper extends mean–variance portfolio construction to incorporate skewness and kurtosis via a fourth-order Taylor expansion of CARA utility, and applies this framework to characteristic-based long-short portfolios in cryptocurrency markets. Using size (log market cap), 1-month momentum, and tokenomics (supply-growth based) as conditioning characteristics, the author tests whether higher-moment optimisation improves upon a characteristic-based Fama–MacBeth benchmark. Headline results differ by characteristic: for size, CARA portfolios achieve a Sharpe ratio of 0.92 versus 0.28 for the FM benchmark (JK Z = −12.26, p < 0.01, Table 2); for 1-month momentum, the FM benchmark dominates (FM Sharpe 0.25–0.26 vs CARA 0.23, JK Z positive but not significant for CARA, Table 3); for tokenomics, shrinkage-regularised CARA portfolios outperform FM (Sharpe 0.33–0.37 vs 0.25, JK Z = −2.17 to −3.64, p < 0.05, Table 4), but only when kurtosis is not penalised. Certainty-equivalent spanning tests show that size and tokenomics (with kurtosis control) generate welfare gains not spanned by the FM benchmark, while 1-month momentum does not. Data spans December 2015 through March 2024, with a backtest window of roughly January 2021–January 2024 (37 monthly observations for size, 36 for tokenomics, 15–37 for momentum).

### Fit with our constraints

All strategies are spot-cryptocurrency long-short portfolios (conditioning characteristic defines the long-short direction). The short leg is required: the framework imposes dollar neutrality ($\alpha^\top \mathbf{1}=0$) and unit characteristic exposure ($\alpha^\top c=1$). The paper acknowledges that shorting is infeasible for smaller tokens and states that "where applicable, portfolios are either constrained to long-only allocations or hedged using large-cap proxies such as Bitcoin or Ethereum" (§3.8). Long and short legs are not reported separately; only portfolio-level metrics are given. No leverage is described. Execution timing assumes a one-month lag between the characteristic observation (end of the 60th month of the rolling window) and portfolio formation. Returns are reported gross; no transaction cost or financing cost model is described. Data replication requires monthly close prices, market capitalisation, and circulating supply for the top 500 cryptocurrencies from CoinMarketCap (December 2015–March 2024), plus Fama-French risk-free rates. A 60-month continuous-history filter is applied, which restricts the estimation sample to the 29 tickers listed in Table 1 and limits the backtest to 36–37 months.

### Strategies

**FM benchmark (Fama–MacBeth, Lioui–Misra specification).** For each month $t$, a cross-sectional regression $R_{i,t} = \alpha_t + \lambda_{C,t} C_{i,t-1} + \varepsilon_{i,t}$ (Equation 2) is estimated. The unconditional price of risk is $\bar{\lambda}_C = \frac{1}{T}\sum_t \lambda_{C,t}$ (Equation 3) with standard errors from the time-series dispersion of monthly estimates. The "FM portfolio" whose Sharpe ratio serves as the benchmark is not explicitly constructed in the extraction — only the regression procedure is described. The paper treats the FM characteristic premium as the benchmark portfolio (Tables 2–7).

**Minimum-variance long-short portfolio (MV).** A zero-cost, characteristic-mimicking portfolio: $\min_\alpha \alpha^\top \Sigma \alpha$ s.t. $\alpha^\top \mathbf{1}=0$, $\alpha^\top c=1$. Closed-form solution: $\alpha = \Sigma^{-1}\bigl(-\frac{B}{AF-B^2}\mathbf{1} + \frac{A}{AF-B^2}c\bigr)$, where $A=\mathbf{1}^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}$, $B=\mathbf{1}^\top\Sigma^{-1}c$, $F=c^\top\Sigma^{-1}c$ (Appendix A). No shrinkage, no higher moments.

**Shrinkage-regularised MV.** Same constraints, objective $\alpha^\top\Sigma\alpha + \rho\|\alpha\|^2$, replacing $\Sigma^{-1}$ with $(\Sigma+\rho I)^{-1}$ (Appendix A.1). $\rho$ is the shrinkage parameter.

**CARA utility with fourth-order moments.** The investor maximises the fourth-order Taylor expansion of expected CARA utility: $\mathbb{E}[U(W)] \approx -\exp(-\lambda\bar{W})\bigl[1 + \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sigma_p^2 - \frac{\lambda^3}{6}s_p^3 + \frac{\lambda^4}{24}\kappa_p^4\bigr]$ (Equation 6), where $\sigma_p^2 = \alpha^\top M_2\alpha$, $s_p^3 = \alpha^\top M_3(\alpha\otimes\alpha)$, $\kappa_p^4 = \alpha^\top M_4(\alpha^{\otimes 3})$, with $M_2$, $M_3$, $M_4$ the covariance, co-skewness, and co-kurtosis tensors (Equations 7–10). Risk aversion $\lambda$ and shrinkage $\rho$ are searched over a grid ($\lambda \in \{3,5\}$, $\rho \in \{0.5,0.8\}$). The problem is solved numerically with a nonlinear optimiser (Equation 15, Appendix A). The paper also tests reduced-moment specifications: models that omit either the skewness term ("Shrinkage-M3, No Skewness") or the kurtosis term ("Shrinkage-M4, No Kurtosis"), or both ("Shrinkage-M3M4"), per Table 4 notes.

**Economic rationale.** The authors frame size as a proxy for liquidity, investor attention, and limited arbitrage in crypto markets, consistent with behavioural underreaction. Momentum is attributed to speculative retail trading, delayed information diffusion, and self-reinforcing price trends in a less efficient market structure. Tokenomics is treated as a forward-looking, structural characteristic that captures supply discipline, dilution risk, and scarcity — factors exogenous to short-term price fluctuations. The authors do not explicitly designate counterparties, but for momentum the implied counterparty is retail speculators; for size and tokenomics the counterparty is not specified.

**Universe and weights.** The initial universe is the top 500 cryptocurrencies by market capitalisation from CoinMarketCap. Assets are filtered to those with at least 60 consecutive months of non-missing returns and nonzero market capitalisation and excess returns. Outliers are winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentiles. Assets are retained through their final trading month (delisting adjustment). Portfolios are formed from the top 75 assets sorted by the conditioning characteristic (figure captions; the tokenomics section text mentions "top 100", a discrepancy). Weights are updated monthly using a 60-month rolling estimation window; the characteristic is observed at the end of the 60th month. Portfolio weights are monthly.

**Turnover.** Not quantified. The paper states qualitatively that tokenomics-conditioned portfolios show "lower turnover" and shrinkage reduces turnover. No turnover figures are reported.

### Test setup

**Data.** Daily close prices and market capitalisation for the top 500 cryptocurrencies from CoinMarketCap (December 2015–March 2024). Monthly returns computed from the last price in each month. Excess returns relative to the Fama-French risk-free rate. Circulating supply data for tokenomics. Assets with fewer than 60 continuous months of data are excluded; the 29 tickers listed in Table 1 constitute the estimation sample. Whether the universe is reconstructed point-in-time is not described. The backtest window is approximately January 2021–January 2024 (per Figure 51 caption; N = 37 monthly observations for size, 37 for momentum, 36 for tokenomics in Tables 2–4). The paper is dated May 2026; data ends March 2024. The paper reports no explicit out-of-sample or post-publication period.

**Survivorship and delisting.** Assets are retained in the dataset through their final available trading month. Return aggregations are adjusted to reflect exits due to project failure, illiquidity, or exchange delisting (§3.8).

**Costs.** Returns are reported gross. No transaction cost, bid-ask spread, or borrow cost model is described. Short-sale constraints are acknowledged qualitatively but not implemented in the reported portfolio returns (the portfolios are dollar-neutral long-short by construction).

**Benchmark.** The Fama–MacBeth characteristic-sorted portfolio (Lioui–Misra single-pass regression) serves as the benchmark for all strategy comparisons.

### Results

**Size (Table 2).** FM Sharpe = 0.28. CARA ($\lambda=3,5$) Sharpe = 0.92, JK Z = −12.26 (p < 0.01), DM stat = 8.79–10.31 (p < 0.01). Shrinkage-regularised CARA Sharpe = 0.61–0.92, JK Z = −6.89 to −12.26 (p < 0.01). Shrinkage-M4 and Shrinkage-M3M4 Sharpe ≈ 0.48–0.49, JK Z = −5.42 to −5.59 (p < 0.01). All model configurations statistically dominate the FM benchmark in both JK and DM tests. CE spanning (Table 5): CARA and shrinkage-only models yield positive and significant $\Delta$CE (0.15–0.26, NW t = 11.65–26.00). All minus-skew and minus-kurtosis models produce large negative $\Delta$CE (−85 to −141, NW t = −3.01 to −4.97), indicating that penalising higher moments destroys the welfare gains from size.

**1-month momentum (Table 3).** FM Sharpe ≈ 0.25–0.26. CARA Sharpe = 0.23, JK Z = 0.18–0.25 (p = 0.80–0.85, not significant). Shrinkage model Sharpe ≈ 0.01–0.06, JK Z = 4.31–8.34 (p < 0.01, FM dominates). DM statistics are positive and significant for most shrinkage specifications, suggesting gains in the time-series loss metric despite Sharpe underperformance. CE spanning (Table 6): nearly all specifications produce negative $\Delta$CE, ranging from −49.32 (t = −1.84, n.s.) to −12,379.47 (t = −10.05, p < 0.01). Two exceptions are a Shrinkage-M4 ($\lambda=5,\rho=0.5$) model ($\Delta$CE = 221.61, t = 1.31, n.s.) and a Shrinkage-M3M4 ($\lambda=5,\rho=0.5$) model ($\Delta$CE = 0.09, t = 10.55, p < 0.01).

**Tokenomics (Table 4).** FM Sharpe = 0.25. Unregularised CARA Sharpe = 0.11–0.17, JK Z = 2.39–4.79 (p ≤ 0.05, FM dominates). Shrinkage-regularised CARA Sharpe = 0.33–0.37, JK Z = −2.17 to −3.64 (p ≤ 0.03, model dominates). Shrinkage-M3 (No Skewness) Sharpe = 0.20–0.49; JK Z is negative and significant (−3.07 to −4.51, p < 0.01, model dominates) except for the $\lambda=5,\rho=0.8$ configuration (JK Z = 1.77, p = 0.08, not significant). Shrinkage-M4 (No Kurtosis) Sharpe = −0.19 to 0.09, JK Z = 9.03–16.29 (p < 0.01, FM dominates severely). CE spanning (Table 7): CARA and shrinkage-only models yield small negative $\Delta$CE (−0.26 to −2.16). Shrinkage-M4 models produce large positive $\Delta$CE (50–6,964, NW t = 1.995–3.006, p < 0.05). Shrinkage-M3M4 models are negative (−0.23 to −3.32). The authors conclude that tokenomics delivers welfare gains *only* when kurtosis is explicitly penalised, while removing skewness enhances Sharpe but CE gains are contingent on tail-risk control.

The authors conclude that size is the most robust characteristic, tokenomics is the strongest but tail-dependent, and 1-month momentum is fragile and unsuitable for higher-moment frameworks.

### Robustness

The paper has no formal robustness section. The following are embedded in the analysis: (i) ex-post realised moments confirm that higher-moment optimisation dampens realised kurtosis and maintains positive skewness relative to MV and CARA-only benchmarks; (ii) a cross-asset comparison (prose only, no table) contrasts the crypto results with equity-market findings from the author's dissertation equity chapter, noting that size and 6-month momentum (equities) are stable but yield limited incremental CE gains, while in crypto size and tokenomics reject CE spanning; (iii) the sensitivity of results to $\lambda$ and $\rho$ is discussed throughout. No subperiod splits, alternative universes, or delayed-execution tests are reported.

### Specification search

The grid covers $\lambda \in \{3,5\}$ and $\rho \in \{0.5,0.8\}$. Model families: MV, shrinkage MV, CARA, CARA-shrinkage, Shrinkage-M3 (no skewness), Shrinkage-M4 (no kurtosis), Shrinkage-M3M4 (neither). Three characteristics (size, momentum, tokenomics). Results are reported for all 14 configurations per characteristic in Tables 2–4 (rolling Sharpe/skewness/kurtosis/CE figures for each) and CE spanning tables (10–14 rows per characteristic). The paper does not state how many additional configurations were examined but not reported.

### Limitations and future work (authors')

The conclusion identifies two extensions: (i) applying the higher-moment framework to decentralised finance and structured digital assets, where return asymmetries are amplified by protocol-level incentives; (ii) integrating the framework with stochastic discount factor estimation to clarify how investor utility preferences shape equilibrium risk premia.

### Separable variants for our pipeline
