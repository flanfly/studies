Category: **literature review** — a PRISMA systematic review of 88 peer-reviewed articles on the determinants of cryptocurrency pricing. It contains no original return series, no portfolio sorts, and no strategy. The unit of analysis is the factor category: the paper's contribution is a taxonomy of documented pricing determinants, with the sign of each reported relationship as found in the underlying studies. This extraction therefore follows the empirical-paper template: the six factor categories stand in for strategies, and every sign reported below is the sign the reviewed studies report, not a result of this paper's own testing.

## Claim

The paper identifies and categorizes the factors that influence cryptocurrency pricing from a systematic review of 88 peer-reviewed journal articles published before August 2022, and maps the research methods used. The influential factors are classified into six non-mutually-exclusive categories: (i) supply and demand, (ii) technology, (iii) economics, (iv) market volatility, (v) investors' attributes and (vi) social media (Abstract; §"Cryptocurrency pricing factors"). In total, 48 different research methods are employed across the reviewed studies; the most common are the vector autoregression model (9 studies) and the autoregressive distributed lag model (6) (§"Research methods"). The authors state the review is "the first to systematically and comprehensively review the relevant literature on cryptocurrency to identify the factors of pricing fluctuation" (Abstract). No headline effect size, t-statistic, or sample of price data is reported anywhere: this is a synthesis of reported relationships, each cited to its source study. Bitcoin is the most studied currency (§"Publishing trends and currency focus").

## Fit with our constraints

This paper is a review, so the mechanical checks apply to the underlying studies it surveys rather than to any strategy of its own. No strategy is constructed; no short leg, leverage, holding period, or turnover is specified for anything; no returns are computed. The determinants surveyed are almost entirely price-level or return-level predictors for Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, expressed in spot markets; nothing in the taxonomy requires short stock or short options, but equally nothing is operationalized as a tradable specification. Replication would consist of re-doing the literature search rather than acquiring price data: three databases (Scopus, Web of Science, EBSCOhost), search terms ("cryptocurrency" OR "encryption currency" OR "digital money" OR "digital currency") AND ("factor" OR "determine") AND "(price)", English-language peer-reviewed articles only (§"Keywords and databases"). No gross/net distinction exists because no costs are modelled in any reviewed study as summarized here; the paper reports no transaction costs, financing costs, or survivorship treatment for any determinant.

## Strategies

The paper's unit of analysis is six factor categories (§"Cryptocurrency pricing factors"; Tables 4–9). Each is summarized below with the documented determinants and the sign of the reported relationship, verbatim where it matters.

### Supply and demand (Table 4)

Bitcoin supply is asymptotically capped at 21 million and governed by a cryptographic algorithm (§"Supply and demand"). A negative relationship between Bitcoin supply and price is reported by Kristoufek (2015, wavelet coherence), Ciaian et al. (2016, vector autoregression), and Dubey (2022, random-effect regression); conversely Wang and Vergne (2017, random-effect and fixed-effect analysis) find supply growth can drive prices up. Demand-side drivers are reported to have a stronger impact on price than supply (Ciaian et al. 2016). Reported positive determinants: transactional need for Bitcoin (Karaömer 2022, ARDL), Bitcoin used as a payment method (Polasik et al. 2015, OLS and tobit), network factors — wallet users, payment accounts, transaction accounts — as the main demand for cryptocurrencies (Liu & Tsyvinski 2021; Nakagawa & Sakemoto 2022), trading volume (Bouri, Vo & Saeed 2021, dynamic equicorrelation model), and the emergence of other cryptocurrencies, which positively affected Bitcoin returns (Będowska-Sojka et al. 2021, Garman–Klass analysis). The price, supply, and usage of Bitcoin are stated to be consistent with the quantity theory of money (Kristoufek 2015).

### Technology (Table 5)

Hash rate: a positive effect on Bitcoin returns is reported (Karaömer 2022), but Kjaerland et al. (2018, ARDL) argue the hash rate is irrelevant because the code makes Bitcoin supply deterministic, and Fantazzini and Kolodin (2020, cost of production model) find no direct effect on price. Mining difficulty: increases raise the Bitcoin price (Guizani & Nafti 2019, ARDL; Li & Wang 2017, ARDL; Kristoufek 2015, wavelet coherence), with a weak long-term impact (Guizani & Nafti 2019). Halving — the block subsidy falls by 50% every four years — positively affects price (Meynkhard 2019, comparative analysis; Ibrahim et al. 2020, VAR; Ramos & Zanko 2020). Mining cost: positively related to price (Sapkota & Grobys 2020, portfolio analysis; Chico-Frias 2021, cost of production model), contested by Baldan and Zen (2020, VAR) and Liu and Tsyvinski (2021). Transaction costs are reported as an important determinant (Crettez & Morhaim 2022, OLG model). Blockchain-technology factors show only small impact on Bitcoin price (Chen 2021, VECM) but matter for Ethereum (Kim et al. 2021); number of unique collaborators and proposals drives returns (Wang & Vergne 2017); consensus-protocol technologies influence returns (Chowdhury et al. 2022). Vo et al. (2022, OLS) report pricing is less sensitive to technological factors and more responsive to economic factors in the long term.

### Economics (Table 6)

Exchange rates: negative relationship with Bitcoin price (Karaömer 2022; Zhu et al. 2017, VECM; Polasik et al. 2015 for USD and EUR; Poyser 2019, Bayesian structural time series, for the Chinese yuan); positive effects on returns via LASSO for JPY/USD, CNY/USD, USD/EUR, GBP/USD (Panagiotidis et al. 2018) and for EUR/USD, GBP/USD, JPY/USD (Huang et al. 2022). Almansour et al. (2020) find USD/JPY, USD/GBP and USD/AU insignificant at 95% confidence, with GBP significant at 90%. Interest rates: higher Federal funds and Chinese interbank rates significantly increase price volatility (Nguyen et al. 2022, GARCH/EGARCH/fixed-effect); higher Fed rates negatively affect Bitcoin price (Havidz et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2017; Jareño et al. 2020), while Panagiotidis et al. (2018) find a positive LASSO effect. CPI: long-term negative (Zhu et al. 2017), short-term positive as a hedging channel (Wang, Sarker & Bouri 2022, wavelet-based), and CPI news with no significant relationship (Corbet et al. 2020, sentiment index). Gold: positive for Bitcoin price (Lamothe-Fernandez et al. 2020, deep learning; Ciaian et al. 2016; Pogudin et al. 2019, wavelet coherence; Panagiotidis et al. 2018). Oil: positive (Pogudin et al. 2019; Panagiotidis et al. 2018) and negative (Jareño et al. 2020; Ciaian et al. 2016). Stock markets: Dow Jones positive (Ciaian et al. 2016; Lamothe-Fernandez et al. 2020) but long-term negative (Zhu et al. 2017); S&P 500 positive (Bakas et al. 2022; Jareño et al. 2020; Nguyen 2022; Vo et al. 2022); Chinese stock index positive (Bouoiyour & Selmi 2015, ARDL); Nikkei positive (Panagiotidis et al. 2018); fear in the equity market positively correlated with Bitcoin, Ethereum and Litecoin returns (Anamika et al. 2021); Havidz et al. (2021) find a negative but insignificant stock-market effect. Economic policy uncertainty: Cryptocurrency Policy Uncertainty Index and Economic Uncertainty Index negatively associated with Bitcoin price (Hasan et al. 2022; Wu et al. 2022; Karaömer 2022; Kalyvas et al. 2020; Wang, Sarker & Bouri 2022; Jareño et al. 2020), with European EPU the most important variable for Bitcoin returns (Panagiotidis et al. 2018). CBDC-related policy has a positive effect (Scharnowski 2022, fixed-effect).

### Market volatility (Table 7)

Downside risk has a positive cross-sectional relationship with future returns (Zhang et al. 2021, univariate portfolio analysis). Three common risk factors — cryptocurrency market returns, market capitalization (size), and momentum — drive returns (Liu et al. 2020, Fama–MacBeth; Liu et al. 2022, three-factor model), with value/growth also relevant (Wang & Chong 2021, Fama-French three-factor). MAX momentum (Li et al. 2021), reversal factors (Jia et al. 2022, MSM three-factor), idiosyncratic volatility (Leirvik 2022; Liu & Tsyvinski 2021) and liquidity (Zhang & Li 2020) are reported as return predictors. Risk and uncertainty of the Bitcoin system negatively affect price (Ciaian et al. 2016); blockchain-specific risk has a stronger effect (Nadler & Guo 2020, asset pricing model). Hacking events drive volatility (Corbet et al. 2019); terrorist attacks positively affect returns (Almaqableh et al. 2022, asset pricing and ARCH). COVID-19 had a positive short-term effect on Bitcoin price (Çelik et al. 2020; Lee et al. 2022, structural break analysis) and increased conditional volatility (Apergis 2022; Nguyen 2022, VAR-GARCH; Sarkodie et al. 2022), with increased cases/deaths positively linked to returns. Exchange-platform risk (e.g., Mt. Gox) is a potential pricing risk (Zhu et al. 2017).

### Investors' attributes (Table 8)

Investor attention is positively related to price and predicts future volatility (Smales 2021, quantile regression; Zhu et al. 2021, value-at-risk; Al Guindy 2021, VAR; Mohamed 2021). Attractiveness indicators matter with variation over time (Guizani & Nafti 2019; Hakim das Neves 2020). Popularity is positively related, especially short-term (Karaömer 2022; Garcia et al. 2014, VAR; Nepp & Karpeko 2022, ARDL/GARCH; Polasik et al. 2015). Positive sentiment correlates positively with price (Kjaerland et al. 2018; Patel et al. 2020, LSTM/GRU; Gurrib & Kamalov 2022; Sapkota 2022; Garcia et al. 2014), while negative sentiment and "buzz" are negatively associated with returns (Huynh 2021, textual analysis; Wang & Vergne 2017). Positive feedback trading increases volatility (Wang et al. 2022). Influential individuals' tweets (Elon Musk) contribute to bubble formation (Shahzad et al. 2022); crypto experts significantly affect returns (Gerritsen et al. 2022). Unethical-discussion frequency is negatively associated with price (Barth et al. 2020, text analytic); developers' emotions drive Bitcoin and Ethereum volatility (Bartolucci et al. 2020, recurrent neural network); emotional factors predict trading volume and return volatility (Ahn & Kim 2021, corpus linguistics); investors' mood is linked to volatility (Rubbaniy et al. 2022, quantile-on-quantile).

### Social media (Table 9)

Wikipedia views positively and statistically significantly affect Bitcoin price (Figà-Talamanca & Patacca 2020; Ciaian et al. 2016; Phillips & Gorse 2018, wavelet coherence). Google Trends/Search volume is positively associated with Bitcoin returns (Polasik et al. 2015; Aslanidis et al. 2022, Shannon transfer entropy; Panagiotidis et al. 2018 — Google Search identified as the most important variable; Chuffart 2022; Bakas et al. 2022; Ciaian et al. 2016; Phillips & Gorse 2018), with online factors having a long-term positive relationship with price (Phillips & Gorse 2018). The sole negative report is Smuts (2019, LSTM), who finds Google Trends search volume negatively affected Bitcoin and Ethereum price movements.

## Test setup

The "test" is the review protocol itself (§Method): the PRISMA approach, applied to a search of Scopus, Web of Science and EBSCOhost with the stated keyword string. The initial search yielded 563 articles — Scopus 313, Web of Science 72, EBSCOhost 178. EndNote X9 removed 185 duplicates; 213 were removed on title/abstract screening; of the 165 assessed for eligibility, 76 were excluded for not examining pricing factors, 18 as conference papers, 15 as non-English; 56 met eligibility and 32 more were added from reference-list examination, giving 88 articles. All studies published before August 2022 were eligible; the earliest reviewed article appeared in 2014. The 88 studies come from 54 journals (Finance Research Letters 27 articles, PLoS One 4, then 2 each for Financial Innovation, Journal of Risk and Financial Management, Journal of Behavioural Finance, Studies in Economics and Finance, International Review of Financial Analysis) and 18 regions (Europe 29, UK 12, China 12, USA 9, UAE 4, Russia 3, India 3, Canada 3, Australia 3, South Korea 2) (Tables 1–2). No price data, instruments, survivorship or delisting treatment, transaction costs, or benchmarks exist because the paper computes no returns. Whether any underlying study is gross or net is not stated by this paper for any determinant.

The authors' conclusion is that cryptocurrency pricing is driven by a broad set of fundamental, technological, economic, volatility, behavioural and social-media factors, that "Bitcoin continues to be the most studied," and that demand-side drivers, investor attention/sentiment and online search activity are among the most consistently documented determinants (§"Cryptocurrency pricing factors"; Implications). Commonly reported strategy metrics — Sharpe ratios, t-statistics on any factor premium, turnover, cost-adjusted returns — are absent, because the paper reports none and no reviewed study's magnitudes are reproduced in it.

## Robustness

The paper has no robustness section of its own. The closest analogue is cross-study disagreement, which it reports explicitly rather than resolves: supply sign conflicts (Ciaian et al. 2016 negative vs. Wang & Vergne 2017 positive), hash rate significant (Karaömer 2022) vs. irrelevant (Kjaerland et al. 2018; Fantazzini & Kolodin 2020), oil positive (Pogudin et al. 2019) vs. negative (Jareño et al. 2020), CPI negative (Zhu et al. 2017) vs. positive short-term (Wang et al. 2022), Google Trends positive (most studies) vs. negative (Smuts 2019), and exchange-rate significance depending on confidence level (Almansour et al. 2020). No subperiod or out-of-sample testing is reported for any determinant.

## Specification search

Not applicable in the strategy sense. The review reports its search funnel in full — 563 → 88 (all counts in §Screening, reproduced above) — and states the number of distinct research methods (48) it catalogued, but it does not report per-study parameter settings, sort variants, or universes; these live in the 88 underlying articles, which the review does not individually enumerate beyond the citation tables.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own: (1) only peer-reviewed articles were considered; conference papers, government reports and theses could enlarge the set; (2) only three databases were used, excluding non-English and other-indexed work; (3) some relevant articles may have been missed given the arbitrary nature of the keyword, title and abstract inclusion/exclusion criteria; (4) studies with a statistically validated measurement scale of influential factors would improve the literature. Suggested future work: examine coins other than Bitcoin; study technological dimensions of cryptocurrency and its software-update dynamic; examine the connection between cryptocurrency and existing financial markets and the impact of economic policy; quantify sentiment factors and the determinants of investor sentiment; and address neglected factors such as cultural and political factors and the development of financial technologies (§"Limitations of the study and future research"; Conclusion).
