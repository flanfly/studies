Category: **methodology** — the paper develops a test-asset construction methodology — characteristic-unsorted random portfolios with varying stock selection, initial weighting, breadth, and weight management — for evaluating factor models, and benchmarks six published models (CAPM, FF3, Carhart, FF5, FF6, q5) under it. There is no strategy backtest: the random portfolios are test assets, and all reported figures are pricing errors, spanning tests, or significant-alpha shares. A comparison element is embedded: the six models are ranked across designs, but the paper's own contribution is the evaluation design, and its conclusion is that rankings are construction-dependent, not that any model is tradable.

## Claim

Factor-model performance depends on test-asset construction. The paper forms characteristic-unsorted random portfolios from a broad CRSP investable universe, varying stock selection (market-equity-proportional MEP or uniform UNIF sampling), initial weights (equal EW, value VW, square-root market-equity SQME, random RAND), portfolio breadth (selection ratios 0.5%–100%), and weight management (buy-and-hold BH, daily constant-weight CW, and intermediate rebalancing frequencies) (Abstract, §3.2). On these portfolios, rankings "shift materially": buy-and-hold favors FF5 and FF6, whereas daily constant-weighting favors FF3, "the most stable model across designs" (Abstract). Direct factor-spanning tests favor q5 as providing the broadest sample mean–variance opportunity set — adding q5-specific factors to FF6 raises the annualized maximum Sharpe ratio from 1.311 to 2.205 (ΔSR² = 3.143) at daily frequency (Table 4.1) — yet q5 leaves comparatively large, construction-sensitive pricing errors on random portfolios, e.g. 490.3 bp mean absolute error under UNIF–EW CW at the 5% selection ratio (Table 6.2). Sample: January 3, 1967 – December 31, 2024, daily CRSP data; 14,598 daily and 696 monthly observations (§3.1, §4.1). No t-statistic or standard error accompanies the random-portfolio error levels themselves; significance is reported as shares of portfolios with 5%-significant alphas (Newey–West standard errors), and spanning is tested with GRS and Newey–West HAC Wald tests (§4.1, §6).

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is US common equities (NYSE, AMEX, NASDAQ) and published factor portfolios; no crypto or derivative instruments. All random portfolios are long-only by construction, and no strategy requires a short leg; whether any leg could be expressed in futures or perpetuals is not addressed because no strategy is proposed. No leverage is used. Replication would need daily CRSP data (prices, shares outstanding, volume, delisting returns, market equity per Eq. (11)–(14)) from January 3, 1967 through December 31, 2024, factor and risk-free series from the Kenneth R. French Data Library and global-q, and the paper's screening and portfolio-generation procedure including the fixed random seed 20260614 (footnote 1). Headline figures are gross; the paper explicitly states daily and weekly rebalancing "abstract from transaction costs and market impact" (§9.4) and reports no cost-adjusted figures anywhere. Universe membership is point-in-time by construction: screening uses only information available by month-end and the universe becomes effective the first trading day of the following month (§3.1); stocks that later delist remain in portfolios through their actual delisting date with the CRSP delisting return, so no ex post survivorship selection is applied (§3.2.4).

## Strategies

There are no trading strategies. The methodological unit is the random-portfolio test-asset design, applied identically to six published factor models; the six models are the subjects being evaluated, not signals. What the method takes as input: daily CRSP returns and a month-end-updated investable universe; the month-end market equity is $ME_{i,t} = |PRC_{i,t}| \times SHROUT_{i,t} \times 1{,}000$ (Eq. (11)), with liquidity measured as average daily dollar volume over the trailing 63 trading days (Eq. (13)–(14)), a cumulative market-equity share $CumME_{i,t}$ (Eq. (12)), hysteresis thresholds (exit if $CumME > 0.999$ or ADV63 at/below the 2.5th percentile; entry only if $CumME \le 0.995$ and ADV63 at/above the fifth percentile), and a NASDAQ liquidity-screen grace period of 63 trading days after volume first becomes observable (§3.1). What it produces: 500 portfolios per design cell at selection ratios $\rho \in \{0.005, 0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90, 1.00\}$ (Eq. (15); one unique draw at $\rho = 1$), formed at each June month-end, held from the first trading day of July through the following June, with initial weights per Eqs. (17)–(20) (EW $1/K_{p,t}$; VW $ME_{i,t}/\sum_j ME_{j,t}$; SQME $\sqrt{ME_{i,t}}/\sum_j \sqrt{ME_{j,t}}$; RAND normalized uniform draws), and weight paths per Eq. (21) (CW, daily rebalancing to target weights) or Eqs. (22)–(26) (BH, weights drift with realized returns including delisting treatment). Stock sets are nested across selection ratios via a common random ordering per formation year and portfolio identifier, reducing Monte Carlo contamination (§3.2.1). The evaluation metric is the cross-sectional mean of annualized absolute pricing errors $\mathcal{L}_1$ / $\mathcal{L}_2$ (Eq. (5)–(6)), tail errors, significant-alpha shares, and time-series $R^2$ (§2.2, §6). What it is benchmarked against: conventional characteristic-sorted portfolios (367 Fama–French sorts, 1,202 global-q anomaly portfolios, 47 industry portfolios, all with complete daily returns over the common sample, §5), direct factor-spanning tests among traded factors (§4), and the same random portfolios under alternative weight-management rules (§8). The central theoretical object is $\mathcal{L}^{CW}_{\mathcal{D}}(M) = \alpha'_M Q_{\mathcal{D}} \alpha_M$ with $Q_{\mathcal{D}} = E_{w \sim \mathcal{D}}[ww']$ (Eq. (8)): different asset-generating distributions weight the same underlying pricing-error vector differently.

### Models under evaluation

- **CAPM, FF3, FF5** (Fama and French 1992/1993/2015): French Data Library factors; the paper references their factor sets by citation only (§2.5).
- **FF6**: market, size, value, profitability, investment, and momentum — the only Fama–French model with its factor set spelled out (§4.1).
- **Carhart** (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993; Carhart 1997): its factor set is not spelled out in the paper (§2.5).
- **q5** (Hou et al., 2015/2019/2020/2021/2024): global-q factors; q5 = market, size, investment (IA), return-on-equity (ROE), expected-growth (EG) (§2.5, §4.1).

Each model is paired with the market factor and risk-free rate from its own data source (Eq. (27)); the two market series are nearly identical — correlation 0.99997 over the 14,598 common trading days, mean daily absolute difference 0.36 bp, and 0.22 bp for the risk-free rates (footnote 2). This is a deliberate symmetry-preserving choice; the reported differences are said to be too large to be explained by data source (§3.3).

## Test setup

Sample period January 3, 1967 – December 31, 2024 (daily) with monthly observations formed by compounding daily returns within calendar months; the sample is the full available history, no out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported (§4.1). Instruments: an average of 77.6% of CRSP common stocks retained by the screen, preserving approximately 99.7% of aggregate market equity and 63-day average dollar volume; at December 2024, 2,426 of 3,804 common stocks, 99.78% of market equity, 99.28% of dollar volume (§3.1). Data sources: CRSP daily data; Kenneth R. French Data Library (FF factors, risk-free rate, 367 characteristic-sorted and 47 industry portfolios); global-q (q5 factors and 1,202 anomaly portfolios) (§2.5, §5). Survivorship/delisting: investable stocks remain in portfolios through actual delisting with CRSP delisting return; temporary missing returns set to zero; post-delisting cash earns the risk-free rate until the next annual formation (§3.2.4). Transaction costs: none; headline numbers are gross and the paper flags that rebalancing-frequency results are "not transaction-cost-adjusted strategy returns" (§8.4.4). The benchmark for model performance is the maximum-Sharpe/spanning criterion of Barillas and Shanken (2017) for the spanning exercise and return-unit pricing errors for the portfolio exercise; the two are kept deliberately separate (§2.2, §4.3). Random-portfolio regressions use Newey–West standard errors for alpha significance; spanning uses GRS and HAC Wald tests with lags of 21 trading days (daily) and six months (monthly) (§4.1).

Home-field asymmetry: FF6 beats q5 on 67.8% of matched Fama–French portfolios; q5 beats FF3 on 61.1% of matched global-q anomaly errors (§5.1). Under BH at the 5% selection ratio, q5 never attains the smallest mean absolute error and its worst cell is MEP–VW (110.3 bp, 100% significant alphas); CAPM, Carhart, and FF6 each win at least one of the eight cells (Table 6.1). Under CW, FF3 has the smallest error in six of eight cells, the exceptions being MEP–VW and MEP–SQME where CAPM wins; outside MEP–VW (14.7 bp, no significant alphas) q5 errors exceed 200 bp in MEP–EW/MEP–RAND and approach 490 bp in UNIF–EW/UNIF–RAND (Table 6.2). Paired BH–CW: BH is generally smaller (Carhart, FF5, FF6 favor BH in all eight cells; CAPM and q5 in seven); differences reach several hundred bp in UNIF–EW and UNIF–RAND; MEP–VW reverses for q5 — CW cuts its error by 95.6 bp and beats BH for all 500 matched portfolios (Table 6.3). Ablation: adding ROE to q3 improves BH pricing errors in seven of eight cells, but q5 (q4 + EG) exceeds q4 in seven of eight cells and in 62 of 64 cells across the full selection-ratio grid; under CW, q3 ranks first in 58 of 64 cells, and q5 exceeds q4 in 60 (Tables 7.1–7.3, §7.3). Breadth (ALL–UNIF–VW): under BH, Carhart ranks first at every selection ratio, falling to 3.4 bp at the 100% boundary vs. 21.6 (FF5), 17.7 (FF6), 20.1 (q5) bp; under CW, FF3 has the smallest error at every ratio while Carhart and FF6 plateau near 110 bp and q5 near 125 bp, with q5's significant-alpha share reaching 97.8% at 50% and 100% at 75%+ (Tables 8.5–8.6). Rebalancing frequency (5% ratio): in UNIF–EW, FF6 ranks first from annual through monthly rebalancing (40.4 bp quarterly, 37.7 bp monthly) but FF3 ranks first at weekly and daily (44.8 and 169.1 bp); in UNIF–VW the minimum moves from Carhart (annual) to CAPM (quarterly–weekly) to FF3 (daily) (Table 8.7). The authors conclude that the results yield no construction-invariant ranking, that q5's high maximum Sharpe ratio and its large random-portfolio pricing errors are compatible facts about different objective functions, and that "the question 'which model?' cannot be separated from 'which portfolios?'" (§10).

Commonly reported metrics absent: no Sharpe ratios or t-statistics for the random portfolios themselves (there are none), no turnover figures, and no capacity analysis; the only reported significance statistics are significant-alpha shares and the GRS/HAC/block-bootstrap tests. Mean R² declines relative to annual rebalancing in all 192 model–design–frequency comparisons, yet pricing errors sometimes fall, so "changes in pricing errors cannot be explained by time-series fit alone" (§8.4.3).

## Robustness

Four robustness exercises, all reproducing the baseline conclusion (§8): (i) NYSE-only universe (§8.1) — Carhart, CAPM, and q5 split the BH cell minima; FF5/FF6 deteriorate sharply (e.g., 256.4 bp for FF5 in MEP–VW BH); block-bootstrap inference rejects the mean-alpha null under BH in all eight cells for FF5/FF6 but only two for q5, while under CW q5 is rejected in six cells, FF5 in five, and FF3 in none. (ii) NYSE 20th-percentile market-equity breakpoint with NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ stocks retained (MEP20/UNIF20, §8.2) — microcaps amplify but do not explain q5's CW errors: q5 still never ranks first in any BH or CW cell, and under CW FF3 stays the most stable model (six of eight cells). (iii) Breadth sweep 0.5%–100% on ALL–UNIF–VW (§8.3) — diversification reduces stock-specific noise but does not eliminate model-specific errors, persisting at the 100% boundary. (iv) Intermediate rebalancing (annual/quarterly/monthly/weekly/daily, §8.4) — rank shifts are not confined to the BH/CW endpoints; the main exception to q5's deterioration is MEP–VW, where its error falls from 110.3 bp (annual) to 9.9 bp (weekly) with zero rejections from monthly rebalancing onward. Appendix A repeats the NYSE-only analysis with the 20% breakpoint applied within the NYSE universe; construction dependence persists, with CAPM and Carhart dividing BH minima and no model minimizing CW absolute errors everywhere (Tables A.1–A.2).

## Specification search

The paper reports the full grid rather than selected cells: 2 sampling rules × 4 weighting schemes × 2 weight-management rules = 8 baseline cells at the 5% selection ratio with 500 portfolios per cell (§6); 9 selection ratios (with nested stock sets) for the breadth sweep in two designs (§8.3); 5 rebalancing frequencies × 8 designs (two reported in the main text, six used for generality checks, §8.4); 4 ablation models (q3, q4, q3+EG, q5) on 3 conventional families, 8 BH cells, and 8 CW cells plus full 64-cell grid counts (§7). A count of total Monte Carlo portfolio-years is not given. All results are reported; no selected-only reporting is indicated.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own: in-sample maximum squared Sharpe ratios "may be inflated by estimation error and data mining" and should not be read as attainable out-of-sample performance (§9.4); daily/weekly rebalancing abstracts from transaction costs and market impact and is not proposed as implementable; the analysis is limited to long-only portfolios of US common stocks, leaving international markets, other asset classes, short-enabled portfolios, and realistic frictions open (§9.4); mean absolute error, RMSE, tail errors, and significant-alpha shares are a subset of possible loss functions, and the economically relevant distribution and loss depend on the purpose of the SDF approximation (§9.4). The mechanism behind q5's positive CW alphas is explicitly not identified; decomposing portfolio-level pricing errors into constituent betas and endogenous weight paths is flagged as a natural future direction (§9.2).

## Separable variants for our pipeline

This is a methodology paper, so the separable units are design elements and model comparisons rather than signals; no variant here is tradable as extracted.

- The random-portfolio design itself (§3.2): MEP vs. UNIF selection; EW/VW/SQME/RAND initial weights; BH vs. CW vs. intermediate rebalancing; selection ratios 0.5%–100% — each is a distinct test-asset population that could be used to score any candidate model's pricing errors, per Eq. (8)–(10).
- The six models (CAPM, FF3, Carhart, FF5, FF6, q5) as pricing-error benchmarks on any external test-asset population (§2.5).
- The q5 factor-ablation hierarchy (q3, q4, q3+EG, q5, Eqs. (31)–(34)) — the marginal ROE and EG contributions as separate objects (§7).
- The spanning-test structure between FF6 and q5 factor blocks, including the individual ΔSR² contributions of ME, IA, ROE, EG, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA, MOM (Tables 4.1–4.2).
- The three conventional test-asset families (367 Fama–French sorts, 1,202 global-q anomalies, 47 industry portfolios) as evaluation sets with documented home-field asymmetry (§5).
- The block-bootstrap inference procedure contrasting BH and CW mean-alpha rejections (§8.1.2, §8.2.3) — an evaluation statistic, not a signal.
