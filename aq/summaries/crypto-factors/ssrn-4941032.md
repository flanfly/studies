Category: **single-strategy** — one signal, the absolute sensitivity of a coin's returns to innovations in a marketwide sentiment index |β^∆CryptoSent|, developed and tested via portfolio sorts, and then repackaged as the sentiment factor CSTM inside a four-factor model. The factor model is a modification of Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022)'s three-factor model, and its baseline is only described by reference to that paper (§4.1).

## Claim

The paper constructs CryptoSent, a Baker–Wurgler (2006)-style composite sentiment index for the cryptocurrency market, and finds that coins with high absolute sensitivity to CryptoSent innovations earn lower returns over the subsequent week and month: the long-short spread (long low-sensitivity quintile, short high-sensitivity quintile) earns an average weekly premium of 2.5% (t = −3.75 on the 5−1 spread) and a monthly (4-week) premium of 5.1% (t = −3.28) (Table 3; §3.3; Conclusion). The relation is robust to replacing the PCA index with a nonlinear deep-learning autoencoder index (CryptoSentDL) and to equal-weighted portfolio construction (§5). Adding a sentiment factor (CSTM) to the market–size–momentum model of Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022) increases cross-sectional explanatory power by approximately 13% and reduces alphas across eleven characteristic-based long-short portfolios (Abstract). Sample: over 2,000 cryptocurrencies, January 2014 to February 2023 (Introduction; §2.2 says the sample "ends in March 2023" — the paper states both). The mechanism is framed as sentiment-driven mispricing, not risk compensation: both high-positive and high-negative sentiment exposures predict lower returns, a symmetric pattern the authors argue is inconsistent with risk-based pricing (§3.3).

## Fit with our constraints

Instruments are spot cryptocurrencies only (CoinMarketCap daily snapshots); no derivatives are used anywhere. The strategy requires a short leg (short the high-|β^∆CryptoSent| quintile), and the paper's universe includes thousands of coins down to $100k market cap, so shorting via perpetuals would only be expressible for the subset with liquid perp markets — the paper does not address shorting feasibility. Long and short legs are reported separately as quintile means (Group 1 and Group 5), so the long leg is recoverable in isolation (Table 3). Leverage: none reported. Headline returns are gross; no transaction cost model, turnover, capacity, or borrow-cost discussion is given anywhere. Replication would need CoinMarketCap daily snapshots (price, volume, market cap), Google Trends for "cryptocurrency", Twitter/X tweet counts for Bitcoin, Ethereum, Dogecoin, Bitcoin and Ethereum active-wallet counts, Ethereum ERC-20 verified-contract counts (ICO proxy), and Bitcoin on-chain transaction dollar volume; the sentiment index itself is posted at github.com/ronming1303/CryptoSent (footnote 7). Survivorship and delisting are not addressed. Point-in-time reconstruction is not discussed explicitly; CoinMarketCap snapshots are historical by construction, but the paper does not state this.

## Strategies

### Sentiment-sensitivity long-short strategy (|β^∆CryptoSent|, §3.3)

**Signal.** Weekly (and monthly, 4-week) portfolio sorts on |β^∆CryptoSent|, the absolute beta from the regression

$$r_t^i - r_t^f = \beta_0 + \beta_{CMKT} CMKT_t + \beta_{\Delta CryptoSent} \Delta CryptoSent_t + \epsilon_t^i, \quad (2)$$

estimated over the preceding 28 days, where CMKT is the cryptocurrency market excess return and ∆CryptoSent is the first difference of the index (taken to achieve stationarity). The absolute value is used because the authors find both high positive and high negative sensitivities predict lower returns (§3.3). **Rationale:** mispricing — sentiment-sensitive coins attract sentiment-driven retail demand, become temporarily overvalued, then revert (§3.3); the counterparty is the sentiment-driven investor, and the paper cites Frazzini and Lamont (2008) and Baker and Wurgler (2006). **Universe and screens:** all CoinMarketCap coins with price, volume, and market-cap data; coins with market capitalization below $100,000 are excluded as an illiquidity screen (footnote 6); results "robust to additional liquidity controls" and "not driven by illiquid or micro-cap cryptocurrencies" (§2.2); a price filter > $0.10 is applied in the robustness section (footnote 12); excluding stablecoins leaves Table 3 unchanged (footnote 9). **Sort and weights:** five quintiles, value-weighted excess returns of each quintile in the subsequent week; rebalanced weekly; long Group 1 (lowest |β|), short Group 5 (highest |β|) (§3.3). **Execution timing:** betas estimated on the prior 28 days, portfolios formed each week, returns measured the following week/month; the exact gap between signal date and portfolio formation is not specified. Turnover: not reported. **Results (Table 3):** weekly means Group 1 = 1.5% (t = 2.68), Group 2 = 1.5% (2.21), Group 3 = 1.7% (2.41), Group 4 = 1.3% (1.66), Group 5 = −1.1% (−1.43), spread 5−1 = −2.5% (−3.75); monthly (4-week) Group 1 = 6.7% (4.98), Group 5 = 1.6% (0.82), spread 5−1 = −5.1% (−3.28). Group 5 is insignificant at both horizons — the spread is partly a short-leg return. The 2.5% weekly spread annualizes to 260.11% (weekly compounding) and the 5.1% monthly to 81.64% (monthly compounding) (footnotes 3, 4).

**Sentiment index underlying the signal (§3.1–3.2).** CryptoSent is built from eight daily components: cryptocurrency market index (value-weighted, per Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu 2022), 30-day historical volatility of that portfolio, market trading dollar volume, Google Trends for "cryptocurrency" (rescaled by each month's overall trend value to be comparable across 2014–2023), tweet counts for Bitcoin/Ethereum/Dogecoin, active wallets on Bitcoin and Ethereum blockchains, number of new verified ERC-20 smart contracts (ICO proxy), and dollar value of Bitcoin transferred on-chain. Components span January 2014–March 2023, except tweets (from April 2014) and ICOs (from November 2015) (Table 2 note). Following Baker and Wurgler (2006), a first-stage index is the first principal component of the eight components and their lags (16 loadings); each component's lead or lag (whichever correlates higher with the first-stage index) enters a second PCA; coefficients are rescaled to unit variance, giving (Equation 1): CryptoSent^t = 0.4095 × Crypto Index^{t−1} + 0.2701 × Google Trend^t + 0.3759 × #Tweet^{t−1} + 0.3678 × Volatility^t + 0.3882 × Volume^{t−1} + 0.3840 × #Wallet^t + 0.3451 × Blockchain Transactions^{t−1} + 0.2554 × #ICO^t. The first principal component explains 80% of sample variance (§3.2). Component correlations run from 25.26% to 91.82%; CryptoSent's correlations with components run from 58.48% (#ICO) to 93.54% (Crypto Index) (Table 2).

### The four-factor model: CSTM, CSMB, CMOM (§4.1)

Baseline: the three-factor (market, size, momentum) model of Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022), described only by reference (§4.1). The modification: adding CSTM. CSTM is formed weekly by sorting coins into three groups on |β^∆CryptoSent| — bottom 15% "neutral" (N), middle 70% ("middle"), top 15% "sensitive" (S) (15th/85th percentile breakpoints, following Stambaugh and Yuan 2017) — value-weighted, with CSTM = sensitive minus neutral returns (the defining equation, "equation (5)", is referenced in the Table 5 caption but not displayed in the extracted text). CSMB and CMOM are built on 2×3 intersections of sentiment exposure with size (market cap) and past three-week returns, respectively, using the same 15/70/15 breakpoints:

$$CMOM = \frac{1}{2}(Neutral\ High + Sensitive\ High) - \frac{1}{2}(Neutral\ Low + Sensitive\ Low) \quad (3)$$

$$CSMB = \frac{1}{2}(Neutral\ Small + Sensitive\ Small) - \frac{1}{2}(Neutral\ Big + Sensitive\ Big) \quad (4)$$

CMKT is the value-weighted market return minus the one-month T-bill rate (§2.2). Factor correlations (Table 5) run from −7.97% to 8.65% (CMKT–CSTM −0.38%, CMKT–CSMB −0.59%, CMKT–CMOM −5.06%, CSTM–CSMB −0.62%, CSTM–CMOM 8.65%, CSMB–CMOM −7.97%). In factor regressions on eight long-short strategies (Table 6), the four-factor model (Model 5) drives the sentiment strategy's alpha from −2.5%/week (t = −3.63, Model 4 three-factor) to −0.3% (t = −0.81), with CSTM loading 0.816 (t = 43.44) and R² rising from 0.005 to 0.803; the one-week momentum strategy's alpha remains significant in all models (§4.1). In 5×5 double sorts, the four-factor model reduces the bottom-right (high-sentiment × high-momentum) portfolio alpha from −1.8%/week (t = −1.723) to −0.3% (t = −0.326) with sentiment loading 0.529 (t = 9.131), average R² improving 8% (Table 7); and the (large × sensitive) sentiment-size portfolio alpha from −3.0% (t = −3.777) to −0.8% (t = −1.291) with sentiment loading 0.743 (t = 19.168), average R² improving 18% (Table 8). In Fama-MacBeth regressions on portfolio-rank characteristics (Table 9), |β^∆CryptoSent| slopes are significant in every specification (t > 5.04); the four-factor model (Model 11) adds 0.52% of cross-sectional R̄² over the three-factor (Model 10) — 3.47% vs 2.95% (§4.3). PCA of the eight long-short strategies: four principal components explain 30%, 24%, 11%, 10% of variance (cumulative > 75%); PC3 correlates −57.05% with CSTM, PC1 50.71% with CMOM, PC2 36.46% with CSMB (Table 10; §4.4).

## Test setup

Sample: January 2014 to February/March 2023 (stated both ways in the paper), CoinMarketCap daily snapshots plus Twitter (X), Google Trends, and blockchain explorer data; 417 coins in 2014, peaking at 2,981 in 2021, 2,096 in 2023 (Table 1). Weeks are calendar-fixed: 52 weeks per year, the first 51 of seven days and the 52nd of eight days (§2.2). The risk-free rate is the one-month Treasury bill rate (§2.2). Returns are weekly value-weighted (baseline) or equal-weighted (robustness) excess returns; no transaction costs, financing costs, or capacity figures are given, and no statement that returns are gross appears — the cost treatment is simply absent. No out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported. The one-week momentum strategy's unexplained alpha is attributed to post-2020 market dynamism (§4.1).

The authors conclude that high absolute sentiment sensitivity is a robust negative predictor of subsequent returns, that the pattern is a mispricing phenomenon rather than risk compensation (symmetric positive/negative exposure effects; §3.3), and that the four-factor model "significantly reduces mean absolute pricing errors" and explains the sentiment strategy's alpha (§4.1, Table 6). Notably absent across the paper: Sharpe ratios, turnover, cost-adjusted returns, and t-statistics for factor means (the factors' own premia are not reported).

## Robustness

(§5) Three sets of checks: (i) the sentiment index is rebuilt as CryptoSentDL using a symmetric 8-4-1-4-8 autoencoder (Hinton and Zemel 1993; Gu, Kelly, and Xiu 2021) trained to minimize mean-squared reconstruction error on the eight components (§5.1, Figure 5); quintile sorts on |β^∆CryptoSentDL| reproduce the pattern (Table 11), and the autoencoder-derived sentiment factor reduces the sentiment strategy's three-factor alpha of −2.4% (significant) to insignificance, with CSTM^DL loading 0.833 (t = 44.53) and R² 0.826 in the four-factor model (Table 12). (ii) Equal-weighted quintile portfolios preserve the pattern at −1.1%/week (t = −2.28) (Table 13, §5.2). (iii) The strategy survives excluding stablecoins (footnote 9), and the results are asserted robust to additional liquidity controls and to a $0.10 price filter (footnotes 6, 12). There is no subperiod or delayed-execution analysis.

## Specification search

The paper reports: two index constructions (PCA, autoencoder) × two weighting schemes (value, equal) × two horizons (weekly, 4-week monthly); eleven Fama-MacBeth specifications (Table 9, Models 1–11); five factor-model specifications applied to eight long-short strategies (Table 6); two 5×5 double sorts (sentiment×momentum, sentiment×size); and a ten-group conditional-return analysis on seven characteristics (Figure 4). Results are reported for the selected specifications; no exhaustive grid is described.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§6): structural models linking sentiment-driven demand, limited arbitrage, and price dynamics; interactions between sentiment and market microstructure (liquidity provision, order flow, exchange fragmentation); the role of institutional participation in mitigating or amplifying sentiment-driven mispricing; and extending sentiment-based mispricing measures to NFTs and tokenized real-world assets.

## Separable variants for our pipeline

- |β^∆CryptoSent| quintile sort, value-weighted, weekly and 4-week monthly (Table 3, §3.3) — the core signal.
- Sort on |β^∆CryptoSentDL| from the autoencoder index (Table 11, §5.1) — the same signal, alternative index.
- Equal-weighted version of the sort (Table 13, §5.2).
- CSTM factor (sensitive-minus-neutral on |β^∆CryptoSent|) as a stand-alone long-short factor (Table 5, 6; §4.1).
- Conditional timing: returns of characteristic deciles split by high/low ∆CryptoSent weeks (Figure 4, §4.5) — presented graphically only.
