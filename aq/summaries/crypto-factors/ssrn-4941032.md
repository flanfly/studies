### Claim

The paper constructs CryptoSent, a daily Baker–Wurgler-style sentiment index for the cryptocurrency market, from eight marketwide indicators: the cryptocurrency market index, its 30-day historical volatility, daily trading dollar volume, Google Trends for "cryptocurrency", tweet/post counts for Bitcoin, Ethereum and Dogecoin, active wallet counts on the Bitcoin and Ethereum blockchains, the number of new verified ERC-20 smart contracts (ICO activity), and the dollar value of Bitcoin blockchain transactions (Eq. 1; §3.1, §3.2). Cryptocurrencies are sorted each week on the absolute value of their beta to innovations in CryptoSent, |β^i_∆CryptoSent|, estimated from a regression of weekly excess returns on the market excess return and ∆CryptoSent over the preceding 28 days (Eq. 2; §3.3). High-sensitivity cryptocurrencies earn significantly lower returns in the following week and month: the value-weighted long-short spread (low-sensitivity minus high-sensitivity quintiles) is −2.5% per week (t = −3.75) and −5.1% per month (t = −3.28) (Table 3). The authors interpret the symmetric underperformance of both positively and negatively exposed coins as sentiment-driven mispricing — sentiment-sensitive coins attract sentiment-driven retail demand, become temporarily overvalued, then revert — rather than compensation for risk (§3.3). Adding a sentiment factor (CSTM, sensitive-minus-neutral portfolios) to the Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022) market-size-momentum three-factor model raises cross-sectional explanatory power by approximately 13% (§4.3; abstract) and drives the sentiment strategy's alpha from −2.5% (t = −3.63) to an insignificant −0.3% (t = −0.81) (Table 6, Models 4–5). Sample: over 2,000 coins, January 2014 to February 2023, from CoinMarketCap daily snapshots (§1).

### Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is crypto spot; a short leg (high-sensitivity quintile) is required and is expressible in perpetuals/futures rather than short stock. Long and short legs are reported separately as quintile means (Table 3), so the short-leg contribution is visible: the spread's magnitude comes from Group 5 mean being negative (−1.1% weekly, insignificant) combined with Group 1's +1.5% (t = 2.68). No leverage is stated anywhere; returns are excess returns gross of any transaction cost model, of which none is described (§2.2, Table 3). Replication needs CoinMarketCap daily snapshots, Twitter/X post counts, Bitcoin/Ethereum blockchain explorer data, and Google Trends (monthly-adjusted); the CryptoSent series itself is posted at github.com/ronming1303/CryptoSent (footnote 7). All eight index components are daily and span January 2014 to March 2023 except tweet counts (from April 2014) and ICO counts (from November 2015) (Table 2 note).

### Strategies

**Sentiment sensitivity, |β^i_∆CryptoSent|** — the paper's single strategy. The signal is the absolute value of β^i_∆CryptoSent in

$$r^i_t - r^f_t = \beta_0 + \beta^i_{CMKT}\, CMKT_t + \beta^i_{\Delta CryptoSent}\, \Delta CryptoSent_t + \epsilon^i_t, \quad (2)$$

with weekly excess returns, CMKT the value-weighted market excess return (over the one-month T-bill rate), and ∆CryptoSent the first difference of the index (taken for stationarity, footnote 8) (§3.3). The beta window is the past 28 days; coins are ranked into five quintiles weekly and value-weighted excess returns are computed over the subsequent week (Panel A) or subsequent 4-week month (Panel B). Absolute value is used because the authors find both high positive and high negative exposures predict lower returns (§3.3). Economic rationale is behavioural: |β| measures exposure to sentiment-driven demand, producing temporary overvaluation and later underperformance; the other side of the trade is sentiment-driven retail investors (§3.3, §1). Universe: all CoinMarketCap coins with price, volume and market-cap data, market cap ≥ $100,000 (footnote 6); weekly returns are built from daily closes with a 52-week year whose 52nd week has 8 days (§2.2). Stablecoins are included in the baseline; excluding them leaves Table 3 unchanged (footnote 9). Holding period equals the one-week (or four-week) formation-return horizon; rebalancing weekly; turnover is not reported. Whether formation-week returns overlap the beta-estimation window is not specified beyond "subsequent week". The factor-model delta on top of this: the four-factor model replaces the LTW (2022) three-factor baseline (CMKT, CSMB, CMOM) with CMKT, CSTM, CSMB, CMOM, where CSTM is formed from value-weighted portfolios of the bottom 15% ("neutral"), middle 70%, and top 15% ("sensitive") of |β| (Stambaugh–Yuan percentile convention), CSTM = sensitive − neutral (§4.1). CMOM and CSMB are 2×3 intersection factors, CMOM = ½(Neutral High + Sensitive High) − ½(Neutral Low + Sensitive Low) (Eq. 3), CSMB analogously on size (Eq. 4). Factor correlations run from −7.97% to +8.65% (Table 5).

### Test setup

Sample January 2014 – February 2023 (Introduction; §2.2 and figure captions say "ends in March 2023" — the paper is internally inconsistent); no out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported. Universe grows from 417 coins (2014) to 2,981 (2021), falling to 2,096 (2023) (Table 1). Data: CoinMarketCap daily snapshots (volume-weighted average price across exchanges; listing requires a public API exchange and non-zero 24-hour volume), Google Trends, Twitter/X, blockchain explorers (§2.1). Survivorship and delisting handling are not addressed; membership is reconstructed from daily snapshots but point-in-time status is not discussed. Headline numbers are gross value-weighted excess returns; no transaction cost or financing cost model is described. Benchmark: the LTW (2022) three-factor model for the factor tests; the eight test-asset strategies (size via MCAP and last-day price PRC; momentum via one-, two-, three-week past returns R1,0/R2,0/R3,0; volume via PRCVOL; volatility via STDPRCVOL) are constructed as in LTW (2022) and remain significant over 2014–2023 (Table 4).

Factor-model evidence: under the LTW three-factor model the sentiment strategy's alpha is −2.5% (t = −3.63); in the four-factor model it falls to −0.3% (t = −0.81) with a CSTM loading of 0.816 (t = 43.44) (Table 6, Models 4–5; R² rises from 0.005 to 0.803). The one-week momentum strategy's alpha is the one the four-factor model cannot explain (0.020, t = 1.74) (§4.1). In 5×5 Sentiment-Momentum portfolios the corner portfolio (high momentum, high sentiment) has a three-factor alpha of −1.8% per week (t = −1.723) that becomes −0.3% (t = −0.326) in the four-factor model, with average R² improving 8%; in 5×5 Sentiment-Size portfolios the large-and-sensitive corner alpha moves from −3.0% (t = −3.777) to −0.8% (t = −1.291), with average R² improving 18% (Tables 7–8). Fama-MacBeth regressions on portfolio ranks (Table 9): |β_∆CryptoSent| slopes are significant in every specification (t > 5.04 in multi-factor models); market beta is insignificant once three or more characteristics enter; average R̄² rises from 2.95% (three-factor, Model 10) to 3.47% (four-factor, Model 11), the +0.52 pp behind the paper's "13%" claim; the abstract's 13% is attributed to §4.3. PCA of the eight strategies: the first four principal components explain 30%, 24%, 11% and 10% of variance; PC3 correlates −57.05% with CSTM, PC4 +52.66% (Table 10, §4.4). The authors conclude CSTM is a distinct, priced mispricing factor, weakly correlated with the other three factors, that primarily kills the alphas of sentiment-sensitive, high-momentum, and large-cap portfolios (§4.2). Sharpe ratios, turnover, and capacity are not reported anywhere.

### Robustness

Two robustness sets (§5). (i) The sentiment index is rebuilt as the one-dimensional bottleneck of a symmetric 8-4-1-4-8 autoencoder trained to minimize reconstruction error (following Gu, Kelly, and Xiu 2021); sorts on |β_∆CryptoSentDL| reproduce the pattern (Table 11), and a DL-based sentiment factor takes the sentiment strategy's three-factor alpha from −2.4% (t = −3.33) to an insignificant −0.5% (t = −1.50) (Table 12). (ii) Equal-weighted quintile sorts preserve the monotone pattern with a −1.1% weekly spread (t = −2.28), and a price filter of > $0.10 is applied "to mitigate the influence of extreme return observations" (footnote 12). Additional checks stated in footnotes: results are "robust to additional liquidity controls" and survive excluding stablecoins (footnote 9). Figure 4 reports conditional returns: high (low) sentiment weeks defined by ∆CryptoSent above (below) its median; non-momentum characteristic portfolios show the largest high-minus-low sentiment differential between decile 1 and 10, while momentum portfolios show it in decile 10. No degradation is reported.

### Specification search

Reported variants: five-quintile sorts at weekly and monthly horizons; three-group (15/70/15) factor constructions; 2×3 double-sorted CMOM and CSMB; 5×5 Sentiment-Momentum and 5×5 Sentiment-Size double sorts; eleven Fama-MacBeth specifications; PCA over eight strategies; autoencoder and equal-weight replications. Counts are as stated in Tables 3, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10. Only selected double-sort cells are reported (corner portfolios and average R²), not all 25 per panel.

### Limitations and future work

Authors' own (Conclusion): structural models linking sentiment-driven demand, limited arbitrage, and price dynamics; interaction of sentiment with market microstructure (liquidity provision, order flow, exchange fragmentation); whether institutional participation mitigates or amplifies sentiment-driven mispricing; extending sentiment-based mispricing measures to NFTs and tokenized real-world assets.

### Separable variants for our pipeline

- |β^i_∆CryptoSent| weekly sort, PCA index, value-weighted — the core signal (§3.3, Table 3 Panel A).
- Same sort at the 4-week (monthly) horizon (Table 3 Panel B).
- Equal-weighted version of the same sort (§5.2, Table 13).
- Autoencoder-index version, |β^i_∆CryptoSentDL| (§5.1, Table 11).
- CSTM as a stand-alone factor for hedging or conditioning (§4.1, Table 6).
- Sentiment-conditioned returns: characteristic decile portfolios split by high/low ∆CryptoSent weeks (Figure 4).
- The four LTW (2022) test-asset signals replicated here (MCAP, PRC, R1,0/R2,0/R3,0, PRCVOL, STDPRCVOL) (Table 4) — baselines, not new signals.
