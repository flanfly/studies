Category: **empirical** — the paper documents the information architecture of a benchmark-anchored commodity-linked perpetual. It does report return-predictive regressions (funding → returns; funding-conditioned order flow), but the authors state in the abstract, §6.5, and §8 that no effect is exploitable net of trading costs and that "the contribution is to market structure, not to alpha." No strategy is proposed or backtested.

### Claim

In the Binance XAUUSDT gold perpetual — a USDT-settled perpetual futures contract referencing one troy ounce of gold, launched 5 January 2026, operated by Nest Exchange Limited under ADGM regulation (§2.1) — minute-level trade-based order-flow imbalance has no unconditional predictive power for 5-, 15-, and 30-minute forward returns: the NOFI coefficient is insignificant in-sample (t = 0.42, 0.19, −0.18 at the three horizons, Table 3), the out-of-sample predictive R-squared is negative, and the result survives multiple-testing corrections across fifteen session-by-horizon cells (§6.1, Table 6). The active channel is the funding mechanism: the funding rate positively predicts returns at all three horizons (t = 2.22, 2.95, 3.71, Table 4), and the funding-by-order-flow interaction is negative and significant at all three (t = −2.64, −2.28, −2.26, Table 4). The authors interpret the pair as a positioning-and-reversal mechanism — flow conveys the state of leveraged positioning, which funding prices — rather than classical informed trading about gold value, which is discovered off-exchange (§8). Session-level effects around the European window are directionally stable but fragile; event-study and placebo evidence do not isolate the London fix as their source (§7). The sample is 203,037 one-minute observations from contract launch (5 January 2026) through 21 May 2026, covering essentially the instrument's entire trading history (§4.1).

### Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is a single crypto perpetual futures contract; long and short are both directly expressible in perps, so no short-stock or short-option leg problem arises. The closest thing to a strategy in the paper — the NOFI quintile sort of §6.5 — is reported only as a long-short spread; the extreme quintiles are not reported separately. Leverage is a feature of the contract but no leverage is applied in the analysis; effects are fractions of a basis point per minute, far below the assumed ~14 bps round-trip cost (4.5 bps taker fee, 1.0 bps half-spread, 1.5 bps slippage, at entry and exit, §6.5). Data replication needs tick-level trades from the Binance Futures API (millisecond timestamps, price, quantity, aggressor side taken from the feed — no Lee-Ready algorithm, §4.2), the four-hourly funding rate merged by backward as-of join, and one-minute bar construction; the contract launched 5 January 2026, so the entire history is ~4.5 months. No survivorship or delisting considerations apply to a single live contract; none are discussed.

### Strategies

The paper proposes no tradable strategy. What follows are the four predictive specifications the paper estimates as evidence channels; the equations are reproduced verbatim from §4.2 and §5.

**The unconditional null: NOFI/OFI.** Signals defined in §4.2: trade-based order-flow imbalance $\text{OFI}(t) = \text{BuyVolume}(t) - \text{SellVolume}(t)$, and its normalisation $\text{NOFI}(t) = \text{OFI}(t) / (\text{BuyVolume}(t) + \text{SellVolume}(t))$, bounded in $[-1, 1]$. Forward returns are $\text{ret\_fwd\_5m} = \log(\text{Close}(t+5)/\text{Close}(t))$ and analogously for 15 and 30 minutes, scaled to basis points. Baseline regression (§5): $\text{ret\_fwd\_h}(t) = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{NOFI}(t) + \gamma \cdot \text{OFI}(t) + \delta \cdot \text{Volume}(t) + \Sigma \theta(j) \cdot \text{Session}(j, t) + \varphi_1 \cdot \text{ret\_1m}(t) + \varphi_2 \cdot \text{ret\_5m}(t) + \varepsilon(t)$, OLS with Newey-West HAC standard errors (lags = 5); inference unchanged under Andrews (1991) automatic bandwidth. NOFI coefficients are 0.023 (t = 0.42), 0.016 (t = 0.19), −0.021 (t = −0.18) at 5/15/30m; the only marginally non-zero term is contemporaneous OFI at 5m (−0.0015, t = −1.80), a "very weak and non-robust short-horizon reversal" (Table 3). The authors frame the null as an architecture prediction: fundamental value is discovered off-exchange, so on-exchange flow should carry limited standalone information (§6.1). They offer no statement about who is on the other side of the trade.

**The funding channel.** §5 and Table 4: $\text{ret\_fwd\_h}(t) = \alpha + \beta_1 \cdot \text{NOFI}(t) + \beta_2 \cdot \text{Funding}(t) + \beta_3[\text{NOFI}(t) \times \text{Funding}(t)] + \text{controls} + \varepsilon(t)$. Funding is the most recently settled four-hourly rate, merged by backward as-of join so it is predictive by construction. Funding coefficients: 278.7 (t = 2.22), 754.2 (t = 2.95), 1404.7 (t = 3.71) bps at 5/15/30m; a one-standard-deviation funding increase (sample mean 0.7 bps, sd 5.0 bps, capped at ±2% per interval but rarely near the bound) raises forward return by ~0.14, 0.38, 0.71 bps (§6.2). Interaction β3: −263.2 (t = −2.64), −410.3 (t = −2.28), −625.7 (t = −2.26); a one-sd funding rise shifts the NOFI slope by about −0.13, −0.21, −0.31 bps, enough to reverse the small positive slope prevailing at low funding (§6.3). Economic rationale is a positioning-and-reversal mechanism: high funding means crowded longs paying shorts, so an extra minute of buying pressure is over-extended positioning followed by reversal; when funding is negative the same buying pressure is followed by continuation (§6.3, §8). The paper frames this as behaviour of leveraged positioning, not as a risk premium.

**Funding-sign split.** Splitting by the sign of funding (Table 5): with Funding > 0 (longs crowded, N = 83,496), NOFI β = −0.082 (t = −0.94), −0.176 (−1.44), −0.276 (−1.60) at 5/15/30m; with Funding < 0 (N = 26,423), β = +0.178 (1.52), +0.265 (1.29), +0.153 (0.53). Coefficients are individually imprecise but signs are uniform across horizons; a funding-quintile analysis shows the same gradient (§6.3).

**Session stratification.** NOFI re-estimated within four UTC-hour sessions (Asia 00–08, Europe 08–14, US 14–22, Overnight 22–24, Table 1). The only pre-correction findings: Europe 15m β = 0.290 (raw p = 0.029) and Overnight 30m β = −0.653 (t = −1.69). No cell survives multiple-testing correction (Europe 15m p rises to 0.435 under Benjamini-Hochberg; Holm and Bonferroni agree, Table 6). The overnight coefficient may be partly mechanical given the fixed off-hours index and smoothed mark price (§2.3, §6.4).

**NOFI quintile sort (economic significance).** Minutes sorted into NOFI quintiles; the Q5−Q1 forward-return spread is −0.198 bps (t = −4.44), −0.137 (−1.75), −0.146 (−1.29) at 5/15/30m gross, and roughly −14 bps net at every horizon after the 14 bps round-trip cost — "No" tradable at all horizons (Table 7). Execution timing, weighting, and turnover are not specified because no strategy is defined; the spread is the paper's only portfolio-like object.

### Test setup

Sample period is 5 January 2026 to 21 May 2026, the full trading history from inception; the API query began 1 January 2026 and bars with missing or zero volume were removed, leaving 203,037 one-minute observations (§4.1). One instrument. Data sources by name: Binance Futures API (tick trades), Binance funding rates, and the public LBMA feed for fix times (Data Availability Statement). Estimation is OLS with Newey-West HAC (lags = 5) and Andrews automatic-bandwidth robustness; overlapping-return serial correlation (Hansen and Hodrick, 1980) is addressed by HAC and by non-overlapping re-estimation (§5). Headline numbers are gross; the only cost adjustment is the 14 bps round-trip in §6.5. No benchmark is used. Out-of-sample evidence is the negative predictive R-squared and rolling-window significance in ~11% of windows (§6.1, Appendix Figure A5); the value of the out-of-sample R-squared is not reported.

The authors conclude that the unconditional order-flow null is the paper's first result, the funding main effect and interaction are its central result, and the session effects are suggestive only (§6, §7). No Sharpe ratios, no total returns, and no turnover figures appear anywhere; the paper's own economic-significance test concludes "not tradable" (§6.5).

### Robustness

Appendix Table A1 holds the data fixed across ~11 perturbations: leave-out January and May, a 21-week jackknife (funding effect positive in 100% of leave-one-out samples; European and overnight effects 100% sign-consistent), shifted European windows (07–13 and 09–15 UTC, Europe 15m t falling to ~1.1), dropping the top 1% of one-minute returns and the top-5-volume days, NOFI z-score and rolling-scaled normalisations (both kill the European effect), and non-overlapping sampling (Europe 15m changes sign). The funding main effect survives every perturbation; the interaction stays negative under all alternative normalisations. Appendix Table A2 lags funding by one and two further 4-hour settlements — interaction stays negative at all horizons (e.g., −453.5, t = −1.63 at 30m under one lag; −294.0, t = −1.67 at 15m under two) but significance attenuates; under non-overlapping sampling the 5m interaction is −379.2 (t = −1.96). Fix-window evidence: an event study around the LBMA fixes shows the 15m NOFI coefficient significant in only one of four PM-fix windows, where it is negative (−0.908, t = −1.77) — the opposite sign to the European-session coefficient — and in no AM-fix window; a placebo with randomly placed pseudo-fix windows reproduces the pre-fix coefficient in roughly half of draws (§7, Appendix A1). A randomised-NOFI placebo places the European 15m coefficient at the 7.4% tail of its permutation distribution.

### Specification search

The paper reports results for essentially all specifications it examines: three horizons × four sessions = fifteen session-by-horizon cells (Table 6, with multiple-testing adjustment), three Newey-West lag lengths, two alternative NOFI normalisations, two alternative session boundaries, two additional funding lags, one non-overlapping sampling scheme, 21 weekly jackknife samples, and five funding quintiles plus a two-way sign split. Appendix A3 additionally reports variable distributions and correlations (NOFI and OFI have |r| ≤ 0.02 against all forward returns). All reported results are shown in the body or appendix tables.

### Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§9): the sample covers a single venue, a single contract, and the instrument's first months, so estimates are early-market evidence; the robustness battery mitigates but cannot eliminate sample-period concerns. They do not control for external gold-market variables — spot gold, COMEX gold futures, the dollar index, and scheduled macro announcements — and state that merging these series is the natural next step to separate benchmark-driven from positioning-driven effects. They identify the companion silver perpetual (XAGUSDT) as an immediate cross-asset extension.

### Separable variants for our pipeline

The paper proposes no strategy, so this list names the predictive variables and conditioning constructs, each with its defining location:

- Trade-based OFI, $\text{BuyVolume} - \text{SellVolume}$ (§4.2).
- NOFI, OFI normalised by total volume, bounded [−1, 1] (§4.2).
- NOFI z-score and rolling-volume-scaled normalisations (Appendix Table A1).
- Most-recently-settled 4-hourly funding rate, backward as-of join (§4.2, §6.2), and funding lagged one or two settlements (Appendix Table A2).
- NOFI × funding interaction (§5, Table 4) and its sign-split / quintile versions (Table 5, §6.3).
- Session-stratified NOFI by UTC hour, including alternative boundaries 07–13 and 09–15 (Table 1, Table 6, Table A1).
- NOFI quintile Q5−Q1 spread as the paper's sole portfolio object (Table 7).
