Category: **single-strategy** — one framework (NB-AAI) developed and tested on BTC/USD and ETH/USD; the five baselines are component/ablation variants of the same pipeline rather than independent strategies.

### Claim

The paper proposes the Naïve Bayes Agentic AI (NB-AAI) framework: a hybrid of the Bernoulli Naïve Bayes classifier formalised by Kakushadze and Serur (2018, Eqs. 541–546), a FinBERT LLM validation layer gated by a posterior-confidence threshold, and a six-agent orchestration pipeline (data collection, NB classification, LLM validation, signal fusion, risk management, orchestration) implemented as a LangGraph state machine. Tested on BTC/USD and ETH/USD spot on a 36-month out-of-sample window (Jan 2022–Dec 2024, four market regimes), the framework achieves an annualised BTC Sharpe ratio of 1.47, annualised return +52.6% net of transaction costs, and maximum drawdown −22.4%, versus 0.31, +18.4%, and −77.1% for passive buy-and-hold (Table 7). Calmar ratio is 2.35 vs. 0.24 for buy-and-hold (Table 7). All improvements over the five baselines are reported significant at the 1% level by the Diebold-Mariano test (Table 10; DM statistics 3.41–6.83). All three hypotheses H1–H3 are confirmed, with outperformance over buy-and-hold in all four regimes (Table 8). An ablation study reports superadditivity: removing Agents 3+5 together drops Sharpe to 0.51, below the NB-only (0.74) and FinBERT-only (0.89) baselines (Table 9). The framing is behavioural: sentiment is most informative in bear markets, consistent with the asymmetric-information/noise-trader literature (De Long et al., 1990; Garcia & Schweitzer, 2015) (§5.6).

### Fit with our constraints

Instruments are spot BTC/USD and ETH/USD (Binance data). The signal is directional and three-state (long/flat/short), so a short leg exists and would be expressed naturally in crypto futures or perpetuals; the paper trades spot and never discusses futures or perps. Long and short legs are never reported separately — every figure is the net of the long/flat/short cycle, and the short-leg contribution cannot be isolated from the reported tables. Position sizing is q(t) = Signal(t)·min(1, σ_target/σ(t)) with σ_target = 0.80, i.e. at most 1× capital (Eq. 19); no leverage beyond ±1× is used or described. Returns are reported net of costs: 0.10% taker fee + 0.05% slippage per side (0.30% round-trip), risk-free rate the 3-month US T-Bill (annualised average 3.2% over the test period) (§4.1). Replication would need Twitter API v2 Academic Access (~2.1M documents), Reddit PRAW (~890K documents), CryptoPanic/CoinDesk RSS (~340K items), Binance 4-hour OHLCV (10,958 bars per asset), and FRED T-bill data (§4.1). Trade count is 298 on BTC over 36 months (Table 7), i.e. roughly eight trades per month; turnover in percent terms is not reported.

### Strategies

The paper's unit is the NB-AAI pipeline; the baselines B1–B5 are single components or subsets of it. Everything below is BTC/USD unless noted.

**NB-AAI (full six-agent pipeline).** Documents T_i are labelled y_i ∈ {+1,−1} from 4-hour forward returns: y_i = sign(r_{t+4h} − δ)·1{|r_{t+4h}| > δ} with δ = 0.005 (Eq. 22). A vocabulary V of M keywords is built by TF-IDF; each document is a Bernoulli vector X_i ∈ {0,1}^M (Eq. 5). The classifier is Bernoulli Naïve Bayes with Laplace smoothing P̂(w_a|C_α) = (count(w_a, C_α) + α)/(N_α + α·|V|), α ∈ [0.01, 2.0] optimised by cross-validation (Eq. 12); classification in log-space (Eq. 11). The posterior-confidence gate is κ(X_i) = max_a P(C_α|X_i) − second-max_a P(C_α|X_i) (Eq. 13); documents with κ < τ are routed to FinBERT (ProsusAI/finbert), which returns a three-class softmax F(T_i) = (p₊, p₋, p₀), resolving the final class per Eq. (15). The optimal threshold is τ* = 0.28, routing 21.3% of documents to FinBERT (Table 6 note). Agent 4 fuses resolved assignments with source-reliability weights ω_i ∈ (0,1]: S(t) = [Σ_i ω_i δ(C*(T_i), C₊) − Σ_i ω_i δ(C*(T_i), C₋)] / Σ_i ω_i (Eq. 16), and thresholds asymmetrically: Signal(t) = +1 (Buy) if S(t) ≥ θ₊, −1 (Sell) if S(t) ≤ θ₋, 0 (Hold) otherwise (Eq. 17). The optimal values of θ₊ and θ₋ are not reported. Agent 5 computes realised volatility over the preceding "24-hour window (96 bars)" as σ(t) = sqrt(252·std{log(P_s/P_{s−1}) : t−H ≤ s ≤ t}) (Eq. 18), applies volatility-scaled sizing (Eq. 19), a −20% drawdown circuit-breaker with 48-hour cooling period, a 50% single-asset exposure cap, and a −5% stop-loss per position (§3.5). Agent 6 triggers vocabulary retraining when rolling accuracy drops below ε = 0.60 and re-calibrates walk-forward at 6-month intervals during the test period (§4.4). The hyperparameter vector θ = (α, τ, θ₊, θ₋, σ_target, DD_max) is chosen to maximise validation Sharpe: θ* = argmax_θ [μ_V(θ)/σ_V(θ)]·sqrt(2) (Eq. 20), on the 2021 validation window. Preprocessing is six-step: URL/mention removal, emoji-to-text normalisation, case folding + TweetTokenizer, stop-word removal (negation tokens retained), Porter stemming, near-duplicate deduplication at cosine similarity ≥ 0.95 (§4.2). Sub-lexicons are assigned by log-likelihood ratio LLR(w_a) = log[P(w_a|C₊)/P(w_a|C₋)] (Eq. 21). The mechanism is framed behaviourally (noise-trader-driven, sentiment-propelled price dynamics; §5.6). The other side of the trade is never named; the paper's source-weighting design is motivated as "a principled defence against coordinated sentiment manipulation" (§5.6), implying manipulators among social-media participants. The universe is exactly two assets; the only screens are on documents, not instruments (accounts ≥ 500 followers, posts/comments ≥ 10 upvotes, lang:en, excluding retweets; §4.1). Execution timing: signals are computed per 4-hour bar and labelled on t+4h returns; no separate signal-date-to-trade-date gap is described, and no point-in-time reconstruction issues are discussed.

**B3: Naïve Bayes only.** Agent 3 disabled; NB classification for all documents. Directional accuracy 58.3%, macro F1 0.541 (Table 6); SR 0.74, +29.7% p.a., max DD −41.3%, Calmar 0.72, Sortino 1.03, 387 trades (Table 7).

**B4: FinBERT only.** All documents through FinBERT; no NB, no risk management or orchestration. DA 61.7%, macro F1 0.588 (Table 6); SR 0.89, +34.2%, max DD −38.6%, Calmar 0.89, Sortino 1.21, 312 trades (Table 7).

**B5: NB + Risk (no orchestration).** Agents 1, 2, 4, 5 active; agents 3 and 6 disabled (Table 5). DA 60.1%, macro F1 0.567 (Table 6); SR 0.83, +31.8%, max DD −35.2%, Calmar 0.90, Sortino 1.18, 341 trades (Table 7).

**B1/B2: buy-and-hold BTC/ETH.** BTC: SR 0.31, +18.4%, max DD −77.1%, Calmar 0.24, Sortino 0.38, 1 trade (Table 7). ETH buy-and-hold performance is not tabulated.

What the comparison establishes: the full pipeline beats every component and every subset on every reported metric; the LLM-validation gain is concentrated in the bearish class (F1 bearish 0.619 vs. 0.512 for NB-only), and risk management halves drawdown (Table 9).

### Test setup

Sample is January 2020 – December 2024 for both assets. Split: training Jan–Dec 2020 (vocabulary and P(w_a|C_α)); validation Jan–Dec 2021 (hyperparameter selection); test Jan 2022–Dec 2024 — the out-of-sample period, covering four regimes: Crypto Winter (2022, BTC −65.0%), Recovery I (Jan–Jun 2023, +82.5%), Recovery II (Jul–Dec 2023, +61.2%), Bull Run (2024, +123.8%) (Tables 3–4). No post-publication period is reported (paper submitted February 2026). Data sources by name: Twitter API v2 Academic Access, Reddit PRAW, CryptoPanic API, CoinDesk RSS, Binance public REST API (4-hour bars, 10,958 bars per asset), FRED (§4.1). Survivorship and delisting handling are not addressed (crypto spot; not discussed). Transaction costs as in §4.1 above; headline numbers are net of them. Sharpe is annualised as SR = (μ_p − r_f)/σ_p · sqrt(365×6) (Eq. 23), i.e. 6 four-hour bars per day. Statistical tests are two-sided Diebold-Mariano with sign-error loss differentials, stated at the 5% level in §4.5 (Eq. 24) but reported at p < 0.001 / p = 0.001 (Table 10). Benchmarks are the five baselines in Table 5.

Commonly-reported metrics absent: no standard errors or t-stats on Sharpe ratios, no monthly return series, no turnover in percent, no capacity or cost-sensitivity figures, no per-leg (long vs. short) performance.

The authors conclude that the confidence-gating architecture preserves NB's tractability while delegating its failure modes to FinBERT, that the results extend Kakushadze and Serur (2018) from a static single model to a dynamic multi-agent system, and that the regime results empirically support the asymmetric-information-transmission hypothesis (De Long et al., 1990) — sentiment signals are most valuable in bear markets (§5.6). They also report that gating reduces GPU inference cost ~79% relative to pure FinBERT while retaining 76% of the classification improvement, and that LLM validation reduces the false-signal rate by 31.2% (Hypothesis Verdict, §5.5).

### Robustness

There is no standalone robustness section. The regime-stratified analysis (Table 8) serves as the out-of-sample robustness evidence (H3 confirmed in all four regimes, including the −65% 2022 bear market). The ablation study (Table 9) tests the architecture's sensitivity to each component. No alternative hyperparameter values, delayed-execution tests, alternative universes, other assets, or cost-level sensitivities are reported; walk-forward re-calibration at 6-month intervals during the test period is described (§4.4) but no results comparing calibrated vs. uncalibrated variants are given.

### Specification search

The hyperparameter vector (α, τ, θ₊, θ₋, σ_target, DD_max) is selected by grid search on the 2021 validation window (§4.3, Eq. 20; future-work item F4 says to replace "grid-search hyperparameter optimisation" with RL), but only the optimal configuration is reported — τ* = 0.28 and the α range [0.01, 2.0]; the optima of θ₊, θ₋, σ_target, and DD_max are not given. The ablation study reports 7 configurations plus the full pipeline (Table 9), and classification performance is reported for 4 model configurations (Table 6). No intermediate grid points are reported.

### Limitations and future work

The authors' own limitations (§5.7): results derive from backtested simulation rather than live trading and are subject to optimism bias; the study covers only BTC and ETH, leaving generalisability to smaller assets untested; FinBERT was not fine-tuned on cryptocurrency-specific language; and regulatory risk, exchange downtime, and liquidity crises are not modelled. Future directions (Section 6): F1 fine-tune a crypto-specific FinBERT; F2 extend to Layer-1/DeFi assets with an on-chain wallet-flow data agent; F3 live paper-trading deployment of at least 6 months; F4 RL-based (PPO or SAC) dynamic threshold optimisation; F5 cross-lingual expansion (XLM-RoBERTa for Chinese, Korean, Spanish); F6 a SHAP-based post-hoc explanation layer for regulatory compliance.

### Separable variants for our pipeline

- Confidence-gated NB→FinBERT routing with threshold τ (Eqs. 13–15), τ* = 0.28 — the gating mechanism is independently testable as a latency/accuracy trade-off.
- Source-weighted sentiment fusion S(t) with asymmetric thresholds θ₊/θ₋ (Eqs. 16–17) — the core tradable signal; optimal θ values not given in the paper.
- Volatility-scaled position sizing with σ_target = 0.80, −20% drawdown circuit-breaker, 50% exposure cap, −5% stop-loss (Eqs. 18–19) — the risk overlay, testable on any signal.
- Walk-forward vocabulary retraining trigger at rolling accuracy ε = 0.60 (§3.6, Agent 6) — the vocabulary-adaptation mechanism.
- B3 (NB-only) and B4 (FinBERT-only) — the two component classifiers, as defined in Table 5.
