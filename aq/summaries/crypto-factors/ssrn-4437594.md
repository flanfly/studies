**Category: comparison.** The paper re-examines 49 cryptocurrency cross-sectional anomalies and builds a new three-factor model (DS3) via Iterative Double Selection Lasso, then compares its anomaly-explaining power against three benchmarks: CAPM, CPT3 (Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022), and IPCA3 (Bianchi and Babiak 2023). The unit of interest is the factor model comparison; the anomaly sorts are the raw material.

## Claim

The paper tests 49 cryptocurrency market anomalies constructed from trading data, in single-sort quintile portfolios, over three periods: in-sample January 2014–July 2020 (the Liu et al. 2022 window), out-of-sample August 2020–December 2023, and the full sample January 2014–December 2023 (§3.1). Short-horizon momentum persists in all three periods — two-week momentum earns 4.0% (t=3.779) in-sample, 3.8% (t=4.943) out-of-sample, 3.9% (t=5.322) per week full-sample (Table 3) — while the size effect (MACP, −2.5% per week, t=−3.711, in-sample) disappears out-of-sample (−0.1%, t=−0.296) (Table 3). Using Iterative Double Selection Lasso (§2.3), the authors select a three-factor model, DS3 = {MKT, MOM2, RMOM} (§3.3), built on in-sample data only. In the out-of-sample period DS3 leaves a mean absolute anomaly alpha of 0.592% per week (t=1.148, GRS p=0.027), versus CPT3's 1.146% (t=2.075, GRS p=0.000) and CAPM's 1.950% (t=3.056) (Table 6, Panel B); IPCA3 reduces anomaly scale but does not eliminate significance (in-sample mean absolute alpha 0.120% but mean absolute t=3.726, 9 unexplained alphas) (Table 6, Panel A). The authors conclude DS3 explains anomalies at least as well as CPT3 in-sample and better out-of-sample and full-sample, and that it spans both CPT3 and IPCA3 factors (§3.4.4).

## Fit with our constraints

Instruments are spot cryptocurrencies only — CoinMarketCap value-weighted prices across exchanges, no futures or perps appear anywhere. All evidence is long–short quintile spreads (highest minus lowest group), so every significant signal requires a short leg; the paper reports only spread returns, never the long and short legs separately (Table 3 caption, §3.1). Momentum (long winners / short losers) and the size effect (long small / short large, negative MACP spread) can in principle be expressed in perp spreads rather than short spot, but the paper itself contains no futures/perp construction. Leverage is never discussed. Returns are gross; the paper describes no transaction costs, financing, or borrow model anywhere. Replication would need CoinMarketCap daily price, market cap, and dollar volume for the full coin universe with all `coin_id`s (survivorship handled by iterating all ids), plus CryptoCompare for the appendix robustness run; weekly rebalancing with a one-week signal-to-return gap. Weekly spread magnitudes of 3–4% are reported without any cost adjustment, and turnover is not quantified.

## Strategies

Four factor models are compared as explanatory devices for the 49 anomalies; the tradable content is inside the anomalies themselves, so those are extracted first.

### Anomaly evidence: 49 single-sort characteristics (raw material)

At the end of each week, cryptocurrencies are sorted into five groups by each of 49 characteristics and value-weighted portfolio returns are computed for the following week; the long-short portfolio is highest minus lowest group, with Newey-West adjusted t-values (§3.1). The characteristics, all built from trading data only (no fundamentals), are defined in Table 2 across seven categories: **Size** — MACP (log last-day market cap in formation week), PRC (log last-day price), MAXPRC (max price in formation week), AGE (days listed on CoinMarketCap); **Mom** — past returns r1,0, r2,0, r3,0, r4,0, r8,0 (Table 2 mislabels it r6,0), r3,1, r4,1, r16,0, r50,0, r100,0, CHMOM, HIGH (last-day close / highest close over past 365 days), RMOM (four-week cumulative residuals of regressing coin returns on the CAPM over the prior 4 weeks, weeks t−4 to t); **Volume** — PRCVOL (log average dollar volume in formation week), VOLSCALED (average dollar volume scaled by market cap); **Vol** — BETA, BETA2, IDIOVOL, RETVOL, MAX (max daily return in formation week), STDPRCVOL (sd of log daily dollar volume), STDVOLSCALED, AMIHUD (average |daily return| / dollar volume), IDIOSKEW, RETSKEW, COSKEW, DELAY, DMBETA, DOWNBETA, SVAR (sd of below-average daily returns over past 365 days); **Left-tail risk** — VAR1/ES1/VAR5/ES5 (1% and 5% quantiles of daily returns over past 365 days × −1 and averages below them × −1), VAR10/ES10 (past 730 days per Table 2), MIN (min daily return in formation week × −1), DVAR1/DES1/DVAR5/DES5/DVAR10/DES10 (changes in the corresponding VaR/ES); **Salience** — STR, STV. Value-at-risk and expected shortfall are estimated non-parametrically (Table 2 note).

Headline results (Table 3): all five short-horizon momentum measures are significant in-sample (r1,0 3.6% t=2.635; r2,0 4.0% t=3.779; r3,0 4.0% t=3.831; r4,0 3.1% t=3.065; r3,1 2.6% t=2.440) and persist in the other two periods; RMOM is 2.1% (t=2.422) in-sample, 1.2% (t=2.378) out-of-sample, 1.8% (t=3.046) full-sample. Size anomalies are significant in-sample (MACP −2.5% t=−3.711; PRC −2.4% t=−2.088; MAXPRC −3.1% t=−2.353; PRCVOL −2.4% t=−2.495; STDPRCVOL −2.7% t=−3.025) and insignificant out-of-sample. Left-tail risk changes sign: out-of-sample MIN is −2.5% (t=−3.990), DVAR1 1.3% (t=2.265), DES1 1.3% (t=2.281) — the authors note this contradicts Zhang et al. (2021), who found VaR positively predicts returns over 2016–2020, attributing the difference to calculation method and interval (§3.1). In the long-term test (Table 4, returns at T+2…T+6), size, price, volume and Amihud characteristics retain predictive power; momentum decays to insignificance by T+3; left-tail risk characteristics (VAR1, VAR5, ES5, VAR10) become significant only at T+5–T+6, which the authors attribute to lagged investor reaction (§3.2).

### DS3 (the paper's model)

Selected by the Iterative Double Selection Lasso procedure of §2.3 (adapted by Chen et al. 2023 from Feng et al. 2020). The algorithm: (Step 1a) Lasso of the cross-sectional average return $\bar{r}_t$ on existing factors, $\bar{r}_t = \ln\gamma_0 + C_h\lambda_h$, keep non-zero-coefficient factors as set $I_1$; (Step 1b) Lasso of each candidate anomaly $g_t$ on existing factors, $g_t = \alpha + \beta_h h_t + \epsilon$, keep set $I_2$; (Step 2) OLS of $\bar{r}_t = \ln\gamma_0 + C_{\{I_3\}}\lambda_{\{I_3\}} + C_g\lambda_g$ on the union $I_3 = I_1 \cup I_2$, collecting the risk premium and t-statistic of $g$. Iterating from the market factor, each round adds the anomaly with the maximum t-statistic until no detected anomaly risk premium is statistically significant; a stepwise regression then removes factors with t < 1.64 (§2.3).

The selected model is DS3 = **MKT** (value-weighted return of all sample cryptocurrencies), **MOM2** (two-week momentum factor, i.e., the r2,0 spread), and **RMOM** (residual momentum). Factor returns use a 30%-40%-30% three-group sort: sort into top 30% / middle 40% / bottom 30%, value-weighted return spread between top and bottom (§3.3). RMOM is double-sorted to orthogonalize: coins first split into winners and losers by past three-week returns, then within each group into 30/40/30 by the RMOM characteristic, with

$$RMOM = \frac{1}{2}(\text{winner high} + \text{loser high}) - \frac{1}{2}(\text{winner low} + \text{loser low})$$

Following Liu et al. (2022)'s orthogonalization convention (only CMOM orthogonalized in CPT3, not CSMB), only RMOM is orthogonalized, not MOM2 (§3.3, footnote 2). The authors report the raw RMOM–MOM2 correlation as 0.31 pre-orthogonalization, falling to 0.15 post-orthogonalization (§3.3); Table 5's correlation matrix as extracted is internally inconsistent with this text (see Extraction gaps). Factor selection uses only in-sample data (January 2014–July 2020); the out-of-sample period is untouched. Weekly factor returns (Table 5): MKT 1.2% (t=2.652), MOM2 3.1% (t=5.541), raw RMOM 1.4% (t=3.720), orthogonalized RMOM 1.8% (t=3.833).

### CPT3 (baseline; Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022)

The published three-factor model {CMKT, CSMB, CMOM} — market, size (CSMB) and momentum (CMOM) factors — which the paper takes as given and reconstructs on its data. It is the reference the DS3 construction is judged against: in-sample it leaves mean absolute alpha 0.972% (t=1.291, GRS p=0.131), but out-of-sample it degrades to 1.146% (t=2.075, GRS p=0.000) (Table 6, Panels A–B). Section 4.1 diagnoses the degradation: after CPT3 adjustment, MACP remains significantly priced in-sample (1.5% per week, t=3.117) and full-sample (1.0%, t=2.971) — the 30-40-30 CSMB grouping does not fully capture the market-cap anomaly — while CMOM fails to explain two-week momentum in-sample (alpha 1.6%, t=1.821 at 10%); CPT3 also *elevates* previously insignificant downside- and left-tail-risk anomalies to significance (absolute t-values from 0.837 to 3.103 in-sample; e.g., ES1 −2.1%, t=−3.103; full-sample ES5 −1.5%, t=−3.254) (Table 8, §4.1).

### IPCA3 (Bianchi and Babiak 2023)

A three-factor latent model estimated by Instrumented Principal Component Analysis. Unlike CPT3 and DS3, IPCA3 is estimated on full-sample data (§3.4). Its distinguishing behavior: it shrinks anomaly alpha magnitudes most (in-sample mean absolute alpha 0.120%, full-sample 0.118%) but leaves the largest mean absolute t-statistics (3.726 in-sample, 3.529 full-sample) and fails to eliminate 9 anomalies in-sample and 9 full-sample (Table 6, Panels A and C).

### What the comparison establishes

Across all three panels of Table 6, DS3 is the only model whose GRS test fails to reject joint-zero alphas at the 5% level out-of-sample (p=0.027) and full-sample (p=0.061), with the smallest out-of-sample mean absolute alpha (0.592%) among the four models. In the mutual-exclamation test (Table 7, §3.4.4), DS3 explains CPT3's CSMB and CMOM (alphas 71.2 bp, t=1.166 and 7.5 bp, t=0.207; joint GRS p=0.897) and two of three IPCA3 factors (alphas 4.697%, t=0.103; 1.742, t=0.688; 3.564, t=1.989; GRS F=1.841, p=0.139), while CPT3 leaves MOM2 and RMOM alphas of 147.6 bp (t=3.482) and 89.4 bp (t=2.579), GRS p=0.000, and IPCA3 leaves alphas of 255 bp (t=2.553) and 95 bp (t=2.471), GRS p=0.001. The paper's conclusion: DS3 contains pricing information that CPT3 and IPCA3 do not, and it is constructed from anomalies with an explicit statistical selection rule rather than by intuition.

## Test setup

Weekly frequency throughout; weeks defined as 51 seven-day weeks per year starting from the first natural day of the year, the last week having 8–9 days (§2.1). Sample: January 1, 2014–December 31, 2023; 3,969 currencies after filters (Table 1's annual coin counts sum to 4,193 across year-rows, an inconsistency). Screens: drop observations with zero market value, transaction value, or missing market cap / volume / close price; drop coins with market cap under $1 million at end of each week; one unnamed currency that briefly exceeded Bitcoin's market cap in October 2021 is removed for abnormal returns (§2.1). Data: CoinMarketCap value-weighted prices (exchanges with wash-trading-prone zero-fee or incentive regimes and flagged outliers excluded per CMC policy); survivorship addressed by iterating all `coin_id`s. The Appendix replicates Table 6 on CryptoCompare data (in-sample then starts March 2018). All portfolio returns are value-weighted; t-statistics use Newey-West standard errors. Returns are gross — no transaction costs, financing, or borrow costs are mentioned anywhere. The benchmark of the anomaly tests is a zero-cost long-short spread (no explicit benchmark portfolio). The out-of-sample period (August 2020–December 2023) is genuinely held out from DS3 factor selection, though the same anomalies used for selection are the objects being explained.

## Robustness

Four robustness exercises, all supportive: (1) §5.1 — univariate value-weighted Fama-MacBeth regressions with characteristics rank-transformed 1–5 within quintiles, WLS weighting residual sums of squares by market cap to the 0.5 power; the regression is $ExR_{i,t+1} = b_{0,t} + b_{1,t}X_{i,t} + \epsilon_{i,t+1}$ where excess return subtracts the weekly one-month T-bill rate; momentum and size results broadly align with the sorts, with the same out-of-sample loss of significance for size (Table 9). (2) §5.2 — single sorts restricted to the top-100 coins by market cap (all eligible coins if fewer than 100): momentum and RMOM survive (RMOM 2.4%/1.7%/2.2% per week across the three periods; Table 10), and DS3 remains dominant in explaining top-100 anomalies (out-of-sample mean absolute alpha 0.336%, t=0.639, GRS p=0.580, zero unexplained; Table 11). (3) §5.3 — replacing CPT3's CSMB with the MACP anomaly and CMOM with r3,0 to neutralize grouping effects, and testing on all anomalies except MACP and r3,0 regardless of significance: DS3 still has the smallest mean absolute alpha and t(alpha) (full-sample 0.610%, t=0.973, 3 unexplained vs CPT3 0.942%, t=1.497, 13 unexplained; Table 12). (4) Appendix Table A1 — full replication of Table 6 on CryptoCompare data: DS3 again best out-of-sample (0.737%, t=1.297, GRS p=0.05) and full-sample (0.625%, t=1.330, GRS p=0.08), though in-sample GRS p is 0.06. The authors note the dataset differs from Liu et al. (2022)'s even in the in-sample period because CoinMarketCap added coins post-July 2020 that existed earlier (§5.3).

## Specification search

The paper reports full results for all 49 anomalies × 3 periods in Table 3 (every anomaly, not a selection), all 49 × 3 in the WLS-FM regression (Table 9), the 5–6 week long-term test for all 49 (Table 4), and the top-100 sorts for the subset of anomalies it focuses on (Table 10, "We only show the results which we focus on"). The DS-Lasso searched over the 49 anomalies with a stated stopping rule (maximum t-statistic until none significant, then t < 1.64 stepwise elimination), reporting only the final DS3 selection. No multiplicity adjustment, data-mining buffer, or hold-out discipline beyond the single in/out-of-sample split is described.

## Limitations and future work

The paper's own stated limitations are thin. It attributes the CPT3 out-of-sample failure to grouping-method mismatch with its dataset and to the model elevating previously insignificant anomalies to significance (§4.1), and treats the changing anomaly landscape (size disappearing, left-tail risk appearing) as evidence that factor models built on earlier logic need re-evaluation (§6). It flags that IPCA3 is not designed for anomaly explanation and therefore its poor elimination performance is not a failure of the method (§3.4). No explicit limitations-and-future-work section exists.

## Separable variants for our pipeline

For this comparison paper, the separable units are the anomalies and the DS3 factors themselves:

- **MOM2 / r2,0** — two-week past return spread, weekly quintile sort; the single most persistent signal (3.9%/wk, t=5.322, full sample; Table 3). Survives top-100 restriction (Table 10) and the WLS-FM test (Table 9).
- **RMOM** — four-week CAPM-residual momentum, independent of raw momentum per the spanning test; stable across all three periods (Table 3).
- **Short-horizon momentum family** — r1,0, r3,0, r4,0, r3,1, r4,1, all significant in-sample and full-sample (Table 3); decay to insignificance by T+3–T+4 (Table 4).
- **Size complex** — MACP, PRC, MAXPRC, PRCVOL, STDPRCVOL: significant in-sample and full-sample, dead out-of-sample (Table 3); in-sample only, arguably not a live signal post-2020.
- **Left-tail risk reversal** — MIN, DVAR1, DES1, ES5, VAR10: negative/positive out-of-sample predictive patterns and delayed (T+5–T+6) predictability (Tables 3–4); candidate only with the noted lag structure.
- **Amihud ratio** — positive and stable across T+2…T+6 (Table 4), positive and significant in-sample (2.1%, t=2.577) but zero out-of-sample (Table 3).
- **MKT factor** — value-weighted market return, 1.2%/wk (t=2.652), the DS3 market leg (Table 5).
