### Claim

A horse-race comparison of cryptocurrency-specific and traditional equity risk factors in cross-sectional (Fama-MacBeth) multifactor regressions on up to 1,900 simultaneously traded cryptocurrencies over January 10, 2014 to October 30, 2020 (356 weeks, 216,369 return observations). The authors find that only three factors are systematically priced with significant premiums: downside market risk, cryptocurrency size (CSMB), and a cryptocurrency policy-uncertainty factor (UCRY). The equity *downside* market risk premium is significant even though the plain equity market premium is not, and equity downside risk dominates cryptocurrency downside risk when both are included — the paper's central, contrarian-to-earlier-literature result that equity market risk is more important than cryptocurrency market risk. The best-fitting multifactor model combines equity downside risk, CSMB, and UCRY (R² = 0.14), yet intercepts remain positive and highly significant, so most of the crypto-market's high average return is left unexplained. Momentum (CWML) shows a large (46% p.a.) but statistically insignificant premium and the authors, contrary to Liu et al. (2020) and in line with Grobys and Sapkota (2019), find no significant momentum pricing. VIX and VCRIX are priced only at 90% confidence. The paper is a comparison/horse-race, so no single strategy is promoted; the explanatory factors are long-short portfolios and exposure betas rather than directly traded signals.

### Fit with our constraints

Instruments are cryptocurrencies (spot, capitalization-weighted), with equity factors drawn from Kenneth French data library, VIX from CBOE, and two crypto-sourced indices (VCRIX, UCRY). The priced "factors" are long-short portfolios constructed from the crypto cross-section; a short leg is required by construction for CSMB and CWML (short the 30% biggest; short the 30% past losers). The paper reports long and short legs only as the combined zero-cost spread, not separately. Execution is weekly rebalancing; no transaction costs, financing costs, or borrow fees are modelled anywhere — all factor returns and premiums are gross. No leverage. Data replication would require coinmarketcap.com price/capitalization history including delisted coins (the dataset is explicitly survivorship-free), the French equity factors, VIX, VCRIX (from thecrix.de), and UCRY (from Brian Lucey's website).

### Strategies

All strategies are tested identically through two-step Fama-MacBeth: on step one, each coin's weekly returns are regressed on the factors over a 26-week (half-year) rolling window to estimate betas; on step two, next-week cross-sectional returns are regressed on those trailing betas to estimate premiums; the window rolls forward one week at a time. A significant positive premium means higher-exposure coins earn higher subsequent returns. Four factor-construction blocks follow.

**Cryptocurrency market (CMKT).** Capitalization-weighted portfolio of the entire sample universe (all coins with cap above $1M and ≥27 continuous weeks of observations). Also used as benchmark: Bitcoin alone (correlation with CMKT 0.91, t=40.84). The crypto market premium (13% p.a.) is insignificant in the CAPM (spec. 1, $t$=0.63) due to high volatility (§3, Table 3, Panel B).

**Cryptocurrency size (CSMB).** Weekly-rebalanced zero-cost portfolio, long 30% smallest coins, short 30% biggest, capitalization-weighted. Built following Liu et al. (2020) methodology but with calendar Monday-to-Friday weeks. Average annualized return 61% ($t$=2.26), Sharpe 0.12, positive skewness (off-the-chart spikes from occasional huge returns on tiny coins); uncorrelated with equity SMB. Priced in cross-section: premium 0.35 ($t$=2.04) in spec. 3; 0.41 ($t$=2.21) in spec. 4 (§3, Table 3 Panel B; §"Size and momentum factors" / Table 2).

**Cryptocurrency momentum (CWML).** Weekly-rebalanced zero-cost portfolio, long 30% highest prior-week return, short 30% lowest, capitalization-weighted. The authors use a one-week sorting horizon because it maximises momentum in-sample (footnote 2; Liu et al. use three weeks). Average return 46% p.a. but $t$=0.95, insignificant, and premium insignificant/negative (−0.18, $t$=−0.48). Recent negative spikes driven by extraordinary returns on a few past-loser coins in 2019–2020; premouvement was significant over 2014–2018 (footnote 4). Equity and crypto momentum factors uncorrelated (correlation −0.05), both negatively skewed (momentum crash risk).

**Downside risk premium.** The CAPM-with-downside-risk of Ang et al. (2006), tested by Lettau et al. (2014): an extra term compensating the *relative* downside beta $(β^{-}-β)$, where downside beta is estimated on market-down weeks. Crypto downside premium 0.28 ($t$=2.56) in Panel B spec. 2; significant in all crypto specifications. Equity downside premium 0.10 (Panel A spec. 2). In the joint horse-race (Table 4) equity downside risk stays significant (0.17, $t$=2.15; 0.18, $t$=2.30; 0.12, $t$=2.33 in specs 2/4/5) while crypto downside risk is driven to insignificance (0.05, 0.03), establishing that equity downside risk subsumes crypto market downside risk.

**Uncertainty (UCRY).** Policy-uncertainty sub-index of Lucey et al. (2021). Premium ~0.03 ($t$≈2.1–2.3) across specs 5–7, robust to controlling for equity factors. In the three-factor model with equity downside risk it suppresses the significance of CSMB (spec. 7, CSMB drops to 0.26, $t$=1.63). The price-uncertainty sub-index is nowhere significant (footnote 7). No sorting/portfolio mechanics given — exposure is a beta.

**Priced at 90% only.** VIX (20.55, $t$=1.73) and VCRIX (12.38, $t$=1.63) premiums are significant at 90% confidence in single-factor specifications (spec. 6 Panel A, spec. 8 Panel B). SMB/HML/CMA/RMW/WML equity factors: all insignificant for crypto returns (specs 3–4, Panel A).

**Execution timing and turnover.** For the long-short factors, sorting uses the prior week's return (momentum) or size at weekly rebalance; the cross-sectional test applies trailing 26-week betas to next-week returns, so there is a one-week gap between beta estimation and trade week. Turnover is not reported for any factor.

### Test setup

Sample period January 10, 2014 to October 30, 2020, chosen because the crypto-market exceeds 20 coins in 2014; it covers stable periods, booms and busts. Universe: all coins with market capitalization above $1 million that have ≥27 continuous weeks of data; 1,986 such "big" coins over the full period, up to ~1,900 simultaneously traded (Figure 1). Data from coinmarketcap.com including delisted coins, so no survivorship bias is addressed because none exists by construction. Weekly returns computed from Monday open to Friday close for comparability with equities (ignoring weekend trading); sample gives 356 weekly returns; 0.005% of outliers winsorized (worst surviving weekly returns span −100% to +12,439%). Equity dimensions: US, global, and four regional (North-American, European, Asian-Pacific, Japanese) from Kenneth French library; VIX from CBOE; VCRIX normalized to VIX's 5.12.2014 starting value. All returns and premiums are gross; no transaction, financing, or borrow-cost model is described. Benchmarks: MKT, CMKT, and Bitcoin as alternate market proxies (footnote 5: using Bitcoin instead gives near-identical results, cross-correlation 0.91).

### Results

Table 1: the crypto market (CMKT) shows roughly four times the equity market's average return (0.88 vs 0.12 p.a.), median return, and volatility (4.86 vs 1.22), with higher Sharpe (0.18 vs 0.10); it has positive skewness (0.52) and lower kurtosis (2.41) than the equity market (−0.94; 8.95). It carries a statistically significant positive weekly correlation with equities (0.11, $t$=2.17); regular equity beta 0.46 ($t$=2.05), downside beta 0.62 ($t$=1.89) higher than upside beta 0.25 ($t$=0.65) — downside comovement is far stronger.

The authors conclude that the crypto-market has only an emerging, weak factor structure — most average return is unexplained (large significant intercepts, low R²) — and that equity downside risk being priced above crypto downside risk indicates partial integration with general financial markets.

### Robustness

There is no dedicated robustness section. Within-specification evidence includes: using Bitcoin as the crypto-market proxy yields near-identical results (footnote 5); momentum sorting horizon of one week maximises the effect in-sample, longer horizons weaken but do not flip it (footnote 2); momentum was significant in 2014–2018 and degraded on the recent-year negative spikes (Footnote 4). Cross-region comparison (Table 5) checks robustness of the equity-factor results to geographic specification. No subperiod, no alternative universe, no delayed-execution, and no transaction-cost sensitivity analyses are reported.

### Specification search

Eight multifactor specifications for equity factors and eight for crypto factors (Table 3), five joint horse-race specifications (Table 4), and the Carhart model under five regional factor sets (Table 5). All specifications' results are reported (no cherry-picking); the price-uncertainty sub-index results are mentioned as insignificant but not tabulated (footnote 7).

### Limitations and future work

The paper's own limitations: the factor structure of the crypto-market is only emerging and a large average return remains unexplained by all tested factors; momentum yield an insignificant premium and its timing differs significantly between markets (Figures the authors flag for further investigation). The authors expect greater market convergence and clearer asset-pricing relationships in future. They note methodological flaws in prior narrow-sample work (constant beta/premium assumptions) that motivated their design. The paper does not claim tradability or provide cost accounting.

### Separable variants for our pipeline

Each priced factor is independently testable as a signal:
- **CSMB** size factor (long ~30% smallest vs short ~30% biggest, cap-weighted, weekly rebalance) — §"Size and momentum factors", Table 2, §3 spec. 3–4.
- **CWML** one-week momentum factor (long 30% winners / short 30% losers) — §"Size and momentum factors", footnote 2.
- **Equity/crypto downside beta** $(β^{-}-β)$ exposure as a cross-sectional signal — §3 specs 2, 4, 6; Table 4.
- **UCRY policy-uncertainty beta** exposure — §3 specs 5–7.
- **VCRIX / VIX volatility-exposure beta** (priced only at 90%) — §3 specs 6/8.
- The four-region plus global Carhart equity factor sets (global dominant) — §"Global and regional factors", Table 5.

For a comparison paper this list largely restates the priced factors, which is intended.
