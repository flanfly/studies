Category: **methodology** — a comparison of static and dynamic copula models for the dependence structure among Bitcoin, the S&P 500 and the US Dollar Index, plus Bai–Perron structural-break dating of that dependence. No strategy, portfolio sort, or return backtest appears anywhere in the paper; the empirical findings (asymmetric tail dependence, regime shifts, the FTX break) are outputs of the dependence models, not tradable signals. This extraction follows the methodology template: the three competing models stand in for strategies, and what the models take as input, produce, and are benchmarked against is described in their place.

## Claim

The paper models the static and dynamic dependence structure between Bitcoin (btc), the S&P 500 and the US Dollar Index (dxy) over January 4, 2021–December 31, 2025 (N = 1,254 daily observations after aligning to common trading days), using GARCH-filtered marginals, a static C-vine copula, a DCC-GARCH model, and a rolling dynamic C-vine, compared strictly out-of-sample via predictive log-likelihoods (N_pred = 1,002) and Diebold–Mariano tests, with Bai–Perron multiple-break tests applied to rolling Kendall's τ series (Abstract; §2; §4). Four main findings: (i) the static C-vine uncovers a heterogeneous structure — a Survival Gumbel copula for S&P 500–btc (θ̂ = 1.278, SE = 0.113, p < 0.001, τ̂ = +0.218) capturing upper-tail co-movement, and a Student-t copula for dxy–btc conditional on S&P 500 (ρ̂ = −0.273, SE = 0.066, p < 0.001, ν̂ ≈ 5, τ̂ = −0.176) capturing symmetric tail dependence invisible to linear correlation (§1, §4.2.2, Table 3); (ii) the static C-vine predicts best out-of-sample (log-score 0.1366/obs vs. 0.1301 for DCC-Student-t and 0.1236 for the dynamic vine), interpreted as parsimony outperforming flexibility (§4.4, Table 5); (iii) Bai–Perron tests identify three breaks per pair, recovering the FTX collapse of November 9, 2022 automatically as a break in btc–dxy dependence (§4.5.1, Table 6); (iv) the S&P 500–btc correlation is strictly positive across all four regimes (mean +0.338, range [+0.079, +0.538]), which the authors read as refuting Bitcoin's diversification role in the post-institutionalisation era (§4.3, Table 4; §5). No t-statistic accompanies the predictive comparison beyond the Diebold–Mariano statistics.

## Fit with our constraints

The paper constructs no strategy, so most mechanical checks are vacuous rather than passed: no short leg is required or reported, no long/short legs are separated, no leverage is used, and no transaction or financing costs are modelled anywhere — all dependence estimates are computed on log-returns gross of any cost. Instrument classes involved are crypto spot (btc/USD), an equity index (S&P 500), and an FX index (dxy); nothing in the analysis requires short stock or short options. Data replication needs daily closing prices for the three series from Yahoo Finance (retrieved January 2026) and R 4.4 with the VineCopula, rugarch/rmgarch and strucchange packages; replication code is available on request (§3; Data Availability Statement). The sample is short — five years, 1,254 observations — and ends December 31, 2025, six months before the July 16, 2026 preprint date; no post-publication validation exists because the paper is itself unpublished.

## Strategies

The paper's units are three dependence models (§2.2–§2.3), all consuming the same input: probability-integral-transformed (PIT) residuals u_{i,t} = F_i(ẑ_{i,t} | ν̂_i, ξ̂_i) from GARCH marginals (eq. 5), verified U[0,1] by a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. The marginals are selected in a two-stage grid search (§2.1) over standard GARCH(1,1) (eq. 2), EGARCH(1,1) (eq. 3), and GJR-GARCH(1,1) (eq. 4), each with Student-t or skewed Student-t innovations: Stage 1 keeps only models passing the Ljung–Box test at lag 10 (5%) on standardised residuals and their squares; Stage 2 minimises BIC_i = −2L̂_i + k_i ln T. Selected: S&P 500 EGARCH(1,1)-sstd, btc EGARCH(1,1)-std, dxy sGARCH(1,1)-std; btc's EGARCH leverage parameter γ̂ = 0.126 (p < 0.001) (Table 1).

### Static C-vine copula (§2.2, §4.2)

The joint density decomposes as an R-vine of pair-copulas (eq. 6); for n = 3 this is two pair-copulas in Tree 1 plus one conditional copula in Tree 2, with S&P 500 as the central node. Tree structure maximises the sum of absolute Kendall's τ in Tree 1; family selection among Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, Gumbel, Frank, Survival Clayton, Survival Gumbel and their 90°/270° rotations uses AIC (Dissmann et al. 2013). Outputs (Table 3, bootstrap SEs B = 500 with structure fixed): Tree 1 S&P 500–btc Survival Gumbel θ̂ = 1.278 (SE 0.113), τ̂ = +0.218, bootstrap 95% CI [1.057, 1.500] entirely above the independence boundary; Tree 1 S&P 500–dxy Frank θ̂ = −0.541 (SE 0.280), p = 0.053, τ̂ = −0.060; Tree 2 dxy–btc | S&P 500 Student-t ρ̂ = −0.273 (SE 0.066), 95% CI [−0.402, −0.144], ν̂ ≈ 5, τ̂ = −0.176. White goodness-of-fit test: stat = 6.881, p = 0.520. Full-sample fit: LogLik = 154.20, AIC = −300.40, BIC = −279.87, k = 4. The authors' rationale is risk-based: Bitcoin and equities co-move more strongly in rallies, so "diversification benefits are reduced precisely when they are most needed" (§4.2.2).

### DCC-GARCH (§2.3.1, §4.3)

The DCC-GARCH(1,1) benchmark (Engle 2002) parameterises H_t = D_t R_t D_t with Q_t = (1 − a − b)Q̄ + a ε_{t−1}ε'_{t−1} + b Q_{t−1}, a + b < 1 (eq. 7). Estimated by two-step QML with Student-t innovations: â = 0.0143 (SE 0.0047, t = 3.047, p = 0.002), b̂ = 0.9749 (SE 0.0100, t = 97.77, p < 0.001), â + b̂ = 0.9892, implying a correlation-shock half-life of ≈ 27 trading days (ln 0.5 / ln b̂) (Table 4a). It produces dynamic conditional correlations (Table 4b): S&P 500–BTC mean +0.3383, min +0.0790, max +0.5381; S&P 500–DXY mean −0.2210, min −0.4857, max +0.1563; BTC–DXY mean −0.1280, min −0.3617, max +0.1468. The scalar-correlation parameterisation cannot accommodate the vine's pair-specific asymmetric tail structure (§4.4).

### Rolling dynamic C-vine (§2.3.2, §4.5.2)

The same C-vine is re-estimated on a moving window of T₀ = 252 trading days; at each step t the vine is estimated on [t − T₀, t − 1] and scored by the one-step-ahead predictive log-likelihood PLS_t (eq. 8). Family selection is AIC-based per window. Over the 1,002 windows, no single family dominates any marginal pair — btc–S&P 500 selects Frank 31.9% and rotated Joe 270° 21.4%; dxy–S&P 500 Gaussian 27.2% and Frank 26.9% — while the conditional dxy–btc pair is markedly more stable (Student-t 42.0%, Frank 21.6%), consistent with the static C-vine's structure (Table 8).

### What the comparison establishes

On a strictly out-of-sample basis (estimation window 252 days, N_pred = 1,002 one-step-ahead scores), the static C-vine ranks first on predictive log-likelihood, predictive AIC and predictive BIC; the DCC-Student-t is second and the rolling dynamic vine last, a shortfall the authors attribute to family instability adding estimation noise (§4.4). The ranking rests on consistent ordinal dominance rather than formal rejection: the Diebold–Mariano tests, reported in Results below, do not reject equal accuracy (Table 5b).

## Test setup

Sample period is January 4, 2021–December 31, 2025, chosen to span the post-COVID institutionalisation cycle: the 2022 crypto bear market (btc peak-to-trough −77%), the Fed tightening cycle (400 bps over 2022–2023), the FTX collapse (November 8–11, 2022), US spot Bitcoin ETF approval (January 10, 2024) and the fourth halving (April 19, 2024) (§1, §3). After alignment to common trading days, N = 1,254; log-returns are r_{i,t} = 100 × ln(P_{i,t}/P_{i,t−1}); prices are daily closes from Yahoo Finance, retrieved January 2026. btc is the most volatile series (annualised σ̂ ≈ 68% vs. ≈ 19% for S&P 500 and ≈ 7% for dxy); all three series show excess kurtosis and negative skewness, and augmented Dickey–Fuller tests reject the unit root at the 1% level (§3). Survivorship and delisting treatment are not applicable to indices and are not discussed. There is no return-based evaluation: the metric is out-of-sample predictive log-likelihood with Diebold–Mariano testing (Newey–West HAC, lag 5) (§2.4); structural breaks are dated by Bai–Perron tests (m_max = 3, modified BIC of Liu et al. 1997) on 252-day rolling Kendall's τ series (eq. 10). No transaction costs, financing costs, or benchmark are involved.

Segment-mean rolling τ̂: btc–S&P 500 rises from +0.071 (segment 1) to +0.261 (segment 3), then +0.198; dxy–S&P 500 peaks in absolute terms at −0.301 in segment 2 (rate-hike cycle), attenuating to −0.098; dxy–btc moves −0.189 → −0.073 (>60% reduction in absolute dependence at the FTX break) → −0.114 → −0.047 (Table 7).

The authors conclude that the static C-vine's informational advantage over DCC resides in the conditional dxy–btc pair, whose structure is stable enough to survive time aggregation (§4.5.2); that the strictly positive S&P 500–btc correlation across all regimes refutes the diversification narrative (§5); and that asymmetric tail dependence means linear or unconditional CoVaR-type indicators would underestimate joint downside risk, so a systemic-risk indicator built on crypto–traditional linkages needs a rolling or regime-aware window (§5). No Sharpe ratio, alpha, t-statistic on returns, or cost-adjusted figure is reported; the paper contains no return series other than the descriptive daily log-returns.

## Robustness

Robustness apparatus: bootstrap standard errors on the static C-vine (B = 500, structure fixed), with 95% intervals reported for the Survival Gumbel parameter [1.057, 1.500] and the Student-t ρ [−0.402, −0.144] (§4.2.2); a White goodness-of-fit test (stat = 6.881, p = 0.520); Ljung–Box diagnostics on all three marginals (squared-residual p-values 0.826, 0.731, 0.713 for S&P 500, btc, dxy; the dxy LB(10) p-value of 0.0655 on standardised residuals is the weakest) (Table 1); the Diebold–Mariano non-rejections, reported honestly (§4.4); and the rolling family-stability analysis of Table 8. A preliminary Monte Carlo exercise suggests a Gaussian copula understates 99% Expected Shortfall by ≈ 6.8% for a 50/50 btc–S&P 500 portfolio, but the authors state formal validation with ES-specific backtests (Acerbi and Székely 2014) is left for future work (§5). There is no alternative-universe, alternative-window, or subperiod re-estimation of the full design beyond the regime segmentation itself.

## Specification search

The marginal grid is stated as 3 × 3 × 2 = 36 candidate specifications (three variance processes, two innovation distributions, plus one further dimension not itemised), with only the winning models reported (Table 1). Copula candidates span eight families plus 90°/270° rotations, selected by AIC per window, with results for 1,002 rolling windows summarised only as the top-two families per pair (Table 8). Bai–Perron searches over m_max = 3 breaks per pair with the modified BIC of Liu et al. (1997) selecting m; only the selected three-break solutions are reported (Table 6). Selected-only reporting applies throughout; no full grid results are given.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§5): formal Expected Shortfall backtesting (Acerbi and Székely 2014) of the Monte Carlo estimate is deferred; the framework could be extended to wider asset universes; observation-driven vine models with fixed family structure could be used; macroeconomic state variables (VIX, yield curve slope) could explain the detected regime changes; and the estimated pairwise dependence structures could be embedded in a formal systemic-risk indicator such as a crypto-augmented CoVaR to quantify cryptocurrency stress transmission to traditional markets.
