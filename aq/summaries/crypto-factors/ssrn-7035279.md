**Category: empirical.** This is a valuation/causality study of DeFi DAO treasuries, not a
trading-strategy backtest. It documents a relationship between a protocol's on-chain cash
share and the market's valuation of its economic activity; it reports no portfolio sorts and
no return series.

### Claim

The paper proposes a taxonomy of treasury token risk and, treating only fiat-backed
stablecoins held as base assets (not redeployed for yield) in DAO treasury smart contracts as
"cash," tests whether the treasury cash-to-asset ratio Granger-causes — and in a panel VAR
with cumulative impulse effects predicts — a protocol's fee-yield valuation multiple. The
instrument is a weekly balanced panel of N = 23 DAOs over T = 112 weeks, January 2024 to
February 2026 (§3.1; note the descriptive table's own header states 108 weeks and N = 2,484
observations, Table 3). The headline finding is a significant, unidirectional, *positive*
cumulative effect of cash share on fee yield (§4.3), which — because fee yield is
fees/Mcap — the authors read as *lower* valuations per unit of activity following higher cash
shares, consistent with the corporate-finance **agency motive** (§4.5). The Granger null of
non-causality from cash to fee yield is rejected at the 1% level with bootstrap p, while
reverse causality is not rejected (§4.2). At the individual-protocol level the effect is
heterogeneous, with large-cap DeFi protocols (Aave, Compound, Balancer, Yearn) showing the
opposite (precautionary, negative) response (§4.4).

### Fit with our constraints

No instruments, no long/short legs, no returns, no leverage, no turnover, no transaction
costs. The dependent variable is a valuation multiple, not a tradable return, and the signal
is protocol cash allocation — an on-chain governance-layer accounting variable with no
functions/perpetuals expression. Data replication would require DeFillama.com treasury
snapshots mapped to token categories, protocol market cap, weekly fees, and market controls
(ETH volume, DPI and BTC returns from Yahoo Finance). Not applicable to our strategy pipeline
as stated; nothing in the paper is expressed as gross or net of costs.

### Anomaly evidence

This is an anomaly-style relationship demonstrated with time-series causality and VAR
impulse responses rather than a portfolio sort. The conditioning variable and its
demonstration are extracted below with the same completeness.

**The conditioning variable.** The cash-to-asset ratio (equation 1, §3.1):

$$\text{CA}_{i,t} = \frac{\text{Cash}_{i,t}}{\text{Total Assets}_{i,t} - \text{Native Tokens}_{i,t}}$$

where Cash is the USD value of fiat-backed stablecoins held as base asset in the treasury
smart contract, and Native Tokens are subtracted to correct for undistributed governance
tokens (which the author argues overstate treasury value). The valuation outcome is annualized
fee yield (equation 2, §3.1):

$$\text{FY}_{i,t} = \left( \frac{7\text{-day fees}_{i,t} \times 52}{\text{Mcap}_{i,t}} \right) \times 100$$

Both use on-chain and market data observed essentially contemporaneously at the weekly
aggregation level; there is no stated lag between signal observation and portfolio formation,
because no portfolio is formed.

**How the anomaly is demonstrated.** Pretests establish cross-sectional dependence (Pesaran CD
rejects independence at the 1% level for both series, FY = 6.718***, CA = 11.935***, Table 4)
and stationarity in levels (CIPS rejects a unit root, FY = −2.583***, CA = −2.372***,
Table 4). CSD-robust Dumitrescu–Hurlin panel Granger tests find forward causality CA→FY
significant at K = 1 (Z̃ = 7.665, pboot = 0.006***) and marginally at K = 3 (Z̃ = 3.096,
pboot = 0.060**), with reverse causality FY→CA insignificant at all lags (pboot = 0.600,
0.366 at K = 1; Table 5). A bivariate panel VAR (FE-OLS with Arellano cluster-robust SEs,
23 clusters, lag order p = 4 by BIC/HQIC) gives a positive cumulative cash coefficient
(Table 7). No portfolio-based breakdown (value- vs equal-weight) exists because no portfolios
are formed.

**Leg decomposition.** Not applicable; there is no short leg.

**What fails to explain it.** There is no factor-model alpha here. The identification
equivalent is the set of market controls (log ETH volume, DPI return, BTC return) absorbed
via time demeaning or explicit controls, which does not remove the effect (§4.3, Table 7). The
three robustness variants in Table 12 keep the cumulative effect significant (z ≈ 2.8, p
≈ 0.004–0.006).

**Where the return concentrates** (here: which protocols). Individual VARs (equation 10, §4.4)
allow protocol-specific slopes. For 13 of 20 retained protocols the impact response interval
excludes zero, and for 10 the cumulative (h = 4) interval excludes zero. DeFi protocols —
Aave, Compound, Balancer, Yearn — record a *negative* fee-yield response to a +0.10 cash
shock (precautionary: higher valuations), while infrastructure/services protocols — Lido,
ENS, Venus, Convex — record a *positive* response (agency: lower valuations). Positive
responses appear early (weeks 0–2) and return to baseline sooner than negative responses
(which span weeks 0–5) for protocols with negative responses (Figure 8).

**Persistence and decay.** Individual impulse responses decay to baseline within roughly a
10-week horizon (Figures 8, 9); the VAR is covariance-stationary with largest companion-modulus
0.93 (Figure 7). No subperiod or announcement-seasonality analysis is reported.

**Frictions the authors acknowledge.** No transaction costs or capacity analysis, and none are
relevant to a valuation regression. The paper notes protocol treasuries below USD 1,000,000
AUM were excluded as "marginal" (§3.1), that ~2,600 token columns were manually mapped to
categories, and that the DAI→USDS conversion mechanically inflated the fiat base share
(§4.1).

**Sample end date versus publication date.** Sample ends February 2026; the paper is dated June
2026. The two are less than a year apart at extraction.

### Test setup

Balanced weekly panel, N = 23 protocols, T = 112 weeks (descriptive header: 108 weeks,
N = 2,484), January 2024–February 2026; system common sample Nobs = 2,392 for the VAR
(Table 6). Data sources: on-chain treasury balances from DeFillama.com (top 110 treasuries,
≈75% of treasury assets, filtered to 49, then 23 after merging market data); market cap, fees,
inflows, age from DeFillama.com; ETH volume, DeFi Pulse Index, Bitcoin, Ether prices from
Yahoo Finance. Estimation: "sandwich" package in R, fixed-effects OLS with Arellano cluster
standard errors (23 clusters), CSD-robust block bootstrap (block length 5, 999 replications)
for Granger tests, B = 500 bootstrap IRF bands. No survivorship concerns are discussed beyond
the noted sample restriction. All results are regression coefficients; there is no
transaction-cost or financing adjustment anywhere.

The authors conclude that the positive cumulative cash effect, robust across specifications
and nonzero for a substantial share of protocols, supports the agency interpretation on
average — markets discount protocols after cash-share increases — while the per-unit
heterogeneity (DeFi protocols precautionary, infrastructure/services agency) shows the signal
is read differently by protocol category and size (§4.5).

### Robustness

The paper reports: alternative lag order p = 1 (forward effect no longer significant, Table
11); adding a linear time trend and switching controls for two-way fixed effects (Table 12,
efforrei stable); individual one-standard-deviation shocks re-admitting low-variance protocols
hegic and qidao with signs preserved (Figure 10); CSD-robust vs asymptotic Granger critical
values (Table 5 note); and per-unit Breusch–Pagan lag diagnostics (14 of 20 units show no
residual autocorrelation at the chosen lag). No subperiod or alternative-universe analysis is
reported.

### Specification search

Reported variants are modest in number: two PVAR specifications (Table 7), three Granger lag
orders K ∈ {1,2,3} (Table 5), three robustness treatments (Table 12), and two shock-scaling
schemes for the individual VARs (Figures 8 and 10). Full results are shown for these; per-unit
lag selection is summarized by median/mean rather than shown for each protocol. No counts of
total models tried are given.

### Limitations and future work

The author's own (§5, §4.5): the sample is a two-year snapshot of protocols with continuous
treasury balances, likely biasing toward mature protocols while cash holdings may matter most
for emerging ones; the sample period is short and regime-specific; the individual-level
findings are "suggestive, pending further validation"; the panel estimator cannot distinguish
a uniform small effect from offsetting positive and negative unit responses (footnote 1,
§4.3). Proposed future work: drivers of treasury cash holdings; interacting governance-token
holder decentralization with cash holdings.

### Separable variants for our pipeline

Not a strategy paper, so there is no tradable variant to separate; the relevant constructs an
ideation loop might reuse are the risk classification (Table 1: token type × deployment state
composite risk) and the two operationalized signals, CA (equation 1) and FY (equation 2),
both defined in §3.1. The per-protocol heterogeneous responses (§4.4) hint that the
cash→valuation relationship differs by protocol category (DeFi vs infrastructure/services),
which is a conditioning distinction the paper itself draws but does not turn into a strategy.
