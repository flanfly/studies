Category: **comparison** — a 22-factor survey of weekly long-short strategies on the top-100 cryptocurrency cross-section, benchmarked against equity, commodity, and crypto-specific factor models across pre-COVID and COVID subperiods, followed by a replication-with-COVID-data of the Borri and Shakhnov (2018) cross/within bitcoin-discount strategies and their two-factor model. The cross/within section is also a **modification** exercise whose baseline (Borri and Shakhnov 2018, "The Cross-Section Of Cryptocurrency Returns") is described only by reference; the delta is the COVID market-stress period data.

## Claim

The paper compares how well macroeconomic and cryptocurrency-specific risk factors explain the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns during a stressed market environment (the COVID-19 pandemic) versus a normal one, using data from July 26, 2016 to August 10, 2020 (Abstract; §2.1). It finds that risk premia are "encapsulated within cryptocurrency-specific market factors" in both regimes; that crypto factors, "particularly relating to liquidity, momentum, and counterparty risk," predict returns more strongly during COVID than pre-pandemic; and that Fama-French 5-factor and commodity (gold, platinum) exposures provide near-zero explainability of crypto long-short portfolio returns (Abstract; §3.1.5; §4). Headline magnitudes: the one-week momentum long-short portfolio earns 0.352 per week (SE 0.043) in the COVID period (Table 3); the daily long-short *cross* bitcoin-discount portfolio earns a mean 0.0622 (SE 0.0034) with Sharpe ratio 1.609 (Table 10); and the first two principal components of the discount-portfolio returns explain 99% of their variance, motivating a two-factor (level = bitcoin returns, slope = long-short cross returns) model (Table 11). The paper's overall finding is that cryptocurrency risk is predominantly crypto-specific and self-contained across market regimes (§5).

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument classes are crypto spot: the top-100 cryptocurrencies by market cap and fiat-to-crypto BTC pairs across 11 currencies (AUD, CAD, DKK, EUR, HKD, ILS, JPY, PLN, CHF, GBP, USD) on numerous exchanges (§2.1). Every quintile strategy is reported as a long-short spread (long highest quintile, short lowest quintile), so a short leg is required; long and short legs are reported separately in all tables (Q5, Q1, 5-1 columns), so a long-only variant is recoverable. The cross long-short strategy "assumes that we are able to short bitcoin on the exchange, which is not supported across all platforms, albeit is becoming increasingly available" (footnote 13) — expressible in futures/perpetuals in principle, but the paper trades spot exchange prices and says nothing about perps. Leverage: none described; the cross strategy borrows 1 USD at the risk-free rate (a funding leg, not leverage) (§3.2.2). Returns are gross: "We did not account for transaction cost or bid ask spreads in our analysis" (§5), and no net figure appears. Replication needs: CryptoCompare fiat-crypto and top-100 OHLCV data (via the *cryptocompy* Python API), daily FX spot, Fama-French 5 factors plus 1-month T-bills (*pandas datareader*), London fixing gold/platinum prices (Quandl) (§2.1). Membership is not point-in-time: the top-100 ranking is "as of August 10, 2020" (sample end), and market-cap weights in the crypto market portfolio "were held constant throughout the period" (§2.1, §2.2). No microcap/penny-stock exclusion beyond the top-100 screen and, for discounts, a mean-daily-volume > 1000 filter (§2.2) is described; survivorship and delisting are not addressed.

## Strategies

### Momentum long-short (r1–r100) (§3.1.2.2)

Signal: weekly quintile portfolios formed on momentum at eight lookbacks — r1, r2, r3, r4, r8, r16, r50, r100 = one-, two-, three-, four-, eight-, sixteen-, fifty-, and hundred-week momentum (Table 1); construction is weekly from "the previous list of 100 cryptocurrencies," with mean excess return over the risk-free rate taken weekly (Table 3). Portfolios are value-weighted (footnote to Tables 2–5). Rationale is inherited from prior literature the paper builds on — Liu et al. (2019) and Liu & Tsyvinski (2018) establish momentum as a crypto-specific pricing factor; the paper does not itself frame momentum as behavioural or risk-based, and does not identify who is on the other side of the trade. Result: the 5-1 spread is 1%-significant for all lookbacks except r100 in the COVID period (r100: 0.028, SE 0.019, insignificant), and decreasing in lookback length within COVID (r1 0.352*** vs r50 0.066*); all eight lookbacks are 1%-significant pre-COVID and full-period (Tables 3, A.1–A.3). Turnover is not reported; the gap between signal date and trade date is not specified beyond weekly formation.

### Volume long-short (vol, prc_vol) (§3.1.2.3)

Signal: vol = log average daily volume in the portfolio formation week; prc_vol = log average daily volume times price in the formation week (Table 1). Longs the highest dollar-volume quintile, shorts the lowest. The paper's prose states the strategy "generates about 2.2% excess weekly returns" with 3–4% outside the pandemic (§3.1.2.3), but the tables give vol 5-1 = 0.035** (SE 0.014) and prc_vol = 0.022** (0.010) for the full period, 0.041** (0.016) / 0.022** (0.011) pre-COVID, and both insignificant in COVID (0.001 (0.012) / 0.019 (0.024)) (Table 4) — prose and table figures are not reconciled. Same weekly value-weighted quintile setup, risk-free excess returns; turnover and execution timing not specified.

### Volatility long-short (10 factors) (§3.1.2.4)

Signals (Table 1): beta and beta_squared (regression coefficient and its square, estimated on the previous 365 days of daily returns before the formation week); idiovol (standard deviation of the residual of that model); ret_vol (std of daily returns in the formation week); ret_skew and ret_kurt (skewness and kurtosis of daily returns in the formation week); max_ret (maximum daily return of the formation week); delay (the paper's table gives only a truncated definition: "improvement… estimated using daily returns of the previous 365 days before the formation week"); std_prc_vol (log std of dollar volume in the formation week); damihud (average absolute daily return divided by dollar volume in the formation week). Only two of the ten produce significant 5-1 spreads during COVID: ret_skew 0.121*** (SE 0.038) and max_ret 0.147*** (0.047); the other eight are insignificant in COVID, whereas most are significant pre-COVID — e.g., ret_vol 0.072*** (0.021), std_prc_vol 0.026** (0.011), damihud −0.03** (0.012) pre-COVID, and beta 0.080** (0.040) and idiovol −0.039* (0.022) pre-COVID only (Tables 5, A.2; §4). Note the damihud and idiovol spreads are negative pre-COVID — low-dollar-volume and high-idio-vol names outperformed.

### Cross discount strategy (§3.2.2–3.2.3) — modification of Borri & Shakhnov (2018)

Baseline: Borri and Shakhnov (2018), cited by name; the paper states no equations from the baseline beyond reproducing its strategy definitions, so the baseline is pinned down only by reference. The discount is $d_{m,j} = P_{m,j} / P_{USD} − 1$, where $P_{m,j}$ is the USD-converted daily open price of the BTC pair on market $m$, currency $j$, and the numeraire is the Coinbase USD open price (§3.2.1). Cross return: $r_{m,j,t+1}^{cross} = \log(P_{m,j,t+1}) - \log(P_{USD,t}) - r_t^f$ — at $t$ the investor borrows 1 USD at the risk-free rate, buys BTC in USD at Coinbase, transfers it to pair $(m,j)$, and at $t+1$ converts back to USD at the daily spot and repays the loan; every leg is against Coinbase. Signal-to-trade gap is one day, with the caveat that "the transfer of bitcoin may take anywhere up to 24 hours" (footnote 13). Seven portfolios are formed from the 83 discounts by ranking on full-sample mean discount, 11 pairs each (portfolio 1 = lowest, portfolio 7 = highest), equally weighted; the long-short longs portfolio 7 and shorts portfolio 1 (Tables 9, A.10). Portfolio assignment uses full-sample mean discounts, so it is not point-in-time; no rebalancing frequency is stated ("Once the pairs are assigned to a portfolio" — §3.2.3). Portfolio means are strictly increasing in rank with roughly constant std, so Sharpe rises from −0.196 (P1) to 0.827 (P7); the long-short has daily mean 0.0622, SE 0.0034, Sharpe 1.609 (Table 10). The paper calls the strategy impractical (footnote 13). Rationale: exploits "the persistence and significance of the bitcoin discounts" (§3.2.2); the slope factor is "related to crypto liquidity, momentum, and counterparty risks" following Borri-Shakhnov (§3.2.4). Counterparties are not identified.

### Within discount strategy (§3.2.2–3.2.3) — modification of Borri & Shakhnov (2018)

Same 83-discount, seven-portfolio construction, but the return is $r_{m,j,t+1}^{within} = \log(P_{m,j,t+1}) - \log(P_{m,j,t}) - r_t^f$: all legs occur inside one pair $(m,j)$, $j \neq$ USD, converting BTC back to currency $j$ then USD at the daily spot — this exploits "the mean-reversion of the bitcoin discounts" (§3.2.2). The long-short shorts portfolio 7 and longs portfolio 1 (opposite sign to cross). Portfolio means are decreasing in rank; the long-short has daily mean 0.0034, SE 0.0057, Sharpe 0.0542 (Table 10). Same practicality caveats.

### What the comparison establishes

Within the factor survey, short-horizon momentum dominates: r1–r4 spreads are the largest and survive all periods, while size factors are insignificant everywhere and only ret_skew/max_ret survive COVID among volatility factors (§3.1.2). Across asset classes, the crypto CAPM has negative adjusted R² for nearly all long-short portfolios (Table 6), Fama-French 3 and 5 factors give adjusted R² between −0.081 and 0.078 (Tables 8.1, 14, A.4–A.5), and gold/platinum regressions are indistinguishable from noise (Table 8.2, A.6–A.7) — the predictable variation is crypto-specific. The two-factor crypto model (CAPM + a momentum portfolio long the top 30% / short the bottom 30% of three-week momentum) raises adjusted R² to 0.812–0.887 for the r2–r4 portfolios (Table 15), and the discount-based two-factor model (bitcoin returns + long-short cross returns) attains adjusted R² of 0.543–0.996 across the seven cross portfolios (Table 16). The paper favours the two-factor crypto structure over all non-crypto benchmarks, on fit (§4).

## Test setup

Sample periods: pre-COVID July 26, 2016 – November 1, 2019; COVID November 1, 2019 – August 10, 2020 (§2.1; §3.1.2 states the COVID period for the quintile sorts as ending August 6, 2020 — the two dates are not reconciled); full period both combined. The discount analysis uses daily data November 1, 2019 – July 1, 2020 (175 dates; pairs with more than 100 missing dates, or mean daily volume below 1000, are dropped) (§2.2). Instruments: 100 cryptocurrencies for the factor sorts; 83 discount pairs for the cross-section. Data sources: cryptocompy (CryptoCompare), pandas datareader (Fama-French data, 1-month T-bills, FX spot), Quandl (gold and platinum London fixings) (§2.1). The sample ends August 10, 2020 (July 1, 2020 for discounts); the paper is dated June 2021 — roughly a ten-month gap, and over five years have elapsed since (current date August 2026). No out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported; the pre-COVID/COVID split is an in-sample segmentation. Survivorship and delisting are not addressed; membership is end-of-sample. Costs: none; headline numbers are gross. The benchmark bitcoin price is the Coinbase USD open price (§3.2.1, footnote 14); the equity benchmark is the Fama-French factor set.

## Robustness

There is no formal robustness section. What the paper tests instead: (i) the full factor survey is repeated in three subperiods with all results tabulated, and the authors report the honest degradation — momentum loses significance at long lookbacks during COVID, volume factors die entirely in COVID, only ret_skew and max_ret survive among volatility factors (§3.1.2, §4); (ii) the same long-short regressions run against crypto CAPM, CAPM+momentum, FF3, FF5, and gold/platinum models appear in the body for the full period and in the appendix for the two subperiods (Tables A.4–A.7), with materially the same conclusions; (iii) PCA independently motivates the two-factor model — the first two components explain 99% of cross-portfolio variance (vs 94% reported for Borri-Shakhnov's earlier sample, read as the factors being "even stronger during the COVID-period," footnote 16). No alternative parameters, delayed-execution, alternative-universe, or transaction-cost tests are reported.

## Specification search

22 factors are constructed under four categories (size 2, momentum 8, volume 2, volatility 10; Table 1), each sorted into weekly value-weighted quintiles in three periods, and results are reported for all 22 × 3 combinations in full (Tables 2–5; appendix A.1–A.3), not selected — except that Table 13 in §4 lists only the 15 factors "key in capturing the cross-section" (8 momentum + 2 volume + 5 volatility). On the cross-section: 83 discounts, of which 13 (15.66%) fail a two-sided t-test of zero mean and 17 (20.48%) fail the augmented Dickey-Fuller stationarity test (95% confidence) (§3.2.1); 14 portfolios (7 cross, 7 within) plus two long-shorts are evaluated; PCA is run on the 7 cross portfolios; and 9 long-short factor portfolios are regressed against 5 model families. All of these are reported; nothing is described as a preliminary search.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§5): market capitalization data are excluded from the analysis "due to lack of publicly available data"; the market did not mature until late 2016 and "Bitcoin has been by far the dominant asset representing approximately 90% of the whole market until early 2017," which "can cause a great bias towards understanding how the market works"; transaction costs and bid-ask spreads were not accounted for and "may have a significant influence on the results"; more historical data would allow more factors to be tested. Proposed future work: a market-cap-weighted crypto market index and a cryptocurrency CAPM; separating the market into small-, medium-, and large-cap and constructing market-cap-weighted portfolios.

Table 14's caption says "4-Factor Fama French Model" while the surrounding text and Figure 12 say 5-factor — likely the 5-factor model. COVID period end is given as August 10, 2020 in §2.1 and August 6, 2020 in §3.1.2. The prose claims "about 2.2% excess weekly returns" for the volume strategy while tables report 0.035/0.022 — unreconciled. The text says the minimum mean discount is "−0.2688%" — a stray percent sign, as the value is a fraction matching Table A.10's −0.2688. §4 says "the additional volume factor beta and idiovol" — "volume" appears to be a typo for "volatility."
