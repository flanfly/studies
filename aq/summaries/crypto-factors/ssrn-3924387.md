Category: **comparison** — twenty-two constructed cryptocurrency factors are tested as weekly quintile long-short portfolios, two exchange-pair strategies (cross and within) are built on bitcoin price discounts, and the resulting long-short portfolios are regressed against crypto-specific factor models, Fama-French 3- and 5-factor models, and gold/platinum, all split into pre-COVID and COVID subperiods. The comparison establishes that crypto returns are explained by crypto-specific factors, not by equity or commodity factors, and more so during the COVID stress period.

## Claim

The paper compares the explainability of cryptocurrency returns from macro and microeconomic risk factors across stressed (COVID-19) and normal market environments (Abstract). Cross-sectional analysis of the top-100 coins by market cap (as of August 10, 2020) shows that "risk-premiums are encapsulated within cryptocurrency-specific market factors in both stressed and normal market conditions"; Fama-French 5 factors provide low explainability in both regimes, and cryptocurrency factors "particularly relating to liquidity, momentum, and counterparty risk, showed evidence of providing stronger predictability of cryptocurrency returns during the COVID-19 pandemic compared to pre-pandemic levels" (Abstract). The evidence for liquidity/momentum/counterparty risk is the discount-based cross strategy: a daily long-short of bitcoin price-discount portfolios delivers a Sharpe ratio of 1.609 (Table 10), and the first two principal components of the cross portfolio returns explain 99% of their variance (Table 11), versus 94% reported for the same two components by Borri and Shakhnov in an earlier sample (footnote 16). The level factor (bitcoin returns) is significant at the 1% level in every portfolio and the slope factor (long-short cross returns) in the extreme portfolios (Table 16). Sample period: July 26, 2016 – August 10, 2020 (discount data November 1, 2019 – July 1, 2020); paper dated June 2021.

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument classes are spot crypto only: the top-100 coins by market cap and bitcoin across fiat-to-crypto exchange pairs (crypto-to-crypto exchanges are excluded as "mostly unregulated", §2.1). Every strategy has a short leg — the bottom quintile in the factor sorts and, for the cross long-short, a short bitcoin position the authors flag as "not supported across all platforms" (footnote 13); whether the short legs could be expressed in futures or perpetuals is not discussed. Long and short legs are reported separately: quintile tables give each quintile's mean return, and the cross/within tables give every portfolio individually. Leverage: the factor portfolios are zero-investment long-short; the cross strategy borrows USD at the risk-free rate; no margin leverage is specified. Returns are gross; the conclusion states "We did not account for transaction cost or bid ask spreads" (§5). Replication needs: daily open/high/close prices and volume for the top-100 coins from ~July 2016 (cryptocompy API), daily open prices and volumes for fiat-to-crypto pairs (cryptocompy), daily USD spot rates (pandas datareader), Fama-French factor returns and 1-month T-bill rates (pandas datareader), London fixing gold and platinum prices (Quandl). Universe membership is not point-in-time: the 100 coins are selected as of August 10, 2020 with weights held constant through the sample, and only 6 of the 100 had price data at the earliest date (July 26, 2016; Figure 3); survivorship is not addressed.

## Strategies

Common construction for the factor sorts (§3.1.2): weekly quintile portfolios formed from the 100 coins on each factor; the reported returns are "time-series averages of weekly value-weighted portfolio excess returns" (table footnotes), value weights being the constant market-cap weights. The long-short portfolio longs quintile 5 and shorts quintile 1. Three periods are compared: COVID (2019-11-01 to 2020-08-06), pre-COVID (2016-07-26 to 2019-11-01), full (2016-07-26 to 2020-08-06). The gap between signal date and trade date is not specified beyond weekly formation; turnover is not reported; no counterparty is identified. No economic rationale (risk-based or behavioural) is given for the individual factors beyond their role as risk-factor benchmarks.

### Factor quintile long-short portfolios (22 factors)

The signals are defined in Table 1, in four categories. Size: `prc` = log of last-day price in the formation week; `maxdprc` = maximum price of the formation week. Momentum: `r1, r2, r3, r4, r8, r16, r50, r100` = one-, two-, three-, four-, eight-, sixteen-, fifty-, hundred-week momentum. Volume: `vol` = log average daily volume in the formation week; `prc_vol` = log average daily volume times price. Volatility: `beta` and `idiovol` = coefficient and residual standard deviation from a regression estimated on daily returns of the previous 365 days before the formation week; `beta_squared`; `ret_vol`, `ret_skew`, `ret_kurt` = standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis of daily returns in the formation week; `max_ret` = maximum daily return of the formation week; `delay` = an autocorrelation-based measure (definition garbled in extraction, Table 1) estimated on the previous 365 days; `std_prc_vol` = log standard deviation of dollar volume in the formation week; `damihud` = average absolute daily return divided by dollar volume in the formation week. Value and size (market-cap) factors could not be constructed for lack of historical market-cap data (§3.1.3).

Results by category. Size: no monotonic pattern; all 5-1 spreads insignificant in all three periods (Tables 2.1–2.3). Momentum: in the full and pre-COVID periods all eight horizons are significant at the 1% level; in the COVID period only the 100-week horizon is insignificant, while r1 (0.352*** weekly), r2 (0.239***), r3 (0.210***), r4 (0.148***) and r16 (0.129***) are 1%-significant and r8 (0.101*) and r50 (0.066*) only 10%-significant (Table 3). Volume: long the highest dollar-volume coins, short the lowest, generates about 2.2% excess weekly return in the full and pre-COVID periods (prc_vol 5-1 = 0.022**, vol = 0.035** full, 0.041** pre-COVID); both are insignificant during COVID (Table 4; §3.1.2.3). Volatility: only `ret_skew` and `max_ret` produce significant long-short returns during COVID — spreads of 12.1% and 14.7% weekly (0.121*** and 0.147***, Table 5) — while most volatility factors are significant pre-COVID: beta 0.080**, ret_vol 0.072***, ret_skew 0.072***, max_ret 0.157***, std_prc_vol 0.026**, damihud −0.030**, idiovol −0.039* (Table A.2). The paper's summary list of the 15 factors "key in capturing the cross-section" is Table 13: all eight momentum horizons, vol, prc_vol, retvol, retskew, maxret, stdprcvol, damihud.

### Cross strategy (bitcoin discounts)

Bitcoin has significant, time-varying USD price differences ("discounts") across currencies and exchanges (§2.1). The discount for market m and currency j is defined as `d_{m,j,t} = P_{m,j,t} / P_{USD,t} − 1` (§3.2.1; rendered equation garbled, reconstructed from prose), with Coinbase USD as the benchmark numeraire. After cleaning — dropping pairs with more than 100 of 175 dates missing and pairs with mean daily volume below 1000 (§2.2) — 83 discounts remain; 13 (15.66%) are statistically insignificant in a 1-sample t-test and 17 (20.48%) non-stationary by ADF test, at 95% confidence (§3.2.1). At time t the investor borrows 1 USD at the risk-free rate, buys bitcoin at Coinbase, transfers it to pair (m,j), and at t+1 sells for currency j, converts to USD at the daily spot, and repays the loan; every exchange is against Coinbase USD. Returns: `r^{cross}_{m,j,t+1} = log(P_{m,j,t+1}) − log(P_{USD,t}) − r^f_t` (§3.2.2), with the 3-month US T-bill as risk-free rate. Seven equally weighted portfolios of 11 discounts each are formed by ranking mean discounts (max 0.3512, min −0.2688; Table 9), portfolio 1 = lowest, portfolio 7 = highest (assignments in Table A.10; 77 of the 83 pairs are assigned — the disposition of the remaining six is not stated). Portfolio mean returns are strictly increasing (−0.0106, 0.0016, 0.0025, 0.0040, 0.0065, 0.0212, 0.0515, daily) with roughly constant standard deviation, so Sharpe ratios rise to 0.8266 on portfolio 7; the long-short (long 7, short 1) earns 0.0622 daily with Sharpe 1.609 (Table 10). Portfolio 7 earned about 20% during the March 2020 shock (Figure 10). Practical issues acknowledged: some exchanges require existing account balances, bitcoin transfers can take up to 24 hours, and the long-short assumes shorting is available, which "is not supported across all platforms, albeit is becoming increasingly available" (footnote 13).

### Within strategy (bitcoin discounts)

Same portfolio assignment, but all trades occur within a single pair (m,j): borrow USD, buy bitcoin in pair (m,j), at t+1 sell for currency j, convert to USD at spot, close the loan. Returns: `r^{within}_{m,j,t+1} = log(P_{m,j,t+1}) − log(P_{m,j,t}) − r^f_t` (§3.2.2). Portfolio mean returns decrease across portfolios (0.0046, 0.0013, 0.0013, 0.0013, 0.0013, 0.0016, 0.0012) with consistent standard deviation; the long-short (here short 7, long 1, exploiting mean reversion) earns 0.0034 daily with Sharpe 0.0542 (Table 10). The paper describes both strategies as "not necessarily practical" and the within results as "not quite as enticing" (Table 10 discussion).

### What the comparison establishes

Regressing the significant long-short portfolios on a cryptocurrency CAPM gives negative adjusted R² (Table 6); adding a market momentum factor — long top 30% / short bottom 30% by three-week momentum — raises adjusted R² to 0.812, 0.887, 0.860, and 0.675 for the r2, r3, r4, and r16 portfolios (Table 15), with significant momentum betas. Fama-French 3- and 5-factor models explain almost nothing (adjusted R² between −0.026 and 0.036 for FF5, Table 14; Table 8.1; Figure 7), as do gold and platinum (adjusted R² between −0.010 and 0.041, Table 8.2), in all three subperiods. PCA on the cross portfolio returns gives two components explaining 90.32% and 8.85% of variance (Table 11), motivating a two-factor model of bitcoin returns (level) and long-short cross returns (slope); in that regression the level factor is significant at 1% in all portfolios, the slope factor at 1% for portfolio 7 and the long-short itself, 5% for portfolios 2 and 4, 10% for portfolio 5 (Table 16). The authors conclude cryptocurrencies' risks are "generally self-contained" (§5), and that the slope factor captures "bitcoin aggregate liquidity risk, momentum, and counterparty risks" per Borri and Shakhnov (§4).

## Test setup

Sample: July 26, 2016 – August 10, 2020 for the factor and market analysis (log returns studied "from 22/07/16 to 01/07/20", §3.1); November 1, 2019 – July 1, 2020 for the discount pairs; three subperiods as above. No out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported. Instruments: 100 cryptocurrencies (top-100 by market cap as of August 10, 2020; weights held constant) and 83 fiat-to-crypto pairs across 11 currencies (AUD, CAD, DKK, EUR, HKD, ILS, JPY, PLN, CHF, GBP, USD). Data: cryptocompy API (prices, volumes), pandas datareader (USD spot rates, Fama-French factors, 1-month T-bills), Quandl (London fixing gold and platinum). Survivorship and delisting are not addressed; the 100-coin set is selected with full hindsight. Transaction costs and financing costs are not modeled; headline numbers are gross. The benchmark numeraire for discounts is Coinbase USD. The risk-free rate used for the factor quintile analysis is not explicitly identified — 1-month T-bill rates are collected (§2.1) and 3-month T-bills are used for discount excess returns (§3.2.2).

The authors conclude that momentum — short horizons especially — and discount-based liquidity/counterparty risk are where crypto return predictability lives, that equity and commodity factors explain essentially nothing, and that this holds a fortiori during the COVID stress period (§4, §5).

## Robustness

The paper's robustness device is the three-period split (pre-COVID, COVID, full), applied to every factor sort and to the FF5 and commodity regressions (appendix Tables A.1–A.7, Figures A.1–A.4), with the COVID results confirming and strengthening the full-sample conclusions. The momentum horizon sweep r1–r100 tests specification sensitivity. PCA provides an independent confirmation of the two-factor structure, with the first two components explaining 99% versus 94% in Borri and Shakhnov's sample — reported as evidence the factors are "even stronger during the COVID-period" (footnote 16). No other robustness tests — alternative universes, delayed execution, alternative breakpoints, or other markets — are reported. The paper has no dedicated robustness section.

## Specification search

22 factors (Table 1) × 3 periods, with every quintile mean reported in the tables (Tables 2.1–2.3, 3, 4, 5; appendix A.1–A.3) rather than a selected subset; 8 momentum horizons; 83 discounts each tested for significance and stationarity; 7 discount portfolios × 2 strategies; 5 regression specifications (crypto CAPM, CAPM+momentum, FF3, FF5, gold/platinum) each over 3 periods where applicable. Results are reported for all of these, not only the significant ones.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§5): market capitalization data were not included due to lack of publicly available data; the crypto market did not mature until late 2016, so many coins were untraded or illiquid early in the sample; bitcoin was roughly 90% of the market until early 2017, creating a bias toward how the market works; transaction costs and bid-ask spreads were not accounted for and "may have a significant influence on the results"; more historical data would have allowed testing more factors. Suggested extensions: a market-cap-weighted market index and crypto CAPM, separating the market into small-, medium-, and large-cap portfolios.

## Separable variants for our pipeline

- Weekly quintile long-short on each of the 22 factors, defined in Table 1 — the momentum family (r1–r100) and the volatility factors retskew/maxret are the strong ones in-sample (§3.1.2).
- The cross strategy on bitcoin discounts, per-pair daily returns as defined in §3.2.2, portfolio assignment in Table A.10; the long-short of portfolio 7 minus portfolio 1 (§3.2.3).
- The within strategy, mean-reversion version of the same discount signal (§3.2.2).
- The two-factor structure (bitcoin returns as level, long-short cross returns as slope) as a pricing model for discount portfolios (§3.2.4, Table 16).
- The three-week market momentum factor (top 30% minus bottom 30%), used as the second factor in §3.1.3.2.
- Bitcoin discount levels themselves as a cross-sectional signal: portfolio 7 pairs (high positive discounts, listed in Table A.10) earned 0.0515 daily on average over November 2019 – July 2020 (Table 10).
