Category: **empirical** — a null-result study of whether Ethereum on-chain metrics and DeFi total value locked explain Ether (ETH) returns. The paper contains no strategy, no portfolio sort, and no backtest; its unit of analysis is a large set of time-series OLS regressions of ETH returns on up to four exogenous blockchain/social/TVL variables at a time (§3, §4).

## Claim

The research question is whether the market prices Ethereum's fundamentals as a digital platform (smart-contract creation, dApp activity, DeFi usage) in the dynamics of Ether, or whether Ether merely reflects general cryptocurrency-market sentiment (§1). The authors regress daily and weekly ETH/USD and ETH/BTC log returns on every feasible combination of up to 4 of 37 exogenous variables (excluding pairs with absolute correlation above 0.6), with and without BTC/USD returns as a regressor, and report the best models by adjusted R² (§3). Across more than 200,000 estimated regressions on a sample running 11/03/2018 to 15/09/2022 (the day of "The Merge", 1,413 daily points), on-chain metrics, social metrics, and DeFi TVL are "almost insignificantly correlated with Ether returns" (Abstract). Without BTC returns the maximum adjusted R² is 0.0557 (daily ETH/USD), 0.0707 (daily ETH/BTC), 0.0919 (weekly ETH/USD), 0.0997 (weekly ETH/BTC); adding BTC/USD returns raises adjusted R² to about 0.71 daily and 0.69 weekly (Tables 1–6). The BTC coefficient is consistently above 1 — 1.078 to 1.112 daily, 1.04 to 1.11 weekly — with an average of 1.09, which the authors read as ETH being perceived as riskier than Bitcoin (§4). The only variables significant on both frequencies and even for ETH/BTC returns are exchange trading volume (tvol) and daily active addresses (active_addr), with economically negligible coefficients (e.g., 1% higher daily volume associates with only 0.03%–0.034% higher ETH price, Table 1). TVL in DeFi is insignificant throughout (§5).

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument classes: a single spot asset (ETH) and BTC as regressor; no derivatives, no short leg anywhere, no portfolio construction, no leverage. The paper proposes nothing tradable — the significant variables (tvol, active_addr) are contemporaneous correlates, and the paper estimates no lagged-signal return. Long/short legs are not applicable; there is no strategy to express in futures or perpetuals. Replication would require: ETH and BTC prices and ETH trading volume from coingecko.com (with a one-time 1-day data shift after 30/01/2018, following Alexander and Dakos 2020, footnote 5); on-chain metrics from etherscan.io and the public Google BigQuery dataset `bigquery-publicdata.crypto_ethereum`; average transaction value, large (>100,000 USD) transaction count, and social-network/page-view metrics from the Cryptocompare.com API; TVL from defillama.com (§3). All sources are public; no proprietary data is used. Survivorship/delisting questions do not arise (single asset, still listed at sample end). No transaction costs or financing costs exist because nothing is traded.

## Strategies

There are no strategies. The following is the regression machinery, extracted completely because the exogenous variables are the closest thing to signals the paper contains.

**Dependent variables** — daily and weekly log returns of ETH/USD and ETH/BTC (the latter computed by direct division of ETH/USD by BTC/USD, to purge the common component, §3). **Regressors** — 37 exogenous variables (Table A.1): prices/volume (tvol); network usage (transactions, uni_addr = total distinct addresses, block_size, ave_gas_price, gas_used, active_addr, active_erc20_addr, ave_trans_fee_usd, hashrate, difficulty, trans_fee); token transfers (trans_erc20, trans_tk_erc20, trans_tk_nft, trans_tk_other); contracts (veri_contracts = verified daily, contracts_new = launched daily); Cryptocompare blockchain metrics (large_transaction_count_cc = count of >100,000 USD transactions per day, average_transaction_value_cc); TVL (tvl_eth, tvl_all); Cryptocompare social metrics (comments, posts, followers, points, per-tab page views: overview, analysis, markets, charts, trades, forum, influence, total; reddit_active_users, reddit_posts_per_day, reddit_comments_per_day). Twitter, Facebook, and Github data were collected but discarded for bad quality — not updated since 11/05/2018, 05/03/2020, and 09/09/2021 respectively (§3).

**Transformation** — all daily variables are log returns/differences $\ln(x_t / x_{t-1})$ (§3). Weekly: crypto prices and TVL become log returns between two subsequent Sundays; other metrics use either the log difference of the mean weekly value (block size, average gas price, active addresses, hashrate, difficulty, and, per footnote 9, TVL — inconsistent with the body text, see Extraction gaps) or of weekly sums (trading volume, transactions, unique addresses, gas used, total transaction fee, tokens transferred, new smart contracts, and all CC social metrics except reddit active users, footnote 8).

**Model** — $r_{eth} = \alpha + \sum_{k=1}^4 \beta_k x_k + \epsilon$, every feasible combination of up to 4 regressors, no BTC first, then BTC returns added to the pool; pairs of variables with $|\text{corr}| > 0.6$ are never combined; models sorted by $R^2$, three best analysed and five best reported; significance via robust errors (§3, Table notes). The stated rationale: more contracts/dApps and rising TVL imply growing demand for ETH as gas and collateral, which should raise ETH/USD; if all on-chain metrics are insignificant once BTC returns are included, ETH dynamics are "mainly associated with the dynamics of BTC," used as a proxy for whole-market sentiment (§3). The paper's own description of who sits on the other side of the trade is limited to this framing; there is no microstructure account.

## Test setup

Sample 11/03/2018 to 15/09/2022, chosen as the longest window for which TVL data exist (first observation 11/03/2018) ending at The Merge (§3). 1,413 daily points; 1,412 daily regression observations and 195 weekly observations (Table notes). No out-of-sample or post-publication period is reported; the sample ends on the publication-day's era-defining event and there is no hold-out. Data sources as in Fit; prices adjusted for the coingecko timestamp shift. Since nothing is traded there are no costs, financing, or benchmarks; all reported statistics are in-sample regression coefficients and adjusted R². The paper reports no standard errors or t-statistics, only stars; it reports no Sharpe ratios, returns, or alphas anywhere.

No strategy is favoured — there is none.

## Robustness

There is no dedicated robustness section. The robustness dimensions built into the design are: weekly in addition to daily frequency (motivated by the possibility that links to blockchain metrics "could be more explicitly observed on a weekly basis", §3); ETH/BTC as an alternative dependent variable to exclude the common component with BTC (Table 3, Table 6); and robust (heteroskedasticity-consistent) standard errors for coefficient significance (Table notes). No subperiod splits, alternative lags, alternative transformation windows, or delayed-execution checks are reported.

## Specification search

Reported model counts: 48,072 (daily ETH/USD without BTC), 6,753 additional (daily ETH/USD with BTC), 48,092 (daily ETH/BTC), 40,584 (weekly, cited in both the with-BTC and without-BTC weekly discussions); §5 summarises "more than 200,000 estimated linear regressions", while §3 states "approx. 15,000" — the paper's own counts are internally inconsistent. Only the five best models per block are tabulated, plus counts of models above the R² = 0.05 threshold; the full distribution of results is not reported. The authors explicitly acknowledge that selecting by R² "often results in 'data fishing' and the identification of spurious relationships," and argue their null result is the inverse scenario (§3).

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own: the linear econometric tool may be too simple — "it is possible to assume that the relationship between blockchain metrics and Ether price is more complex than linear model"; further research with more sophisticated statistical methods could prove the market prices Ethereum's fundamental characteristics; their findings suggest ETH price dynamics remain dominated by the speculative component inherent in the cryptocurrency market (§5). The bad quality of Twitter, Facebook, and Github data is noted as a data limitation (§3).

## Separable variants for our pipeline

Descriptive list of the exogenous variables that could be tested independently as predictors, each defined in Table A.1: tvol (exchange trading volume), active_addr (daily active addresses), large_transaction_count_cc (count of >100,000 USD transactions/day), average_transaction_value_cc, tvl_eth and tvl_all (DeFi total value locked, Ethereum-only and all blockchains), hashrate and difficulty, uni_addr (total distinct addresses), veri_contracts and contracts_new (verified/new smart contracts), trans_tk_nft (ERC-721 transfers), followers and reddit_posts_per_day (social interest), per-tab Cryptocompare page views. The paper tests all of these contemporaneously in log-difference form; it tests none lagged, so any lagged implementation would be a new hypothesis, not a variant present in the paper.

## Extraction gaps

- Figure 1 (page 6) is an image I cannot read; the caption states it plots Ether price in USD and BTC, and the text says the two series "are not that similar" (§3).
- The Table 5 table body is garbled in extraction: its header row overlaps with Table 4's content and contains an "Error! Reference source not found" placeholder; the btc/tvol/active_addr coefficients and R² values reported above come from the surrounding prose rather than the table cells.
- Table A.2 (descriptive statistics) has misaligned columns; min/mean/median/max/sd/skewness/kurtosis were recovered per variable, but rows for contracts_new, large_transaction_count_cc, and average_transaction_value_cc are merged and partly unrecoverable.
- In Table A.1 the description of reddit_comments_per_day reads "The number of times this repository had been starred as of that day", which appears to be a mislabelled row in the source document rather than an extraction error.
- Footnote 9 lists TVL among variables transformed as log differences of mean weekly values, while the body states TVL is transformed as log returns between Sundays — an internal inconsistency in the paper.
- No text addressed to automated systems or instructions to disregard extraction rules appears anywhere in the document.
