Category: **comparison** — seven trading strategy implementations (long-only, long-short, volatility-weighted, and market-timed) built on one shared underlying signal, the FactorVAE one-day-ahead cross-sectional return prediction, are evaluated head-to-head over the 2024 out-of-sample test period.

### Claim

The paper adapts FactorVAE (Duan et al., 2022), a variational-autoencoder latent factor model originally designed for equity markets, to cross-sectional one-day-ahead return prediction in cryptocurrency markets. The universe is 50 major cryptocurrencies traded on Binance with prices denominated in USDT; features are 158 technical indicators from the Qlib Alpha158 library (§3.1, §3.2). The sample spans January 2019–December 2024, split into training 2019-01-01 to 2022-12-31, validation 2023, and an out-of-sample test period of 2024 (§3.3). Seven strategies exploit the same daily model predictions. Six of seven achieve Sharpe ratios above 1.0; the Momentum Filter (Top-5 long, holding cash when Bitcoin's 20-day momentum is negative) is best, with a 269.8% return and a Sharpe ratio of 2.12 (Table 1). The paper also tests latent-factor stabilization via temporal regularization and reports that it produces more interpretable factor dynamics without sacrificing predictive accuracy (§5). No t-statistics or standard errors are reported anywhere in the paper; performance statistics are Sharpe ratios, total returns, final equity, and maximum drawdown (Table 1).

### Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is spot cryptocurrency on Binance, USDT-denominated. Five of the seven strategies (Top-5 Long, Top-10 Long, Vol-Weighted, Momentum Filter, Score-Weighted) are long-only with a cash option, and need no short leg; the Quintile Long-Short and Decile Long-Short strategies require a short leg (bottom quintile/decile) and in crypto that leg would need perpetuals or margin — the paper does not discuss how shorts are implemented. Long and short legs are never reported separately; only combined portfolio-level metrics are given (Table 1). Leverage is not described; backtests start from $1,000,000 in capital and report final equity. Returns are net of a transaction cost of 1 basis point per trade, applied in all strategy backtests (§3.4); no financing, borrow, or slippage costs are described. Data replication requires Binance OHLCV for 50 coins over 2019–2024 and the Qlib Alpha158 feature set; survivorship and delisting handling are not addressed. The model uses only its prior distribution at inference time, so no forward-looking leakage from the posterior is claimed (§2.2).

### Strategies

All seven strategies consume the same daily signal: the FactorVAE predicted mean return per asset. The model decomposes returns as $y_t = \alpha_t + \beta_t z_t + \varepsilon_t$ (Eq. 1), with all quantities parameterised by neural networks, $\hat{y}_t = f_\alpha(x_t) + f_\beta(x_t) \cdot f_z(x_t)$ (Eq. 2). Latent factors are Gaussian random variables: posterior $q(z|x,y) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\text{post}}, \text{diag}(\sigma_{\text{post}}^2))$ (Eq. 3) and prior $p(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\text{prior}}, \text{diag}(\sigma_{\text{prior}}^2))$ (Eq. 4); training minimises $\mathcal{L} = -\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(y|z,x)] + \gamma \cdot \text{KL}(q(z|x,y) \| p(z|x))$ (Eq. 5). Architecture: a GRU feature encoder compresses each asset's T-day feature history into an embedding $e_i = \text{GRU}(\{x_i^{(t)}\}_{t=1}^T)$ (Eq. 6); portfolio-level representations are formed by learned attention $p_j = \sum_{i=1}^N a_{ij} \cdot y_i$, $a_{ij} = \text{softmax}(W_p e_i)_j$ (Eq. 7); the decoder combines latent factors with asset components, $\mu_y^{(i)} = \mu_\alpha^{(i)} + \sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k^{(i)} \mu_z^{(k)}$ and $\sigma_y^{(i)} = \sqrt{(\sigma_\alpha^{(i)})^2 + \sum_{k=1}^K (\beta_k^{(i)})^2 (\sigma_z^{(k)})^2}$ (Eqs. 8–9). Configuration: 32 latent factors, hidden dimension 64, sequence length 20 days, 64 dynamically constructed portfolios, 20 training epochs with early stopping on validation loss (§3.4). The paper offers no risk-based or behavioural rationale for the predictability; it treats cross-sectional predictability as an empirical fact and does not identify who is on the other side of the trade. Rebalance is daily for all strategies (explicitly stated only for Top-5 Long, §4.2.1); execution timing beyond "each day" and the gap between signal formation and trade execution are not specified. Turnover is not reported for any strategy.

**Strategy 1: Top-5 Long (§4.2.1).** Equal-weighted portfolio of the five cryptocurrencies with the highest predicted returns each day. Concentrated exposure to the strongest signals. 106.9% return, Sharpe 1.24, max drawdown −53.9%, final equity $2,069,122 (Table 1).

**Strategy 2: Quintile Long-Short (§4.2.2).** Sorts assets into quintiles by predicted return; long the top quintile (highest 20%), short the bottom quintile (lowest 20%); described as market-neutral. 47.6% return, Sharpe 1.16, max drawdown −33.4%, final equity $1,475,707 (Table 1).

**Strategy 3: Top-10 Long (§4.2.3).** Equal-weighted portfolio of the ten highest-predicted-return assets; reduces concentration at the cost of diluting signal strength. 133.9% return, Sharpe 1.42, max drawdown −51.7%, final equity $2,338,648 (Table 1).

**Strategy 4: Decile Long-Short (§4.2.4).** Long the top decile (10%), short the bottom decile — a more aggressive market-neutral variant concentrating in the most extreme predictions. −0.6% return, Sharpe −0.07, max drawdown −26.3%, final equity $993,957 (Table 1).

**Strategy 5: Volatility-Weighted (§4.2.5).** Positions within the Top-5 selection weighted inversely to recent volatility, $w_i \propto \frac{1}{\sigma_i^{(20)}}$ (Eq. 10), where $\sigma_i^{(20)}$ is the 20-day rolling standard deviation. 123.8% return, Sharpe 1.37, max drawdown −52.7%, final equity $2,237,733 (Table 1).

**Strategy 6: Momentum Filter (§4.2.6).** The Top-5 Long strategy modified to hold cash when Bitcoin's 20-day momentum is negative — a market-timing overlay. 269.8% return, Sharpe 2.12, max drawdown −36.2%, final equity $3,697,680 (Table 1).

**Strategy 7: Score-Weighted (§4.2.7).** Weights within the Top-5 proportional to prediction scores rather than equal, $w_i \propto \mu_i - \min_j \mu_j + \epsilon$ (Eq. 11), letting model confidence drive position sizing. 84.5% return, Sharpe 1.07, max drawdown −41.6%, final equity $1,844,908 (Table 1).

The comparison establishes that the long-only family dominates the long-short family on return and Sharpe, that the aggressive Decile L/S variant destroys value (−0.07 Sharpe) while the Quintile L/S offers the best drawdown protection among non-timed approaches (−33.4%), that diversification (Top-10 vs Top-5) improves both return and Sharpe, and that the Bitcoin-momentum cash overlay is the dominant implementation (§4.4).

### Test setup

Sample period is January 2019–December 2024, split by calendar year: training 2019-01-01 to 2022-12-31, validation 2023, test 2024 (§3.3). The 2024 test period is the only period for which strategy performance is reported, and it is described as out-of-sample; no post-publication period exists. Number of instruments: 50 (§3.1). Data sources: Binance OHLCV (prices in USDT), transformed to 158 Qlib Alpha158 technical indicators covering momentum at 5/10/20/30/60-day horizons, volatility measures and price ranges, volume patterns and price-volume correlations, and mean-reversion/trend indicators; features are cross-sectionally standardised within each date (§3.2). Survivorship and delisting are not addressed. Costs: 1 basis point transaction cost per trade applied in all backtests (§3.4); headline figures are therefore net of this cost only, with no financing, borrow, or slippage costs and no leverage. No benchmark index or buy-and-hold comparison is reported. All seven strategies share this setup.

The authors' conclusions from Table 1 (§4.4): the Momentum Filter dominates, avoiding exposure during adverse conditions via the Bitcoin trend filter and producing one of the lowest drawdowns (−36.2%); long-only strategies deliver strong risk-adjusted returns with Top-10 Long the best unfiltered performer (133.9%, Sharpe 1.42); market neutrality carries trade-offs — Quintile L/S gives the best drawdown protection among directional approaches (−33.4%) but the more aggressive Decile L/S underperforms, with extreme-quantile selection introducing noise; and diversification from Top-5 to Top-10 improves both return (106.9% → 133.9%) and Sharpe (1.24 → 1.42). The paper's overall favour: the Momentum Filter, on both return and risk-adjusted grounds.

### Robustness

There is no strategy-level robustness section: no subperiod splits, alternative parameters, alternative universes, delayed-execution tests, or additional markets are reported for the seven strategies. The only robustness-style analysis is Section 5 on latent factor stabilization: temporal regularization adds $\mathcal{L}_{\text{stab}} = \mathcal{L} + \lambda_1 \cdot \text{KL}(q_t \| q_{t-1}) + \lambda_2 \cdot \| \beta_t - \beta_{t-1} \|_2^2$ (Eq. 12) to the training loss. The paper reports that stabilization makes latent factors z capture slow-moving market-wide conditions, loadings β adapt rapidly to asset-specific changes, and idiosyncratic alpha absorbs remaining variation (§5.3), and that adjacent-day factor correlation and L2 distance between successive distributions confirm high temporal consistency "while preserving predictive accuracy" (§5.4) — but no numerical accuracy comparison between stabilized and unstabilized models is given in the text, and strategy performance under the stabilized model is not reported separately from the main results.

### Specification search

The paper reports one model configuration (32 latent factors, hidden dim 64, 20-day sequence, 64 portfolios, 20 epochs, early stopping — §3.4) and exactly seven strategy implementations, with results reported for all seven (Table 1). It does not state how many configurations, hyperparameter settings, or strategy variants were examined but not reported. RankIC (daily Spearman correlation between predicted and realized returns) is described only qualitatively as "positive throughout the test period" (§4.1); no numeric values are given in the text.

### Limitations and future work (authors')

The authors list, from §6: expanding the asset universe and historical coverage to test robustness across market regimes; incorporating alternative data sources including on-chain metrics, social sentiment, and funding rates, potentially with language models for text signals; graph-based architectures to capture interconnectedness; regime-switching (HireVAE) and spatio-temporal (STORM) models to adapt to distinct market states; and discrete latent representations via vector quantization (FactorVQVAE) for more interpretable factors.
