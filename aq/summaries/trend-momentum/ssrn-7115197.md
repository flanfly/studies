*Category: comparison.* Six machine-learning long–short strategies (three sequence-based, three image-based) are evaluated against each other and against three simple benchmarks under a common portfolio protocol. The paper is a comparison horse race whose headline result is that tradability survival is narrow and selective rather than broadband.

## Claim

The paper asks whether cross-sectional machine-learning return predictability in cryptocurrency markets survives into net-of-cost long–short performance. Using daily OHLCV data from February 1, 2018 to December 15, 2024, five rolling out-of-sample study periods, and six deep-learning architectures restricted to a fixed OHLCV information set, it finds that model scores contain weak but statistically detectable ranking information, especially at short and intermediate horizons, but that strict benchmark-adjusted economic survival is concentrated at the 7-day horizon, mainly in LSTM and GRU. At 14 days, GRU gives the strongest descriptive pooled case within the model set but does not survive the benchmark-adjusted criterion; at 28 days no model produces pooled or benchmark-adjusted economic survival. The mechanism offered is a "predictability-tradability gap": weak, regime-dependent, benchmark-sensitive signals that are difficult to monetize once turnover, costs, timing, compounding, and benchmark competition are imposed. The 7-day survival pockets are stronger in down-market, high-dispersion, and high-volatility states. Preprint, not peer reviewed.

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is cryptocurrency quoted via the CryptoCompare CCCAGG volume-weighted index (spot synthesis). Every strategy is a long–short spread ("buys the top k assets and shorts the bottom k assets") with a genuine short leg; the paper does not describe a shorting mechanism, borrow cost, funding cost, or liquidation rule, and explicitly lists borrow constraints and funding costs among what it does not model — so shorting here is a cost-free accounting construct. The paper reports pooled net Sharpe regardless of sign of the short leg, not separate long-leg and short-leg returns; it reports only **selection-retention** of long and short buckets, not their separate contributions (Table B.1). No leverage is used: equal notional within each leg, self-financing book. Headline numbers are net of proportional transaction costs at 15 bps per half-turn; gross-of-cost values appear only in the cost-sensitivity table. Replication would need CryptoCompare CCCAGG OHLCV + market cap data (the exact data feeds the paper relies on) and the model training pipeline; processed tables and replication code are available from the authors on request subject to provider restrictions.

## Strategies

All six models share one pipeline, so it is described once here and only the per-model score differs. For every signal date `t` and horizon `h`, assets in the tradable cross-section C(t) are ranked by the scalar score `s = p̂(up) − p̂(down)` from a three-class directional classifier whose label uses a neutral band η = 0.005 around zero:
- +1 if $R_{t,h}^c ≥ \eta$, 0 if $|R_{t,h}^c| < \eta$, −1 if $R_{t,h}^c ≤ −\eta$, with $R_{t,h}^c = \prod_{j=1}^h (1+r_{t+j}^c) − 1$ (§2.2).
The portfolio buys the top `k` and shorts the bottom `k` assets, `k = 5` being the representative breadth. Formation is horizon-matched: a new tranche is opened each trading day and held `h` days, so `h` overlapping tranches are active in steady state. A baseline one-day implementation lag (δ=1) is applied; δ=0 is reported only as an execution-timing proxy. Positions are fixed at entry, not rebalanced to maturity; within-tranche weights drift with prices. Gross return is position-level mark-to-market PnL divided by equity. One-way turnover is `TO = ½∑_c |w − w̃|` (trade-induced only; passive drift is not counted), costs are `TC = λ·TO` with λ = 15 bps per half-turn, and the zero-lag/15bps values in Table 4 and 5 are actually average lag-1 Sharpe under alternate λ. No turnover magnitude is reported anywhere; the diagnostics most adjacent to it are rank autocorrelation ("Rank AC") and one-day selection retention (Table B.1). Path-stability safeguards: sleeve returns winsorized at ±25%, portfolio daily returns clipped to [−0.99, 0.99], portfolios whose net equity falls below 10⁻⁶ treated as failed (failed NAVs retained in family averages).

**Sequence-based family.** Inputs are five channels — one-day percentage changes of open/high/low/close and the one-day log-difference of volume ($\ell V_t^c=\log(1+V_t^c)$, $\Delta\ell V_t^c=\ell V_t^c-\ell V_{t-1}^c$) — over a 30-day lookback, standardized with training-window statistics only (§2.2). All sequence models use Adam, LR 10⁻⁴, batch 64, max 100 epochs, early stopping, gradient clipping norm 1.0 (Appendix A.2).

- **LSTM** — single-layer LSTM, hidden size 64, MLP head 64→32→3 (ReLU). At 7-day: IC 0.050, pooled net Sharpe 0.981 — the best economic case in the paper. At 14-day: IC 0.033, pooled Sharpe −0.008. At 28-day: IC −0.006, Sharpe −0.926 (Table 2). Score–momentum correlation −0.185 at 7-day; NW t-stat 6.24 for the 7-day IC (Table C.3). Survives strict (IC>0, Sharpe>0.50, gap≥0) at 7-day (Table 3).
- **GRU** — single-layer GRU, otherwise identical topology. At 7-day: IC 0.058, pooled Sharpe 0.615. At 14-day: IC 0.042, pooled Sharpe 0.731 (highest descriptive Sharpe in the model set at 14-day, but does **not** beat the momentum benchmark). At 28-day: IC 0.009, Sharpe −0.167 (28-day IC NW t-stat 0.55, not distinguishable from zero). Score–momentum correlation −0.155 at 7-day and −0.031 at 14-day. Survives strict survival at 7-day (Table 3).
- **Transformer** — input projection 5→64, 4-head self-attention, feedforward width 64, dropout 0.15, mean pooling, 3-class head. Has the highest mean IC at 7-, 14-, and 28-day horizons (0.060, 0.067, 0.041) yet negative or near-zero pooled net Sharpe (0.129, −0.351, −0.325) (Table 2). Shows isolated positive pockets at 28-day in low-dispersion/low-volatility states only (§3.5). This is the paper's clearest demonstration that IC does not map to tradability.

**Image-based family.** The same 30-day OHLCV window is rendered as a fixed candlestick-and-volume chart (generation rule fixed across assets, dates, study periods, and architectures), resized to 224×224, fed to an ImageNet-pretrained backbone fine-tuned end-to-end with a 3-class head; optimizer Adam, LR 5×10⁻⁵, batch 16, max 100 epochs, no stochastic augmentation (Appendix A.2). The family consistently underperforms the sequence family.

- **ResNet** — ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50. ICs 0.023/0.015/0.007; pooled net Sharpe −1.168/−1.070/−1.101. Always negative.
- **DenseNet** — ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet-121. ICs 0.040/0.031/0.010; Sharpe −0.378/0.274/−0.823.
- **ViT** — pretrained ViT-B/16. ICs 0.021/0.016/0.004; Sharpe −0.614/0.150/−0.913.

The comparison establishes that statistical survival (positive mean IC, 6/6 models at 7- and 14-day, 5/6 at 28-day) is far broader than economic survival (positive pooled Sharpe: 3/6 at 7-day, 3/6 at 14-day, 0/6 at 28-day) and that benchmark-adjusted survival is narrower still (3/6 at 7-day, 0/6 at 14- and 28-day) (Table 3). The three benchmarks, under the identical trading engine, lag-1, and 15 bps: momentum Sharpe 0.019/1.037/1.162 at 7/14/28-day; random ranking −0.914/−0.652/−0.571; reversal −0.080/−0.906/−1.487 (Table 6). Momentum is the binding hurdle at 14- and 28-day, while at 7-day the hurdle is low and the best ML cases clear it.

## Test setup

Sample period Feb 1, 2018 to Dec 15, 2024, split into five rolling study periods of 1,350 trading days each (train 810 / validation 270 / test 270); adjacent periods overlap in estimation but produce non-overlapping test windows, which are concatenated for pooled results (Table 1). Universe: top 100 coins by market cap on the first day of each training window, held fixed within the study period (point-in-time construction); 64 assets excluded by two filters — 11 stablecoins/stable-value tokens and 53 with insufficient continuous OHLCV history (Table A.1). Assets with missing/non-finite inputs are dropped from C(t) date-wise. Data source named: CryptoCompare CCCAGG. Survivorship/delisting handling is not described beyond the fixed-universe and failed-path conventions. Headline numbers are net of 15 bps per half-turn proportional costs with a one-day lag; costs at 0 and 30 bps are robustness. Leverage none; benchmark is horizon-matched momentum (plus reversal and random ranking). Annualized Sharpe/vol use the √365 convention. Each model is fit separately per study period and horizon. Note that the headline Sharpe tables report pooled net Sharpe, while Tables 4/5/6 report study-period **averages** of Sharpe; the paper flags that the appropriate comparator for benchmarks is the lag-1 average, not the pooled figure.

The 1-day horizon is omitted throughout the body as a "high-turnover reference case," not a tradability claim. Maximum drawdown and terminal NAV figures are reported prose-wise for family-level paths (Table D.2, below) but not as a per-model drawdown table. No gross-of-cost pooled Sharpe is tabulated separately; gross values surface only through the cost-sensitivity averages (Table 4: at 0 bps, average net Sharpe 0.708 at 7-day, 0.282 at 14-day, 0.006 at 28-day; at 15 bps, 0.140/−0.009/−0.142; at 30 bps, −0.383/−0.306/−0.306). Table 4's message: removing costs helps but does not create broadband tradability at 14- and 28-day.

Execution-timing check (Table 5, average Sharpe lag0/lag1): at 7-day GRU 0.624/1.214, LSTM 0.665/1.076, Transformer 1.171/1.070; at 14-day GRU 0.546/0.949, Transformer 0.716/0.192; at 28-day GRU −0.440/0.565, Transformer 0.879/0.579. The lag can reorder strategies rather than uniformly subtract value.

The authors conclude that benchmark competition is the decisive economic filter: the 14-day GRU case is the strongest model-level pooled case but loses to horizon-matched momentum (which reaches Sharpe 1.037 at 14-day and 1.162 at 28-day), and no model beats momentum at longer horizons. Within the ML set, LSTM then GRU at 7-day are the only survivors of the strict criterion (IC>0, Sharpe>0.50, nonnegative benchmark gap) (Table 3). Family-level pooled NAV (Figure 1, Table D.2) shows the sequence family's 7-day survival is robust to median aggregation (mean NAV 2.535, median 1.325 vs. momentum 0.406), but the 14-day sequence-family mean NAV 1.106 weakens to a median 0.535 (momentum 5.243), and both families fail at 28-day under mean and median aggregation.

## Robustness

Transaction-cost robustness at 0/15/30 bps (Table 4, Appendix C.1), showing costs matter but are not the sole bottleneck. Execution timing lag0 vs lag1 (Table 5). Regime conditioning by market direction (sign of cross-sectional mean one-day return), cross-sectional dispersion, 30-day rolling market volatility, and momentum strength (Table D.1, §3.5): 7-day LSTM Sharpe 0.476 up-market vs 1.409 down-market; GRU 0.213 vs 1.119, with similar patterns under high dispersion and high volatility. Regime splits do not overturn the benchmark results; 14- and 28-day cases remain benchmark-sensitive. Family aggregation switches mean vs median (Table D.2). Inference: Newey–West IC t-stats (lag h−1) confirm detectability at short/intermediate horizons (7-day LSTM 6.24, GRU 6.68, Transformer 6.37; 14-day GRU 3.86, Transformer 4.88; 28-day GRU 0.55 insignificant, Transformer 2.10) (Table C.3). Paired moving-block bootstrap (block length h) for Sharpe differences finds no comparison statistically significant — LSTM−GRU 7-day ΔSharpe 0.366 (CI [−0.246, 1.220], p 0.262), GRU−Momentum 14-day −0.376 (p 0.572), Transformer−Momentum 28-day −1.436 (p 0.324) (Table C.4). The paper therefore declines to claim architecture dominance on any check.

## Specification search

The paper reports results for 6 models × 4 horizons (1-day body-omitted), one portfolio breadth k=5 in the main text with "available portfolio-breadth settings" referenced but their values not reported in the extracted text, cost levels 0/15/30 bps, and lag 0/1. Full model-level diagnostics for all six models at 7/14/28-day appear in Appendix B.1; selected cases only are shown in the main text. It reports selected results, not every cell of every run.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own: the design is conditional on a fixed daily OHLCV information set, a fixed ex ante universe, representative deep-learning architectures, and a stylized long–short engine with proportional costs; it omits richer predictors (order book, derivatives, on-chain, funding rates, sentiment) and does not model exchange liquidation rules, borrow constraints, market impact, funding costs, or full liquidity/capacity limits. They flag a possible mismatch between ImageNet-pretrained visual backbones and sparse 30-day financial charts as a candidate reason for the weaker image-family results. Future directions named: richer predictors, cost-aware or decision-aware learning objectives, regime-conditional deployment rules, dynamic universe selection, and more detailed execution and liquidity models.

## Separable variants for our pipeline

The six models are already independent signal specifications of a common protocol (definitions §2.2, Appendix A.2), and each could be tested alone; the same is true of the three benchmarks (§2.4). Because all six share the identical portfolio engine, the truly separable differentiators here are (a) the sequence-family ranking score vs. the image-family ranking score, (b) horizon choice h ∈ {7, 14, 28}, and (c) the neutral band η = 0.005 and lookback 30 days as free parameters in the classifier. No portfolio-side design parameter beyond k and the lag is varied independently in the paper.
