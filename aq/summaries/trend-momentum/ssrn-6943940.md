Single-strategy paper with strong methodology content: the unit of interest is the *Dynamic ART-DRL* system, a hierarchical deep reinforcement learning (H-DRL) wrapper around one underlying signal, the Kalman-denoised SMA crossover swing, on two commodity ETFs. The paper's stated novelty is architectural (H-DRL "structural regularization"), not a new optimization algorithm (§1).

## Claim

The authors claim that standalone continuous DRL policies (PPO, DDPG, A2C) fail under realistic frictions — a "Friction-Regime-Leverage Paradox": transaction-cost-induced performance collapse from whipsawing, and margin liquidation under 5.0x leverage when volatility regimes shift (§1, Abstract). Their fix is a five-stage pipeline: (1) a recursive 2D Kalman filter denoises price; (2) a Variable Selection Network with a Transformer encoder gates features; (3) a rolling-volatility router deterministically binds one of four pre-trained specialists (DQN/PPO/DDPG/A2C) and its SMA-crossover configuration; (4) the bound specialist acts as a neural meta-policy scaling exposure; (5) active overlays set volatility-scaled leverage, regime-specific stops, and inverse-volatility capital weights (§3). Headline result: over out-of-sample 2018 – May 29, 2026 on daily GLD and USO data, the H-DRL Sliding WFA portfolio earns **+3,466.19%** cumulative (ann. +53.02%), Sharpe **1.043**, max drawdown **-42.64%**, 251 trades (29.9 trades/yr), while standalone continuous DRL loses **-30.11%** and static leveraged buy-and-hold (GLD 5x, USO 3x) is wiped out **-100.00%** (Tables 3, 4). No t-statistic is reported for the headline Sharpe; the paired t-test on daily returns gives t = 2.102 (p = 0.0357) (§5.4.1).

## Fit with our constraints

- **Instrument classes.** Two commodity ETFs only: GLD (gold) and USO (crude oil), daily bars (the abstract mentions hourly data; all reported backtests are daily — 2,118 daily out-of-sample observations, §A.1). No stocks, no options.
- **Short leg.** None required. The low-level signal is binary long/flat: S_t ∈ {0, 1} (§3.4.1), so the strategy is long-only with leverage; no short leg is reported or needed. Long and short legs are therefore not separately reported — there is no short leg.
- **Leverage.** Heavy: L_max = 5.0 (Eq. 19), applied at 5x (GLD) and 3x (USO) in the swing suites; the headline WFA portfolio also trades under these constraints. Leverage is scaled down by volatility (Op A), so average realized leverage is not reported.
- **Replication.** Needs daily OHLCV for GLD and USO (data vendor not named), fees 5 bps buy / 15 bps sell, and the four frozen DRL agents; source code is promised at github.com/prtk2001/dynamic-art-drl (§6) but no code is in the paper.

## Strategies

### H-DRL Swing Execution (Dynamic ART-DRL)

**Signal.** The core directional signal is a slow SMA crossover computed on *Kalman-denoised* price. Prices are filtered with a 2D constant-velocity state-space Kalman model, x_t = [p_t, ṗ_t]^T, with F = [[1,1],[0,1]], H = [1,0] (Eqs. 1–3); process and measurement noise covariances are scaled by rolling ATR: Q_t = Q_base × (1 + γ·ATR_t/Price_t), R_t = R_base × (1 + δ·ATR_t/Price_t)^{-1} (Eqs. 4–5). The values of Q_base, R_base, γ, and δ are not given. The crossover produces a binary signal S_t ∈ {0,1} (§3.4.1). The regime router (Eqs. 12–14) binds agent-and-window: LOW → DQN with SMA 15/80 (GLD) or 10/40 (USO); NORMAL → PPO, same windows; HIGH → DDPG with SMA 10/80 (GLD) or 10/40 (USO); EXTREME → A2C, out of market (cash). Volatility is the rolling 30-period standardized return volatility σ_t; thresholds are θ_1 = μ_σ − 1σ_σ, θ_2 = μ_σ + 1σ_σ, θ_3 = μ_σ + 2σ_σ computed from the rolling 252-period mean and std of volatility (Eqs. 12–13), on strictly lagged data {p_1, …, p_{t−1}} (§3.4.1).
**Meta-policy.** The active specialist outputs a_t ∈ [−1,1] mapped to an exposure multiplier α_t = (a_t + 1.0)/2.0 ∈ [0,1]; DQN outputs an index i ∈ {0,…,4} with α_t = 0.25·i (Eqs. 16–17). Final exposure: E_t = S_t × α_t × L_t (Eq. 18). Reward: r_t = ΔV_t − λ_1·DD_t − λ_2·|Δa_t|·c + λ_3·SR_rolling (Eq. 15); the λ weights are not given.
**Risk overlays.** Leverage L_t = min(L_max, L_max × σ_target/σ_t) with L_max = 5.0, σ_target = 0.006 daily (0.002 hourly) for gold (Eq. 19); stops θ_t = 0.015 (DQN), 0.020 (A2C), 0.030 (DDPG), 0.060 (PPO) (Eq. 20); joint capital W_GLD = σ_USO/(σ_GLD + σ_USO), W_USO = σ_GLD/(σ_GLD + σ_USO) (Eq. 21).
**Training and re-calibration.** All four agents are pre-trained once on the initial 252-day window (2017), 200,000 environment steps, then frozen; only FAST/SLOW windows are re-optimized at each walk-forward step by in-sample grid search over FAST ∈ {2,3,5,8,10,15}, SLOW ∈ {20,30,40,50,60,80,100,120}, picking the in-sample Sharpe-maximizing set under 20 bps fees (§3.4.1, §5.3). Hyperparameters per agent are locked in Table 10 (e.g., PPO lr 3×10^{-4}, batch 64, 10 epochs, clip 0.2, [64,64] net; DDPG [256,256] nets, OU noise θ=0.15, σ=0.2; DQN linear exploration decay 1.0→0.05).
**Economics.** The authors frame the alpha source as "leveraged trend-following on Kalman-denoised price series" with the DRL pool providing risk-scaling only — "the core directional signal is statistical, not neural" (§5.8). Who is on the other side is not discussed. Universe is the two ETFs themselves; no screens, no microcap/penny issues, no point-in-time reconstruction concerns beyond the walk-forward protocol described. Execution timing between signal date and trade date is not specified. Turnover is 29.9 trades/yr (joint, H-DRL sliding WFA; Table 4).

## Test setup

Training window: calendar 2017 (252 days); out-of-sample: 2018 – May 29, 2026, N = 2,118 daily observations (§A.1). Two instruments (GLD, USO). Data source not named; survivorship/delisting not addressed (two ETFs, likely moot). Results are **net of fees**: transaction costs 5 bps buys, 15 bps sells (20 bps roundtrip) (Table 1 note); no financing, borrow, or interest-on-leverage cost is described. Benchmarks: 1x 50/50 buy-and-hold (+558.89% cum., Sharpe 0.623; Table 3; +590.14%, 0.613 in Table 9), leveraged buy-and-hold (GLD 5x / USO 3x, −100.00%), 12-month momentum (5x/3x), monthly rebalanced (5x/3x), plain SMA(10,50) without Kalman (Table 9). Cost model identical across strategies within each table; leverage constraints identical where stated.

## Robustness

Extensive: component ablation (Table 5 — removing Kalman: +1,284.15%, Sharpe 0.582; removing VSN: +1,912.44%, 0.741; removing router: +1,142.18%, 0.518; removing risk overlay: −100.00%); single-agent vs. routed (Table 6 — best single PPO-only +1,984.12%, Sharpe 0.732; random routing +452.18%, 0.284); all 4! = 24 regime-to-agent permutations in direct exposure mode (Table 7 — the proposed mapping is rank 4 by raw return; ranks 1–3 beat it on raw return in that mode, and the paper defends its choice structurally); grid-search bias via Hansen SPA, p = 0.0300 (§5.4.3) though the appendix restates the threshold as p < 0.01 (§A.3); stationary block bootstrap (mean block length 10 days, B = 1,000): Sharpe CI [0.438, 1.648] (§5.4.1, §A.1); Monte Carlo trade shuffling (10,000 shuffles in §A.2, "1,000 times" in §5.4.2): actual +3,466.19% vs shuffled mean +1,156.78%, p = 0.0020; fee sensitivity 10–50 bps (Sharpe stays ≥ 1.214 at 50 bps; Table 8); 20-seed policy sweep: H-DRL mean +10,241% (std 1,098%) vs no-router +7,643% (std 2,988%), a 3.6x variance reduction (§5.7); a 2026 isolated stale-vs-fresh calibration test (GLD: +36.54% stale vs +112.15% fresh; USO: +146.38% both) (§4.7). Delayed-execution and out-of-market-excluding-GLD/USO tests are not reported.

## Specification search

The paper reports: 48 FAST×SLOW grid points re-swept each WFA step (§5.3, §A.3); 24 routing permutations (only top-3, proposed, and bottom-3 shown) (Table 7); 6 denoise thresholds (Table 2); 4 fee levels (Table 8); 20 seeds (§5.7). All 48 grid points enter the SPA test; other sweeps report selected results only.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own (§5.8): results cover two commodity ETFs in one macro regime (2017–2026 gold bull market, oil crash-and-recovery), so cross-asset/regime generalizability is open; alpha is leveraged trend-following, not neural; DRL weights are frozen, with online refinement a natural extension; reported returns reflect leveraged compounding, with Sharpe as the leverage-neutral measure. Future work: learn the routing policy itself end-to-end via hierarchical RL instead of pre-defined volatility thresholds (§6).

## Separable variants for our pipeline

- **Kalman-denoised SMA crossover swing** (SMA 15/80 GLD, 10/40 USO, binary long/flat) — the standalone low-level signal, Eqs. 1–8, §3.4.1; the plain SMA(10,50) no-Kalman variant is benchmarked in Table 9.
- **Volatility-scaled leverage** (Op A, Eq. 19) as a standalone overlay with L_max = 5.0, σ_target = 0.006 daily.
- **Regime-specific stop-losses** (Op B, Eq. 20): stops 0.015/0.020/0.030/0.060 bound to regime class.
- **Inverse-volatility 50/50-style capital allocation** (Op C, Eq. 21) across two assets.
- **Action-denoising rebalance threshold** on a continuous policy (θ_rebal; Table 2 shows 15% optimal, 0.438 Sharpe vs 0.348 at zero) — §4.3.
- **Volatility regime classifier** (Eqs. 12–13) as a stand-alone conditioning variable, thresholds at μ ± 1σ, μ + 2σ of rolling 252-day volatility.
- **The exposure-scaling meta-policy** E_t = S_t × α_t × L_t (Eq. 18) with linear rescaling α = (a+1)/2 — separable from the neural pool as a static α rule.
