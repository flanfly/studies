Category: **single-strategy** — one signal (*RetDiff*) developed and tested as a unit, then decomposed into its two components (pairs momentum and short-term reversal), each of which the paper sorts on separately, and compared against industry momentum as an explanatory factor.

## Claim

The paper proposes an equity pairs trading strategy built on historical return correlations and shows it earns large abnormal returns that survive common factor models. For each stock, the previous five years of monthly returns identify 50 highest-correlation "pair" stocks; the signal is the divergence between the pairs portfolio return and the stock's own lagged return: $$RetDiff = beta^C \cdot (Cret-Rf) - (Lret - Rf)$$ (§2.1), where *Cret* is the previous month's equal-weighted return of the 50 pair stocks, *Lret* is the firm's own previous-month return, and *beta^C* is the slope of the firm's monthly return on its pairs portfolio return over years *t-4* to *t*. Decile 10 – Decile 1 portfolios sorted on lagged *RetDiff* earn 1.40%/month (*t* = 8.81) value-weighted and 3.59%/month (*t* = 12.57) equal-weighted over January 1931 – December 2007, with six-factor (market, size, book-to-market, momentum, short-term reversal, liquidity) alphas of 0.77% (*t* = 3.75) and 3.03% (*t* = 13.35) (Table 1). The profit decomposes into a short-term-reversal component (*Lret*) and a "pairs momentum" component (*Cret*); the pairs momentum is largely explained by the **one-month** version of industry momentum, while the conventional six-month industry momentum (Moskowitz and Grinblatt 1999) does not subsume it (Tables 9–11). The paper concludes pairs trading profits are "largely explained by the short term reversal and a version of the industry momentum" (§5).

## Fit with our constraints

Instruments are single-name US common equities from CRSP; nothing is expressible in futures or perpetuals as constructed. The strategy requires a short leg: the self-financing Decile 10 – Decile 1 portfolio shorts the low-*RetDiff* decile of stocks (short stock; borrow cost never discussed). Long and short legs are reported separately — Table 1 gives all ten deciles, so the short-leg (Decile 1) raw returns are visible: 0.45%/month VW and 0.00%/month EW, versus 1.86% and 3.59% for Decile 10; the EW spread is almost entirely a long-leg effect while the VW spread is split roughly 1.4:0.45. No leverage is used (portfolios are self-financing). Replication needs monthly returns for the full CRSP cross-section from 1926 (to have five years of history by 1931); pair membership is reconstructed from trailing five-year correlations, so it is point-in-time feasible, but the paper does not describe any delisting handling. All returns are gross; no transaction cost, borrow, or capacity model is described anywhere.

## Strategies

### Pairs trading on *RetDiff* (the paper's strategy)

**Signal.** For each stock *i* in year *t+1*, compute Pearson correlations of *i*'s monthly returns with all other CRSP stocks using data from January of year *t-4* to December of year *t*; the 50 highest-correlation stocks form the pairs portfolio, whose equal-weighted monthly return is *Cret* (§2.1). Robustness checks use 10 and 20 pair stocks "and the empirical inferences are similar" (footnote 1). *RetDiff* = *beta^C*\*(*Cret-Rf*) − (*Lret−Rf*), with *beta^C* from the firm's monthly return regressed on the pairs portfolio return over the same five-year window; a simple alternative *Cret−Lret* gives "similar results, with comparable magnitude" (footnote 2).

**Rationale and counterparty.** Two channels are offered (§3.3): information delay — price divergence between a firm and its peers reflects news that takes time to disseminate — and short-term liquidity provision, in which the profits are "compensation for market makers who buy the shares of a particular stock when there is liquidity shock." The authors frame the mechanism as under-reaction (pairs momentum) and over-reaction (reversal) rather than risk: profits "do not persist beyond the first month," which they argue makes a fundamental risk-based explanation unlikely (§3.2).

**Universe, weighting, timing.** Universe is all CRSP firms with 60 monthly returns in years *t-4* to *t* (§2.1); microcaps and penny stocks are included in the main test — screens for price > $5 and size > 10% of the NYSE cutoff appear only in Appendix Table A.2 (content not extracted). Each month, ten portfolios are formed on the *previous month's* *RetDiff* and held one month; only stock *i* enters the portfolio, the 50 pair stocks serve only as benchmark (§2.1). Both value- and equal-weighted portfolios are reported. Pair membership is re-estimated annually; the gap between signal month and trade month is one month (signal is month *t-1* returns, holding is month *t*), with no intra-month timing described. Turnover is not reported. Fama-MacBeth regressions (July 1951 – December 2007, average 1,922 stocks per month) confirm the sort: coefficient on *RetDiff* 0.080 (*t* = 18.01) with controls (Table 3, col. 2).

### Pairs momentum (*Cret*)

Sorts on the lagged pairs portfolio return alone (Table 7, col. 3). Value-weighted Decile 10 – Decile 1 profit is 0.74%/month (*t* = 3.78) in the first month after formation and essentially zero in months 2–6 (sum −0.14%); equal-weighted, 0.76% (*t* = 3.23) in month 1, sum 0.1% in months 2–6. The paper concludes "the pairs momentum exists in the first month after portfolio formation, and is basically zero afterwards" (§3.2). In double sorts (Table 6), after controlling for lagged return, the incremental pairs effect averages 0.96% (*t* = 7.41) VW. In cross-sectional splits (Table 8), *Cret* profits do not differ significantly (at 5%) between small/large, covered/uncovered, low/high recognition, low/high analyst coverage, or liquid/illiquid groups — differences of −0.23%, 0.55%, 0.29%, −0.32%, −0.09%, 0.06% respectively, none significant.

### Short-term reversal (*Lret*)

Sorts on the firm's own lagged return (Table 7, col. 4). Month-1 Decile 10 – Decile 1 spread is −0.97% (*t* = −4.91) VW and −3.07% (*t* = −11.82) EW (a reversal: high lagged return underperforms); from month 2 the spread turns positive, cumulative months 2–6 of +0.34% VW and +2.39% EW — "the reversal after the 1st month exceeds those in the first month in value-weighted portfolios" (§3.2). Controlling for pairs return, the reversal hedge averages 1.07% (*t* = 8.77) VW and 2.18% (*t* = 13.47) EW (Table 6, Panel B). Unlike *Cret*, *Lret* profits concentrate in poor-information and illiquid names: differences (small minus large, etc.) of −3.65% (size), −1.92% (media), −1.33% (investor recognition), −2.75% (analyst coverage), −4.07% (Amihud), −4.09% (dollar volume), each significant at 1% (Table 8).

### Industry momentum as the explanation of pairs momentum

~17% of a stock's 50 pair stocks share its industry (versus 5% unconditional), rising from ~12% to ~25% over the sample (Figure 2). Double sorts on lagged industry return (*IndRet*) and pairs-minus-industry return (Table 9): after controlling for lagged industry return, incremental *Cret−IndRet* effect is 0.29% (*t* = 2.51) VW and 0.20% (*t* = 1.41) EW; after controlling for *Cret−IndRet*, the one-month industry momentum hedge is 0.7% (*t* = 5.07) VW and 0.88% (*t* = 6.23) EW — "pairs momentum (*Cret*) can be largely explained by the one-month version of the industry momentum" (§4.1). Fama-MacBeth (Table 10): with *IndRet* and controls, the *Cret* coefficient falls from 0.14 to 0.11 (*t* = 8.81); with the six-month *IndRet6* it is 0.13 (*t* = 10.22) — the six-month industry momentum "does not subsume the predictive power in the pairs momentum" (§4.2). Time-series (Table 11): regressing HML*Cret* (0.74%) on industry winner-minus-loser factors leaves alpha 0.70% (*t* = 3.15) and beta 0.15 (*t* = 0.86) for the (6,0,6) version; alpha 0.66% (*t* = 2.97), beta 0.26 (*t* = 1.84) for (6,0,1); but for the one-month (1,0,1) version alpha collapses to 0.09% (*t* = 0.61) with beta 0.97 (*t* = 14.59), adj. R² 0.47. No industry momentum version explains HML*RetDiff* or HML*Lret* (their alphas actually rise, e.g., HML*RetDiff* alpha 1.66% (*t* = 10.79) against (1,0,1)). Table 12 shows the six-month industry momentum "concentrates in the one-month period": the (formation 0, holding 1) portfolio earns 0.75% (*t* = 5.65) VW and 0.93% (*t* = 6.84) EW, with all other formation/holding cells insignificant or small.

## Test setup

Sample period January 1931 – December 2007 (main tests); chosen as the full CRSP span, extended to 2008–2015 only in the appendix as a "true out-of-sample test" (Appendix Table A.4, content not extracted). Fama-MacBeth regressions run July 1951 – December 2007 because of Compustat availability (average 1,922 stocks/month). Six-factor alphas start January 1968 (Pastor-Stambaugh liquidity factor); Q-factor tests January 1967 – December 2007; funding-liquidity tests February 1962 – December 2007; intermediary leverage factor Q1 1968 – Q4 2007 with a traded mimicking portfolio (LMP) over 1931–2007 (Table 4 notes). Media-coverage splits use 1998–2007, investor recognition 1981–2007, analyst coverage 1984–2007 (Table 8 notes). Data sources: CRSP (returns), Compustat (book equity, shares), I/B/E/S (analyst coverage), 13-F filings (investor recognition), Factiva for three newspapers (media coverage), Kenneth French factor data including the *ST_Rev* factor, Pastor-Stambaugh traded liquidity factor. Survivorship is not addressed beyond the 60-month history requirement; delisting treatment is not described. All returns are gross; no transaction costs, shorting costs, or financing costs appear anywhere. All *t*-statistics are Newey-West with six lags (four in Table 4). Benchmark factor models: Fama-French three-factor, five-factor (plus momentum, short-term reversal), six-factor (plus liquidity), the q-factor model (Hou-Xue-Zhang), funding liquidity, and intermediary leverage; conditional CAPM and consumption CAPM are noted as failures (Appendix A.1, footnote 4).

## Robustness

Tested and reported: pair counts of 10 and 20 instead of 50 (footnote 1, similar); simple difference *Cret−Lret* instead of beta-scaled (footnote 2, similar); q-factor, funding-liquidity, and intermediary-leverage factor models (Table 4, profits survive); conditional CAPM with four information variables (Appendix A.1, does not explain); price > $5 and size > 10% NYSE cutoff (Appendix A.2, still positive and significant); out-of-sample 2008–2015 (Appendix A.4) — "the trading profits have declined in the recent sample period. However, the hedged portfolios are still statistically and economically significant for the equal-weighted portfolios"; unreported consumption CAPM also fails (footnote 4); three industry-momentum factor constructions (6,0,6), (6,0,1), (1,0,1) (Table 11); residual-type predictors of Da et al. (2014) and Collin-Dufresne-Daniel (2015) — after controlling for the Da et al. residual, the *RetDiff* HML is 0.56% (*t* = 3.22) and after CAPM residual 0.63% (*t* = 4.50), while the reverse controls leave insignificant spreads (Table 5). The numeric content of the appendix tables is not available in the extraction, so the magnitude of the degradation in the out-of-sample period cannot be reported here.

## Specification search

Reported variants: three pair-count settings (50 main; 10, 20 robustness); two signal specifications (beta-scaled and simple difference); two sorts of the signal's components; a 6×6 formation-by-holding matrix of industry momentum returns (Table 12); three industry-momentum factor versions (Table 11); six information-environment/liquidity splits (Table 8); two residual-type predictors (Table 5); five factor models plus conditional and consumption CAPM; and the extended 2008–2015 sample. The paper reports results for all of these, but the Harvey-Liu (2016) variable-selection analysis — which "finds that *RetDiff* plays the most important role in predicting cross-sectional returns" (§4.2) — is relegated to the unextracted appendix.

## Limitations and future work

There is no dedicated limitations or future-work section; the paper's stated self-critique is limited to (i) the cost of news data restricting media-coverage tests to three newspapers and 1998–2007 (§3.3), and (ii) the acknowledgment that the information-environment and liquidity variables "are likely correlated" (§3.3). The conclusion frames the residual question as the pairs momentum reflecting "slow diffusion of industry news in the horizon of one month" (§4.3).

## Separable variants for our pipeline

- *RetDiff* cross-sectional decile strategy, value- and equal-weighted (§2.1, Table 1) — the core signal, computable from monthly returns alone.
- Pairs momentum: sort on lagged equal-weighted return of the 50 highest-correlation stocks (§3.2, Table 7).
- Short-term reversal: sort on own lagged return (§3.2, Table 7).
- One-month industry momentum: sort on lagged value-weighted industry portfolio return, 20 industries per Moskowitz-Grinblatt (§4.1, Tables 9–12).
- Six-month industry momentum (*IndRet6*) (§4.2, Table 10).
- *RetDiff* conditional on information environment / liquidity splits (size, media coverage, investor recognition, analyst coverage, Amihud, dollar volume) (§3.3, Table 8).
- *RetDiff* conditional on Da et al. (2014) and CAPM residuals (§2.4, Table 5).
- The pairs benchmark itself — the equal-weighted portfolio of the 50 highest-correlation stocks — as a stand-alone signal input (§2.1).
