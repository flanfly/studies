Category: **single-strategy**. One signal — the pairs-return-minus-own-return difference *RetDiff* — is developed, tested on the full CRSP cross-section, and then decomposed into its two components (pairs momentum and short-term reversal), each of which is analysed separately. The paper is best read as a strategy paper whose central finding is a decomposition result.

## Claim

An equity pairs trading strategy that bets on the convergence of a stock toward the equal-weighted return of its 50 most highly return-correlated peers generates large abnormal returns. For each stock *i* in year *t*+1, pairs are the 50 CRSP stocks with the highest Pearson correlation with *i*'s monthly returns over years *t*−4 to *t*; the signal is the previous month's divergence between the stock and its pairs portfolio, and portfolios hold one month. Sorting all stocks into deciles on this signal, the value-weighted Decile 10−Decile 1 hedge earns 1.40% per month (*t* = 8.81) raw and a six-factor alpha (market, size, book-to-market, momentum, short-term reversal, liquidity) of 0.77% (*t* = 3.75); the equal-weighted hedge earns 3.59% per month (*t* = 12.57) raw and a six-factor alpha of 3.03% (*t* = 13.35) (Table 1). The abstract states these as alphas of "up to 9% annually" (VW) and "36%" (EW). The signal is algebraically the difference between pairs momentum and short-term reversal; the paper shows the two components have different dynamics and cross-sectional properties, and that pairs momentum is largely explained by the one-month version of industry momentum, while the conventional six-month industry momentum (Moskowitz and Grinblatt 1999) does not explain it (Tables 7, 9, 11). Sample period January 1931–December 2007, extended out-of-sample to 2015 (§2.3).

## Fit with our constraints

Instrument class is single-name US common equity (all CRSP firms with 60 months of history). The strategy requires a short leg — Decile 1 (stocks that outperformed their pairs) is shorted — and nothing in the signal is expressible in futures or perpetuals; the edge is stock-level relative mispricing against a peer group. Long and short legs are reported separately at the decile level (Table 1 gives every decile's return and alpha), so the long-leg-only variant is recoverable: the effect is monotone across deciles, and Decile 10 alone has a positive six-factor alpha (VW 0.55, *t* = 3.43; EW 2.06, *t* = 9.43). No leverage is described; the hedge portfolio is described as "self-financing". Returns are gross: no transaction costs, short-sale constraints, borrow costs, or capacity estimates are described anywhere in the extracted text, and turnover is not reported. Replication needs CRSP monthly returns (for correlations, betas and portfolios), Compustat (size, book equity, for the Fama-MacBeth controls), I/B/E/S (analyst coverage), 13F (investor recognition), and news counts from three newspapers for the media-coverage tests (Table 8). Delisting handling and survivorship are not addressed; membership requires 60 prior monthly returns, so the pairs universe is reconstructed from trailing data only.

## Strategies

### Pairs trading on *RetDiff* (the paper's strategy)

**Signal, verbatim (§2.1).** For stock *i* in a month of year *t*+1, let *Cret* be the equal-weighted average return of *i*'s 50 pairs stocks and *Lret* be *i*'s own return, both in the previous month:

$$RetDiff = beta^C * (Cret-Rf) - (Lret - Rf),$$

where *Rf* is the risk-free rate and *beta*^C is the regression coefficient of firm *i*'s monthly return on its pairs-portfolio return using monthly data between year *t*−4 and *t*; the beta scaling "addresses the issue of different return volatilities between the stock and its pairs portfolio" (§2.1). An alternative simple difference *Cret* − *Lret* gives similar results (footnote 2). Correlations are recomputed annually; pairs portfolios are fixed for year *t*+1. Only stock *i* enters the test portfolios — the 50 pairs stocks serve purely as the benchmark. Deciles are formed each month on the previous month's *RetDiff* and held one month; the implicit execution gap is one month (sort on month *t*−1 returns, trade in month *t*), and no intra-month timing is described. Robustness on pair count: 10 and 20 pairs give similar inferences (footnote 1).

**Economic rationale.** The authors frame two candidate channels, both behavioural/limits-to-arbitrage: slow information diffusion of pair- (industry-) specific news (under-reaction, driving the *Cret* component) and short-term liquidity provision (compensation for market makers absorbing liquidity shocks, driving the *Lret* component); the overall *RetDiff* effect is characterised as "an over-reaction to firm-specific news relative to peers" (§3.3). They do not name the counterparty beyond "market makers" and arbitrageurs.

**Universe and weighting.** All CRSP firms with 60 monthly returns from year *t*−4 to *t*; no microcap, penny-stock, or liquidity screens in the main tests. Both value- and equal-weighted deciles are reported (Table 1). Monthly rebalance, one-month holding period, self-financing long Decile 10 / short Decile 1.

### Pairs momentum (*Cret* sorts) and short-term reversal (*Lret* sorts) — the decomposition

Because *RetDiff* = scaled *Cret* − *Lret*, the authors sort separately on the two components (Tables 6–8). Decile 10−Decile 1 on *Cret* (long high-pairs-return): month-1 return 0.74% VW (*t* = 3.78), 0.76% EW (*t* = 3.23); returns are "basically zero" from month 2 onward, cumulatively −0.14% (VW) over months 2–6 (Table 7). Decile 10−Decile 1 on *Lret* (long high-prior-return): month-1 return −0.97% VW (*t* = −4.91), −3.07% EW (*t* = −11.82) — i.e., the reversal trade (short winners, long losers) earns 0.97% VW / 3.07% EW — and the sign flips positive from month 2 (VW month 2: +0.30%), cumulative +0.34% VW over months 2–6 (Table 7). In sequential double sorts, controlling for prior-month return, the pairs-return spread averages 0.96% VW (*t* = 7.41); controlling for pairs return, the reversal spread averages 1.07% VW (*t* = 8.77) and 2.18% EW (*t* = 13.47) (Table 6). Cross-sectionally (Table 8, equal-weighted): the *RetDiff* hedge is 5.05% per month in small vs 1.48% in large firms (difference 3.56%, *t* = 13.74); 5.25% in low-liquidity vs 1.20% in high-liquidity firms (Amihud, difference 4.05%, *t* = 17.80); similar splits on media coverage (2.32% difference), investor recognition (1.32%), and analyst coverage (2.51%), all significant. The *Lret* hedge concentrates in small/illiquid/low-coverage stocks (differences of −1.92% to −4.09%, all significant), while the *Cret* hedge shows no significant difference on any of the six proxies (differences −0.32% to 0.55%, none significant at 5%) (Table 8).

### Industry momentum as the explanation of pairs momentum

Double sorts on prior-month industry return (*IndRet*, Moskowitz–Grinblatt 20 industries) and *Cret*−*IndRet* (Table 9): controlling for *IndRet*, the *Cret*−*IndRet* spread averages 0.29% VW (*t* = 2.51) and 0.20% EW (*t* = 1.41); controlling for *Cret*−*IndRet*, the *IndRet* spread averages 0.70% VW (*t* = 5.07) and 0.88% EW (*t* = 6.23). In Fama-MacBeth regressions (Table 10), the *Cret* coefficient falls from 0.14 (Table 3, col 4, no industry control) to 0.11 (*t* = 8.81) when *IndRet* (coefficient 0.12, *t* = 13.48) is added, but is essentially unchanged (0.13, *t* = 10.22) when the conventional six-month *IndRet6* is used instead. In time-series tests (Table 11), the *Cret* hedge's alpha against the (6,0,6) industry winner-minus-loser factor is 0.70% (*t* = 3.15, beta 0.15, *t* = 0.86) and against (6,0,1) is 0.66% (*t* = 2.97, beta 0.26, *t* = 1.84), but against the one-month (1,0,1) version the alpha collapses to 0.09% (*t* = 0.61) with beta 0.97 (*t* = 14.59), R² = 0.47. The conventional six-month industry momentum itself concentrates in its first month: the (formation month 0, holding month 1) industry WML earns 0.75% VW (*t* = 5.65) and 0.93% EW (*t* = 6.84), while all other formation/holding-month cells are largely insignificant (Table 12). Same-industry stocks are over-represented among pairs: unconditionally 5% of a stock's peers share its industry, versus ~17% of its 50 pairs, rising from ~12% in early years to ~25% recently (Figure 2).

## Test setup

Main sample January 1931–December 2007, chosen as the CRSP full history (no pre-registered split); six-factor alphas run January 1968–December 2007 (Pastor–Stambaugh liquidity availability); Fama-MacBeth regressions July 1951–December 2007 (Compustat availability); Table 4 subsamples: Q-factor January 1967–December 2007, funding-liquidity February 1962–December 2007, intermediary leverage Q1 1968–Q4 2007 (LevF) and January 1931–December 2007 (LMP); Table 5 residuals January 1982–December 2007; Table 8 media coverage 1998–2007, investor recognition 1981–2007, analyst coverage 1984–2007. Data sources by name: CRSP (returns, prices, volume), Compustat (size, book equity), I/B/E/S (analyst coverage), 13F (breadth of ownership, per Lehavy and Sloan 2008), Factiva counts in *Wall Street Journal*, *The New York Times*, *USA Today* (media coverage). t-statistics are Newey-West (six-lag in most tables, four-lag in Table 4). Survivorship/delisting: not addressed. Costs: none; all headline numbers gross. Factor benchmarks: FF3, FF3 + momentum + short-term reversal (5-factor), those plus Pastor–Stambaugh traded liquidity (6-factor), the q-factor model (Hou-Xue-Zhang), funding liquidity proxied by investment-bank excess returns (Ang-Gorovyy-Van Inwegen 2011), and the Adrian-Etula-Muir intermediary leverage factor (plus its traded mimicking portfolio LMP); an unreported consumption CAPM also fails to explain profits (footnote 4).

## Robustness

Tested and reported: 10 and 20 pairs instead of 50 (footnote 1); *Cret*−*Lret* in place of beta-scaled *RetDiff* (footnote 2); conditional CAPM with four conditioning variables (lagged T-bill rate, dividend yield, 10y−3m term spread, BAA−AAA spread) fails to explain the spread (Appendix Table A.1); a screen to price > $5 and size > 10% NYSE cutoff leaves hedged returns positive and significant (Appendix Table A.2); the Harvey-Liu (2016) variable-selection exercise ranks *RetDiff* as the most important predictor by Fama-MacBeth t-statistics and R² (Appendix); and a genuine out-of-sample extension to 2008–2015 in which "the trading profits have declined" but "the hedged portfolios are still statistically and economically significant for the equal-weighted portfolios" (§2.3, Appendix Table A.4). The 2008–2015 decline is reported without further elaboration. The appendix tables A.1–A.4 are not present in the extracted text, so only the body's summary of each is available.

## Specification search

The paper reports decile sorts (Table 1), quintile sequential double sorts (Tables 6, 9), three pairs-portfolio sizes (50, with 10/20 in footnote 1), three industry-momentum variants — (6,0,6), (6,0,1), (1,0,1) formation/skip/holding — (Table 11), two residual-type predictors (Table 5), three alternative factor models plus funding-liquidity and intermediary-leverage factors (Table 4), and six information/liquidity splits (Table 8). Results are reported selectively: the decile-level detail is full, but the media-coverage test is restricted to 1998–2007 for data-cost reasons, the consumption-CAPM result is relegated to a footnote, and the Harvey-Liu and out-of-sample results are in appendices the extraction does not contain.

## Limitations and future work

The authors' own statements: both information diffusion and liquidity provision "may help explain the pairs trading" profits, but "there may be other economic channels at work" (§3.3); the pairs momentum is explained by the one-month version of industry momentum, and the paper does not go further in identifying what drives that one-month industry momentum beyond slow diffusion of industry news (§4.3). The paper has no dedicated limitations section; the acknowledged decline in profits over 2008–2015 (§2.3) is the only performance caveat offered.

## Separable variants for our pipeline

The strategy components are separable as independent single-signal sorts; for each, the signal is the previous month's value, portfolios are deciles held one month, both VW and EW reported:

- *RetDiff* = *beta*^C·(*Cret*−*Rf*) − (*Lret*−*Rf*) — the full pairs-trading signal (§2.1, Table 1).
- Pairs momentum *Cret* — previous month's equal-weighted return of the 50 highest-correlation stocks (§3, Tables 6–8).
- Short-term reversal *Lret* — previous month's own return (§3, Tables 6–8); note the sign convention is long high-return stocks, so the profitable leg is short.
- *Cret*−*IndRet* — pairs return net of prior-month industry return (§4.1, Table 9).
- *IndRet* and *IndRet6* — one-month and six-month industry returns (Moskowitz-Grinblatt 20 industries) (§4, Tables 9–10).
- Industry winner-minus-loser (1,0,1) factor — long three winner / short three loser industries, one-month formation and holding (§4.3, Tables 11–12).
